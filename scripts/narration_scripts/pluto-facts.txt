This article talks about seven surprising facts about Pluto. It is not called a planet anymore, but it is still one of the strangest objects in our solar system.

First, let's talk about time. One year on Pluto takes 248 Earth years. That is how long it takes to go around the Sun one time. A baby born on Pluto would not live to see their first birthday, in Pluto years.

Here is a fun fact. Disney's cartoon dog is also named Pluto. People often think the dog came first. But actually, both got the name around the same time, in 1930. An eleven-year-old girl suggested the name for the newly found world, based on an old Roman god.

Pluto's biggest moon is called Charon. It always shows the same side to Pluto, and its orbit is almost a perfect circle. Because of this, scientists say you could build something like a bridge connecting Pluto and Charon directly. This is not possible with any other planet and moon that we know of.

Pluto is also famous for a heart-shaped mark on its surface. We can see this in photos from a spacecraft called New Horizons. But because of the camera angle, the heart actually sits higher up on Pluto than it looks in the pictures.

Strangely, Pluto spins backward compared to most planets, and at a strange angle too. Scientists think a huge crash, billions of years ago, caused this. It may be similar to the crash that created Earth's own Moon.

Pluto even has a thin layer of air around it. Scientists thought it would be very thin, but it turned out to be thicker than expected. And it keeps getting thicker as Pluto moves farther from the Sun — which is the opposite of what scientists predicted.

Maybe the most beautiful fact is this: during sunrise and sunset, Pluto's thin air glows a strange electric blue. You can see separate blue layers stretching for hundreds of miles. Nobody knew this until a spacecraft flew close enough to see it.
