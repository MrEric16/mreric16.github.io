This article is about a piece of homework that is almost two thousand years old. It was written by a schoolchild in ancient Egypt. It sounds like something a parent would say today.

The homework is on a wooden board about the size of a small tablet computer. It is covered with wax. To make a board like this, people poured melted black wax into a flat space in the middle of the wood. When the wax was cool, a teacher or a student could scratch letters into it with a metal pen. The letters showed up in a lighter colour against the dark wax.

The lesson is in the Greek language. It has two parts. The first part is a writing exercise. In the first line, the teacher wrote a sentence as an example. In the next two lines, the student copied the same words. An expert says the student's writing is clumsy. That is the same as with young pupils today, who copy lines their teacher has written. The rest of the homework includes a multiplication table and some reading exercises.

And what are the words? They are lessons about life. One line says that you should only accept advice from a wise man. Another says that you cannot trust all your friends. So it sounds like a warning about a bad crowd. The expert says the lines were not only for practising handwriting. They were also meant to teach good behaviour. In his words, it is not only the hand and the fingers, but also the mind that is being taught.

We do not know the name of the child. But it was probably a boy from a rich family. At that time, only boys from rich families usually went to school.

Why did it last so long? Wax usually breaks down when it is wet. But the dry weather in Egypt helped to protect it. So we can still see the bad handwriting of a primary school pupil from almost two thousand years ago.

The board belongs to the British Library, which bought it in eighteen ninety-two. For many years, almost no one could see it. It was shown in a special exhibition about the history of writing.

The writer of the article makes a joke. No one has found a piece of ancient homework that says, if your friends jumped into the river, would you do it too? But it would not be a surprise.
