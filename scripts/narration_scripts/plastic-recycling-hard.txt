This article explains why recycling plastic is so hard, and what you can do to make it work better.

Think about a takeout meal. There is a plastic bag, a greasy pizza box, plastic forks, paper napkins and a soda can. Which of these can be recycled? It feels like a quiz. The rules change from city to city, and that may be one reason why only about thirty-two percent of our rubbish gets recycled. For plastic, it is much worse. In the United States in twenty twenty-one, only about six percent of plastic was recycled. Paper does best, with sixty-eight percent. The rest of the plastic breaks into tiny pieces that end up everywhere, from the ocean to our own bodies.

First, do not trust the little triangle of arrows on plastic. Many people think it means the item can be recycled. In a survey of two thousand Americans, sixty-eight percent thought so. But the triangle only shows the type of plastic, in one of seven groups. Only some groups are good for the recycling bin. The confusion is so common that California made a law to limit the symbol, and the state has taken an oil company to court. It says the company used the symbol to mislead people.

Why is plastic so difficult? One expert says plastic is extraordinarily finicky. When makers produce plastic, they add colours and other chemicals to make it hard or soft. These change how it melts, and so how it can be recycled.

As a general rule, plastics marked with a number one or two are the best. These are hard plastics, like water bottles and milk jugs. Recycling plants have machines to clean, shred and melt them. Number five, found on some food and medicine containers, is sometimes accepted, but not everywhere. Plastics numbered three, four, six and seven, like bubble wrap, shopping bags and soft food wrap, usually end up in a landfill if you put them in the bin.

There is a name for a common mistake, which is wish-cycling. This is when you hope that something can be recycled, so you put it in the bin and cross your fingers. At best, it goes to a landfill. At worst, it jams the machines. Plastic bags wind around the machinery and workers have to cut them off by hand. One recycler said they are the bane of their existence. On average, about ten percent of what her company collects has to be thrown away, because people put in things that cannot be recycled, from bags to clothes.

Also keep your recycling clean. Food, dirt and liquids can ruin paper and other items. That is why greasy pizza boxes usually go in the rubbish. Many cities have guides or online databases that tell you what to put in the bin.

Another problem is money. Towns pay for recycling by selling the material. But plastic is hard to sell, because buyers want a simple recipe, not a mixture. Metal is different. Aluminium can be recycled again and again without losing quality. Plastic is usually turned into lower quality things, like carpet. For many years, China bought most of the used plastic from the United States. In twenty seventeen, it raised its standards, and American recyclers lost their main buyer.

What can help? Some places have bottle deposit laws, where you pay five to fifteen cents extra and get it back when you return the bottle. States with these laws have half as much litter. Some shops collect plastic bags separately, so they stay clean. Some states now make companies responsible for how recyclable their products are.

But some experts say the answer is not only recycling. As one said, the best thing we can do is make less waste. Refilling is one idea. A drinks company has promised to reuse a quarter of its bottles by twenty thirty. A start-up even washes and refills bottles in a vending machine. As one expert said, that is the future.
