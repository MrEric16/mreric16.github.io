This article is about airports. Almost everything in an airport is planned. The lights, the signs, even the gift shop. Some of it helps you relax. Some of it helps you spend money. Here are the secrets.

First, airports try hard not to let you get lost. A good designer says that a sign is a sign of failure. The best airport needs very few signs. So in many new airports, you can see the planes through the windows as soon as you leave security. You see the tails of the aircraft. It is not your plane, but it tells you where you are. When signs are needed, they are made very carefully. Most airports use one of three simple fonts, because they are easy to read from far away. And for every inch of letter height, you can read the sign from about forty feet away.

Art helps too. The big sculpture in the terminal is not just for decoration. It gives people a place to meet. At one airport, people say, meet you at the sculpture. Art also shows where you are in the world. Many travellers say they want an airport that feels like its own city, not a boring tube that moves people.

Now, the soft carpet. The long walk to your gate has a hard floor. But the waiting area has carpet. This is on purpose. It feels cosy, like your living room. And this matters, because relaxed people spend more. On average, they spend seven percent more in shops and ten percent more in duty-free.

Next, the golden hour. This is the first hour after you pass security. Airport managers know this is when you are most likely to spend money. You have time, you are in a good mood, and you are waiting. One more hour in an airport means about seven dollars more spent by each person. That is why machines for check-in and bags are everywhere. They are about twenty-five percent faster than people. They help you get through quickly, so you have more time in the shops.

Shops are also placed with care. Most things people buy at airports are impulse buys. Nobody really needs a huge bar of chocolate. So shops go where the most people walk. Some airports make you walk through the duty-free shop to reach your gate. A winding path is even better. One report says that duty-free shops with a winding walk-through sell sixty percent more.

Most people are right-handed. So airports also use this. A path that curves from right to left, with more shops on the right, sells more.

Then there are the souvenirs. They are linked to the place. At one airport in the United States, a shirt from an old film is still a top seller, more than twenty years later. At another, people buy potted cactus plants. These things make you feel you are somewhere special.

Even the line at security has a plan. One long line is better than many short lines. It feels fairer, because nobody sees another line moving faster. So people feel less stress.

Finally, security officers sometimes talk to you. A study found that simple open questions, like where are you travelling, are twenty times better at finding a problem than only watching how people behave.

So, the next time you walk through an airport, look around. Almost nothing is an accident.
