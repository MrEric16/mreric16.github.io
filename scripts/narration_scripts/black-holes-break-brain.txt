This article is about black holes, and about how strange they really are. Scientists still do not understand many things about them. Here are some of the strangest ideas.

Let's start with a simple rule. Nothing can escape a black hole. Not even light. The edge where this starts is called the event horizon. But here is the surprise. Not everything near a black hole falls in. Gas and dust often spin around it, a bit like water going down a drain. This spinning material gets very hot. It makes storms. Some of it even shoots straight up, like a fountain.

Now, here is a bigger surprise. Black holes may slowly shrink. Scientists think tiny pairs of particles keep popping in and out of existence near the edge. Sometimes one falls in, and the other flies away. Each time, the black hole loses a tiny bit of energy. This takes a very, very long time. But in the end, a black hole could shrink and disappear completely.

This idea creates a puzzle. The two particles are linked. What happens to one is felt by the other. So what does the particle that flew away feel, when its partner is crushed? Nobody knows. One idea says the link breaks. And that would make a huge amount of energy. If that is true, every black hole might be wrapped in a wall of fire.

Here is another puzzle. The rules of physics say that information can never be lost. But if something falls into a black hole, it seems to be lost forever. Scientists call this the information paradox. They still have no final answer. One new idea says the information is scrambled inside, and then slowly passed out with those flying particles. But this is not proven.

Now think about the beginning of the universe. Soon after the Big Bang, there may have been many tiny black holes. They give off no light. So some scientists asked: could they be the dark matter? Dark matter is the invisible stuff that most of space is made of. But most experiments say no. At best, black holes of medium size could explain only a small part of it.

Another idea is about time. In the middle of a black hole, the maths says everything is squeezed into one point of zero size. That makes no sense in real life. So one theory says space bends so strongly there that the black hole reaches into the future. Anything that falls in would travel forward in time. This is only an idea for now.

Here is the strangest idea of all. Some scientists think our universe is not the first one. They say there may have been an older universe before ours, with its own black holes. And they believe that we can still see marks from them in the oldest light in space. Many scientists disagree. They think it is just random noise. But nobody has proven it wrong.

So black holes are a place where our best ideas about space, time and tiny particles do not fit together. That is exactly why scientists love them.
