This article tells how basketball was invented. It started with a group of bored students, a snowy winter, and two fruit baskets.

The year was 1891. A big snowstorm had just hit a town in the United States. A school there trained young men to be sports teachers. The students could not go outside. They had too much energy, and they were making trouble in the halls. Even a rough game of football indoors did not help.

So a young teacher got a job to solve the problem. He had to make a new game for the winter. He was thirty years old, and he came from Canada. He thought about the games he knew, like rugby, and a childhood game with throwing and chasing. Then he made something new.

He cleared the gym floor. He picked up a soccer ball. He asked the school worker for two square boxes. But the worker only had two old peach baskets. So the teacher nailed them to the balcony, about ten feet high, at each end of the gym.

He split the students into two teams of nine. The goal was simple. Throw the ball into the other team's basket. And that was nearly all he told them.

It did not go well. With almost no rules, the students pushed, kicked, and tackled. One boy was knocked out. A few got black eyes. Another hurt his shoulder. The teacher said later that it was terrible.

But there was good news. The students loved it. They begged to play again. So the teacher sat down and wrote thirteen rules. His secretary typed them on two pages, and he put them on the wall.

The most important rule was this. You cannot run with the ball. You must throw it or pass it. That one rule stopped the rough play. After that, nobody got hurt, and the game became fair and fun.

The students wanted to call it by his name. He said no. He picked a very simple name: basket ball.

The game spread fast. Other schools tried it. Colleges tried it. Soon girls and women played it too. It became one of the fastest-growing sports ever.

Over time, the rules changed. The bottoms of the baskets were cut out, so the ball could fall through, and those became hoops. Dribbling was added in 1901. And five players on each team became the normal number.

Here is a fun fact. The original two pages of rules still exist. In 2010, a man bought them at an auction for four million dollars. They now sit in a university building. And today, a rule book for professional basketball is more than sixty-five pages long.

All this from a winter problem, a gym, and two peach baskets.
