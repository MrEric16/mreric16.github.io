This article is about national anthems. If you listen to many of them in a row, they all sound similar. They are often like a march or a hymn. The words are about freedom, beautiful land and pride. But each anthem has its own story. Here are some of the best.

First, Malaysia. In eighteen eighty-eight, the ruler of one Malaysian state was invited to London by Queen Victoria. His helper was asked for the music of the state's anthem, to play at the welcome ceremony. But there was no anthem. The helper panicked. He thought it would look bad to admit this. So he hummed a popular tune from the Seychelles. Then he told the ruler what he had done, and reminded him to stand up when it played. The state kept the tune. And when Malaysia became independent in nineteen fifty-seven, it became the country's anthem, with new words.

Next, Mexico. In eighteen fifty-three, the country held a contest for the best poem to be its anthem. A poet's girlfriend wanted him to enter. But he was not interested. So she locked him in a room at her parents' house. The room was full of pictures of Mexican history. He was let out when he pushed a poem of ten verses under the door. That poem became the anthem. And the girlfriend became his wife.

Then there is a tiny island in the South Atlantic called St. Helena. It has a song that is played when its ship leaves the port. The man who wrote it was an American who had never been there. A friend told him to write an anthem. He looked at some postcards of the island and wrote it. It is probably the only country-style anthem in the world.

The Dutch anthem is hidden in a puzzle. It has fifteen verses. The first letter of each verse spells the name of a hero of the war against Spain. It was only made official in nineteen thirty-two, but the song is more than three hundred years old.

And finally, the anthem of the Czech Republic and Slovakia. When the two countries were one, in nineteen eighteen, the anthem was made of two parts. One verse came from a Czech opera, and one from a Slovak folk song. When the country split, in nineteen ninety-three, they just split the anthem too. The first verse went to the Czech Republic. The second went to Slovakia.

Some other facts are about the words. Only one anthem is told in the first person. The anthem of Andorra speaks as a princess protected by her princes, who are the people. France's anthem is very violent. In its full version, it talks about blood on flags and soldiers cutting throats. South Africa's anthem has words in five languages, and joins two older songs.

So an anthem is more than a tune. Each one is a small piece of history.
