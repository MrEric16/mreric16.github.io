This article explains why one very small octopus is one of the most dangerous animals in the sea. It is the blue-ringed octopus.

Most people know that octopuses are clever. They can hide by changing colour. But this one is famous for something else. It is deadly.

It is small. With its arms, it is only about twelve to twenty-two centimetres long. It lives in shallow water, in coral reefs and on rocky sea floors in the Pacific and Indian oceans. When it is calm, it looks brown. But when it is in danger, bright blue rings glow on its body. That is a warning.

The danger is a poison called tetrodotoxin. It is also found in the pufferfish. If a pufferfish is not prepared the right way, it can kill the person who eats it. This poison is twelve hundred times stronger than the well-known poison cyanide. And there is no known cure. Interestingly, the octopus does not make the poison itself. Tiny living things called bacteria make it, inside the octopus's mouth glands.

What does the poison do? It stops nerves from talking to muscles. A body part that cannot get messages cannot move. In people, this can stop the muscles that let you breathe. It can also stop the heart.

Why does the octopus have it? A small animal in shallow water has many enemies. The poison is its best protection. It is also useful for hunting. The octopus usually bites its enemy or its food. Most of the poison is stored in its saliva glands. But it can also let some poison out into the water. A fish that breathes this water will slowly stop moving. And female octopuses put the poison into their eggs, so that other animals do not eat them.

Do these octopuses attack people? Very rarely. There are only a few known deaths. Two were in Australia, and one in Singapore. Most bites do not cause serious trouble. Some bites caused nothing at all.

But it can be very serious. In one case, a small boy was bitten in Australia. Within ten minutes, he was sick three times, could not see clearly, and could not stand. He was taken to the hospital and a machine helped him to breathe for seventeen hours. Twenty-eight hours after the bite, he went home. He had no lasting problems. Experts say that the right action is to get medical help quickly, and to have a machine to help with breathing.

So this small and beautiful animal can defend itself very well. If you ever see one on a beach, do not touch it.
