This article is about a surprising fact. In Norway, millions of families eat tacos every Friday. It is a national tradition. It has a name: Taco Friday.

Picture a Friday evening in a Norwegian city. A father comes home from work. A mother puts the seasoning on the meat. The children carry bowls of toppings to the table. They are not eating fish, even though Norway is famous for its fish. They are eating tacos.

How did this happen? It started with oil. In the late nineteen-sixties, Norway began to drill for oil in the North Sea. It needed experts from other countries. Many came from Texas, in the United States. They brought their drilling skills, and also their taste for Tex-Mex food. A clever shopkeeper in the city of Stavanger started to import food from abroad for these workers. Over the years, supermarkets began to sell taco kits. These came in colourful boxes, with taco shells, seasoning and mild sauce. What began as a craving for foreigners slowly became a ritual for the whole nation.

Today tacos are so important in Norway that the government's statistics office has a Taco Index. It tracks the price of the usual taco ingredients. Even the country's most famous footballer has said that tacos are one of his favourite things.

Why did tacos become so popular, when other foods stayed a novelty? The answer is that tacos solved a Norwegian problem. A family dinner is usually not a big event in Norway. People are busy. Children go to sports clubs. Many families do not eat together. But on Friday, things change. A food writer says Friday is almost a holy day, when people finally relax together. Tacos suit that. The kit makes them easy. Everyone makes their own, with the toppings they like. She calls it the Norwegian Happy Meal.

The Norwegian taco is very different from the Mexican one. It usually has a hard shell. It is filled with minced beef, with cheese, sour cream and lettuce. It is about simplicity, not about being authentic.

But Norwegians also get creative. In the north, people may use reindeer or moose instead of beef. Along the coast, you can find smoked salmon, shrimp and other fish. At a big cooking competition, a chef even made a taco with dried cod, in honour of the Friday tradition.

A Mexican chef who lives in Norway had a funny reaction. She found it a beautiful tradition, because families share food together. But she said she would never make it herself.

So the Norwegian taco is not really Mexican, and not completely Texan. It is something Norway made its own.
