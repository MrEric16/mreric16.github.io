This article asks a simple question. Do any animals other than cats purr? The answer depends on what you mean by purr.

First, what is a purr? Cat owners know it well. It is a low, soft hum. We think it means a cat is happy. But cats also purr when they are stressed or in pain. Kittens start to purr soon after they are born. Mothers and kittens often purr while they find each other and while the kittens drink milk.

Scientists still do not fully understand how it works. But we know that the voice box in the throat vibrates. And here is a special thing. Most animal sounds happen only when the animal breathes out. A purr is different. It goes on all the time, when the cat breathes in and when it breathes out. An expert says this is one of the main signs of a real purr.

So which cats can do it? Not only pet cats. Wild cats such as the cheetah, the cougar, the serval and the bobcat can purr too. But big cats like lions, tigers, jaguars and leopards cannot. They roar instead. This is because of a small bone in their throat. Some scientists say that it is better to sort cats into purring cats and roaring cats, not into big and small.

Outside the cat family, only one other animal is known to purr in the same way. It is the genet. It looks like a mix between a cat and a mongoose, with a long pointed nose and a thin body. At least two kinds of genet purr.

But if we use a looser meaning, many more animals make purring sounds. Raccoons make a soft sound when they are happy and relaxed. This often happens with babies and their mothers. A scientist even heard a baby raccoon do it while she fed it by hand. It is not made the same way as a cat purr, but it sounds close. Rabbits can chatter their teeth when they are relaxed. Bats may make a purr sound when a person they trust holds them. Mother grey squirrels and their babies make a sound like purring when they are together. Even wolf spiders can do it. They rub body parts together to make a purr sound when they want to find a mate.

It is hard to study this. Many animals make these sounds only when they are calm, and a human watching can make them nervous.

The answer is clear, though. By the strict meaning, only cats and a few close cousins can truly purr.
