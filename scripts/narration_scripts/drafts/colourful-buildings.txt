This article shows how some of the world's most famous buildings use bright colour, and why architects choose it.

People have used colour in buildings for thousands of years. Ancient Egyptian and Greek temples were painted in bright shades. Medieval churches glowed through stained glass. Today, some architects say colour is the secret ingredient that turns a plain building into something people remember.

Let us look at three examples.

The first is a huge temple complex in southern India. Work on it began almost two thousand years ago and went on for many centuries. It covers more than one hundred and fifty acres, which makes it one of the largest religious sites in the world. It has twenty-one tall gateways, and every one of them is covered with bright, painted figures. Visitors walk in from the ordinary world and feel they have entered a place full of wonder.

The second is a concert hall in Barcelona, built in nineteen hundred and eight. Every column, window and lamp has its own rich colour and pattern. An expert in the article says it looks like something from a fairy tale forest. The hall mixes many styles, and it came to stand for the proud, wealthy and independent spirit of the region.

The third is the most surprising one. It is a rubbish burning plant in Osaka, in Japan. You might expect a building like this to be grey and dull. But the artist who designed it hated plain, flat buildings. He wanted every building, even a factory, to feel alive. So this plant looks like a castle from a children's book. It has yellow and red stripes that seem to run down the walls, a bright tower, and short, round legs painted in blocks of colour. People often think it is part of a nearby theme park. The article calls it rebellious, and it is hard to disagree.

There are other buildings on the list too, like a department store in the Netherlands that was inspired by the view of fields from an aeroplane window, and a library in Spain with pastel tiles that cover its round walls. But these three show the main idea well.

The message from the experts is clear. Colour is not a decoration you add at the end. Used with courage, it can make a building joyful, give it meaning, and make you look up from your phone for a moment.
