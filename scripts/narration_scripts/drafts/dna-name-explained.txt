This article explains what the three letters DNA actually stand for, and why that long scientific name makes sense.

DNA stands for deoxyribonucleic acid. It is a real tongue twister. It sounds like a spell from a fantasy film. But the name is not random. It is a short description of what the molecule is made of and where it was first found.

Let us break it into three parts. The first part, "deoxyribo," comes from the name of a sugar. This sugar is a key piece of the molecule's backbone. The second part, "nucleic," tells you where scientists first discovered it, which is inside the nucleus, the centre of a cell. The last part, "acid," describes a chemical property of the molecule. It does not mean that DNA tastes sour or burns. It only describes how it behaves in chemistry.

So the long name really means a sugar-based, acidic molecule from the nucleus of a cell. Once you know that, it looks much less scary.

Now, what does DNA do? Think of it as an instruction book for a living body. Every living thing on Earth uses it, from a tiny plant to a blue whale to you. It carries the information that tells a body how to grow, how to work, and how to make new life.

The book is written with small building blocks that are joined in a long line. The order of these blocks is what matters, just like the order of letters in a sentence. If you change the order, you change the message.

DNA has a famous shape. It looks like a twisted ladder, and scientists call this shape a double helix. The shape is not only pretty. It is also very useful. When a cell divides, it must copy all of its instructions so that the new cell gets a full set. The two sides of the ladder can separate, and each side helps to build a new partner. That is how DNA is copied so accurately.

Here is one more fact. People are almost exactly the same in their DNA. We share about ninety-nine point nine percent of it with every other person. The tiny part that is different explains things like the colour of your eyes, the colour of your hair, and the shape of your nose. So DNA connects all humans, and it also makes each person different.
