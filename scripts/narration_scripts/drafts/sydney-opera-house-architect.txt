This article is about a building that is famous around the world, and about the man who made it. He never saw it finished.

The Sydney Opera House is one of the most famous buildings on Earth. Some people call it the eighth wonder of the world. You would think its designer would be a hero. But the truth is very sad.

The story starts in nineteen fifty-six. The government of New South Wales, in Australia, held a competition to choose a design. The winner was a young Danish architect. He was not well known. He had only won a few small contests at home. The story says that his plan was found in a pile of rejected ones. A famous judge saw it and said it was genius. The judge said that many opera houses look like boots, and this designer had solved the problem.

The design looked like sails, filling with wind over the harbour. It had large curved shells. The architect took the idea from the shape of an orange peel. If you put the shells together, they would make a perfect ball.

But the problems started early. Building began before the plans were finished. No one yet knew how to hold up the huge shells. The design was very ambitious, and it had no solution for the hardest parts. The architect tried to solve the problems almost alone. The project used far more money than planned.

Then politics made it worse. A new government came in. The new leader had always been against the project. His minister for public works questioned every choice and every cost. In the end, the government stopped paying the architect. In nineteen sixty-six, it owed him a lot of money, and he could not pay his workers. After a tense meeting, he quit. He even climbed over a back wall so that reporters would not see him.

People in Sydney were angry. There were marches in the streets. But the government did not change its mind. A new architect was brought in. The final cost was about one hundred and three million dollars. That is about fourteen times more than the plan.

In nineteen seventy-three, the Queen of England opened the building. The man who designed it was not invited. His name was not even said.

When he went home to Denmark, he had more bad news. He had a big tax bill. And his own architects' group told him he had done wrong by leaving. They said a builder must never leave a job. They took back this ban only in twenty thirteen.

He never went back to Australia. He never saw his building finished. Many years later, in nineteen ninety-nine, the opera house asked him to write rules for how to change the building in the future. In two thousand three, he won the top prize in architecture. In two thousand four, a room in the building was named after him. He said that this gave him the greatest pleasure.
