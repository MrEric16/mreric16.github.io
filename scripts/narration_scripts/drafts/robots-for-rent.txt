This article is about a new idea: you do not have to buy a robot. You can rent one.

Let's start in hospitals in the United States. Many of them have a friendly white robot. It is about one point two metres tall, with one arm and wheels. It carries medical supplies around the building. Nurses say good morning to it. Some give it a high five, or even a hug. It answers with heart-shaped eyes on its screen and a happy beep. The company says that many people feel it is a part of the team. And the hospitals do not buy these robots. They rent them.

Companies call this robots-as-a-service. You get the robot, and also repairs, updates and support. Sometimes a human engineer in a control room can take over the robot, if it needs help. For the hospital, there are two good points. First, it costs less at the start. Second, and more important, the technology is changing fast. The company keeps improving the robot's software and skills.

You can rent robots for a day or for years. There are robot bartenders, and robots that pull weeds on farms. And now, the first human-shaped robots are available, too. They are still a work in progress. So they are usually rented for simple, clear jobs. Often that means entertainment. A robot might dance, sing or serve guests at a wedding or a company party. An expert explains how the dance works. A real dancer performs, and it is filmed. The film trains the robot. Even so, an engineer often goes with the robot, in case the stage is difficult.

Companies also plan robots for the home. One American company will soon send out a home helper robot. Early customers can pay twenty thousand dollars to own it. Or they can pay four hundred and ninety-nine dollars a month. The company says a monthly plan makes it affordable for many more people.

Why rent? The main reason is speed. Robot makers bring out a new model every year. If you buy one now, it may soon be out of date. If you rent, you can always get the newest. Another reason is that you do not need to be an expert. If something goes wrong, you call the company.

It is not only about human-shaped robots. One company in Chicago has more than two hundred and fifty factory robots for rent. It charges one flat price each month, with everything included. If a robot arm breaks, the company brings a new one. This helps small businesses that could not afford to buy factory robots. Some customers ask to pay according to how much human work the robot saves.

For the makers, renting is also good. It lets them test their robots in the real world, and collect useful information. This matters for human-shaped robots, because an analyst says the technology is still not mature.

China is an early leader. Some companies there rent robots to hotels, and offer them on cleaning apps. One big Chinese company says its robots can be rented in seventeen countries, including the United Kingdom. But in China, many robots are bought, because of government support.

So for many people, renting may be the easiest way to start using robots.
