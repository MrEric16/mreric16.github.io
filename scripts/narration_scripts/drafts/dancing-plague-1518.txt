This article is about a very strange event in history. In the year fifteen eighteen, people in a city danced for days, and some of them died. It is called the dancing plague.

It happened in the city of Strasbourg, which was then part of the Holy Roman Empire. In July, a woman walked into the street and started to dance. She danced alone and in silence. She twisted, twirled and shook. She did not stop for almost a week. Soon, about thirty-six other people joined her. By August, as many as four hundred people were dancing.

Nobody knew why. The local doctors had a theory. They said it was caused by hot blood. Their answer was to let the people dance until the fever left their bodies. So the city built a stage. They hired professional dancers, and even a band to play music. But this made things worse. Many people collapsed from tiredness. Some died from strokes and heart attacks. The dancing only ended in September. The dancers were taken to a shrine on a mountain, to pray.

It sounds like a legend, but it is well recorded in the documents from that time. And it was not the only case. Similar events happened in Switzerland, Germany and the Netherlands. But this one was the largest and the deadliest.

What caused it? Nobody is sure. One historian thinks the answer is a saint. At that time, many people believed that this saint could curse people with a dancing sickness. In that same year, the city was suffering from hunger and disease. All of this was very frightening. So the fear may have caused a kind of mass panic. People call it stress-induced hysteria.

Other ideas have been suggested. Maybe the dancers belonged to a religious group. Maybe they ate bread made from rye that had a poisonous mould. This mould can cause spasms and strange visions. But no one has proved any of these ideas.

So the dancing plague is still a mystery. Scientists and historians still do not know exactly why the people of Strasbourg danced.
