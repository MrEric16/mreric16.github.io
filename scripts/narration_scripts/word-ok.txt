This article tells the story of the word "OK." It is one of the most common words on Earth. People say it in almost every language. But where did it come from? For many years, nobody knew.

There were lots of guesses. Some Germans said it came from the short form of a military rank. Some French people said it came from the name of a port town in Haiti. Others said it was short for "open key," a term from the old telegraph. And some said a famous American general picked it up from a word in a Native American language.

The answer came in the 1960s. A scholar at Columbia University, in New York, decided to dig into old newspapers. First, he proved that the earlier guesses were wrong. One of them was even about a brand of biscuits from the Civil War. Then he found the real story.

It starts in the year 1839. In New England, people had a funny craze. They loved to make short forms of words. It was a bit like people today who write "BTW" or "NBD." One Boston newspaper led the way. Its editor made up many short forms. He wrote "SP" for "small potatoes." He wrote "KG" for "know go," which was a joke spelling of "no go."

Most of these short forms disappeared. But one stayed. On the twenty-third of March, 1839, the editor wrote "OK" in an article. He meant "oll korrect," a silly spelling of "all correct." He was making fun of a newspaper from a nearby city.

At first, "OK" was just a joke between two editors. Then, in the next year, it got a big push. A man named Martin Van Buren was running to be President. He came from a town called Kinderhook, in New York, and people nicknamed him "Old Kinderhook." His supporters made a slogan: "Vote for OK!" They even started "OK Clubs."

It did not help him much. The voters did not think he was "OK" enough, and he lost. But the word lived on. It spread across North America, and then across the world. In the end, it even reached the Moon, when an astronaut said it there.

Some scholars still disagree. A later study said the word might have several roots, and one could be a Scottish phrase meaning "oh, yes." Still, the newspaper story is one of the best explanations we have. So the next time you say "OK," remember. It began as a joke in a newspaper.
