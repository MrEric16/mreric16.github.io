This article tells the story of a famous football. It is the ball that was used in the 1966 World Cup final, when England won. Now, sixty years later, scientists have looked inside it.

First, the final itself. It was played at Wembley on the thirtieth of July, 1966. England beat West Germany four goals to two. About ninety-seven thousand people watched in the stadium. Thirty million more watched on black-and-white television. One English player scored three goals in that match.

After the game, something strange happened. A German player who had scored the first goal picked up the ball and took it home. He said that it was a tradition that the first player to score could keep it. So the ball stayed in Germany for thirty years.

In the end, it came back. Before the European championship in 1996, a group of companies paid eighty thousand pounds to bring it home to England. Some letters on the ball belong to a famous man who does tricks, who signed it for luck before the tournament. Today, the ball is in the National Football Museum, next to the shirt of the player who scored three goals.

Now the new part. Scientists at a university in Manchester took the ball and made the first 3D X-ray pictures of it. They could see everything. They saw the outside, made of leather in twenty-five panels, all sewn by hand. And they saw the rubber bag inside, which has been falling apart since the day of the final.

The pictures show an old craft. Each ball took two and a half hours to sew by hand, at a factory in a town in Yorkshire. The ball had to follow strict rules. It had to weigh between fourteen and sixteen ounces. And it had to be twenty-seven to twenty-eight inches around. This ball won its place in the final by winning a test. People kicked more than one hundred footballs at the headquarters of the football association, without knowing which was which.

Why was it orange? It was not for style. It was so that people could see it on the black-and-white television sets of that time.

The scientists also compared it with a ball from this year's World Cup final. That ball is called the Trionda. It has only four panels, stuck together with heat, and made from modern materials. It even has a sensor inside that records every touch. In 1966, the technology was a needle and thread.

One scientist said that nobody had ever looked inside such an important football before. And now, sixty years on, the ball has finally shown its secrets.
