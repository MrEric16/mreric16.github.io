This article tells the story of the word "panic." A dictionary says it means a sudden, overpowering fright. You can use it as a noun, and you can use it as a verb. But either way, the word comes from a god of ancient Greece.

His name was Pan. In the old stories, he had the top half of a man and the bottom half of a goat. He was linked with many things. These included springtime, farming, hunting, shepherds, meadows, and the wild. Nobody is sure where he came from. Ancient stories give many different answers about his parents. He lived in the wild, in the mountains and forests of a region called Arcadia, in the south of Greece. It was quiet and far away from the big towns. There, he played a simple set of pipes. We still call them panpipes today.

Pan was different from many other Greek gods. People did not worship him in big temples in the cities. They went to caves, fields, and clearings in the forest. Shepherds asked him to keep their sheep healthy. Hunters asked him to help them catch animals. They hoped that he would listen. The people of Arcadia thought that Pan could do more than help with sheep and hunting. He could also cause trouble.

Let's look at the trouble. Pan could scare people. He was believed to live deep in the thick woods, where nobody could see him. So when something strange happened, people blamed him. Sometimes a herd of animals suddenly jumped up and ran away, for no reason at all. Pan did that, people said. Sometimes the animal that a hunter was following suddenly escaped. That was Pan, too.

And it was not only animals. The wild places are full of strange sounds. There are hoots, cries, howls, and barks. If you were alone in the mountains, or lost far from home, these noises could frighten you very much. People said this was also the work of Pan.

There is one more story. In a famous old battle, at a place called Marathon, an army from Persia is said to have turned and run away in terror. Some people said it was because they heard the cry of Pan.

So, when you panic, you feel what Pan could make people and animals feel long ago. That sudden, wild feeling of fear that makes you want to run. And all of this started with a half-goat god, who liked to play his pipes in the quiet hills.
