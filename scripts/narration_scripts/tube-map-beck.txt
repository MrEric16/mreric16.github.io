This article is about the London Underground map. You have probably seen it. It is very simple. But it was not always that way. And the man who made it was almost never thanked.

The Underground is old. The first line opened in 1863. It was the first underground railway in the world. Over time, more lines were built. Different companies ran them. Each company made its own map.

Later, in 1908, the companies joined together under one name, the Underground. Then they made one map for everything. It used colours for the lines. Some of those colours are still used today.

But there was a big problem. The map copied the real streets and distances. So it looked like a messy bowl of noodles. The middle of London was very crowded. The names of the stations were too close together. It was hard to read. People tried to fix it. They made the centre bigger. They took off the street names. They straightened some lines. But it was still not good enough.

Then a young man had an idea. His name was Harry. He worked for the Underground as a drawer of plans. His job was to draw electrical diagrams. Those diagrams are not like real maps. They do not show real distances. They only show how things connect.

Harry thought, "A passenger does not need to know the real distance. A passenger only needs to know where to change trains." So he drew the Underground like an electrical diagram.

He used only three kinds of lines. They went across, up and down, or at a slant. The corners were tight and neat. And he made the crowded centre much bigger, so every name was easy to read.

At first, the bosses said no. They thought it was too strange. But in 1932, they agreed to a small test. People liked it. So in 1933, the new map was printed. A year later, the boss of the new transport company said it was better than anything before.

Harry kept making the map for many years. But he got little thanks. Later, other people changed it. Some changes were bad. People did not like them. After a few years, designers went back to his ideas.

Harry died in 1974. Not many people knew his name then. But a friend wrote a book about him. After that, people began to remember him. Today, every Tube map says it comes from his design.

His idea still works. New lines have been added again and again. And the map is still easy to read. Cities all over the world copied it. All because he thought about what the passenger really needs.
