This article talks about cheating in the ancient Olympic Games. People often think cheating in sports is a modern problem, but the ancient Greeks had the exact same issue, thousands of years ago.

One common type of cheating was bribery. Rich city-states would offer money to a great athlete from a different city, to make him compete under their name instead. This made the bribing city look stronger. In one case, an athlete switched cities for money, and his old hometown got so angry that they destroyed his statue and turned his house into a jail.

Athletes also bribed each other directly. In one famous case, a boxer paid three other fighters to lose on purpose, just so he could win more easily.

There was also plain cheating during the actual events. Athletes would bite, poke eyes, or even grab sensitive body parts during wrestling or boxing matches. Runners would try to start races early, before the signal.

Some athletes even tried a stranger method: magic. They wrote curses on small pieces of lead and buried them near the competition area, hoping to bring bad luck to their rivals.

But punishments were serious and very public. Cheaters paid heavy fines. Some were even beaten with rods in front of everyone. The fine money was often used to build bronze statues at the Olympic grounds — with the cheater's name written on them, so everyone who walked by would know exactly what they did. Entire cities could be banned from competing. One city, Sparta, was banned completely for several years after breaking the rules.

Even watching the games illegally could get you in trouble. Women were not normally allowed to watch the men's events. One woman disguised herself as a man just to watch her own son compete — and when she was caught, she was punished too.
