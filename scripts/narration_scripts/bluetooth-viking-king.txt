This article is about the name Bluetooth. Where does it come from? The answer is a Viking king, who lived more than one thousand years ago.

First, the technology. Bluetooth connects your phone to your headphones, or your laptop, without any wires. Almost everyone uses it. But the name has nothing to do with technology. It is the nickname of a king who ruled Denmark in the tenth century.

Why Bluetooth? Nobody is completely sure. People think it was because one of his teeth looked blue, or dark and rotten. The nickname was written down about one hundred and fifty years after he died. So even then, people were guessing.

The king did something very big. Around the year nine hundred and sixty-five, he made Denmark a Christian country. Before this, northern Europe was split into many small areas. Each area had its own local leader. The new religion helped the king. It joined his people together, and it made friends for Denmark with the rest of Christian Europe.

You can still see his story today, in a small town called Jelling. Only about four thousand people live there now. But in the king's time, it was the centre of power. In the town, there are two big stones with old Viking letters on them. The older stone was made by the king's father, for his mother. It has the first known written use of the word Denmark. People there call it the birth certificate of the country.

The bigger stone was made by the king himself. It says he won all of Denmark and Norway, and made the Danes Christian. It also shows one of the earliest pictures of Christ in Scandinavia. So people call this stone the baptism certificate of Denmark.

Now, how did a Viking king become a name for technology? In the late nineteen-nineties, engineers from several big companies were building a new way to connect devices. The problem was this. Devices from different brands could not talk to each other. This new technology would fix that. They needed a secret name for the project.

One engineer was working with a team from Sweden. One day, they started talking about Vikings. He found a book about the history, and he loved the story of the king who brought Denmark and Norway together. That is exactly what the new technology would do for devices. It would unite them. So he suggested the name Bluetooth, only for the time being.

Then the name leaked, before the company wrote its official announcement. The marketing people did not like it at all. They thought it sounded like bad teeth. They wanted a boring name that explained the product. But people remembered Bluetooth, and it stayed.

The logo is also from the Viking world. It joins two old Viking letters, the letter for H and the letter for B. Those are the king's initials.

In twenty twenty-three, the inventors visited the museum in Jelling. They joked that they were sorry for using the king's name without asking. The museum accepted their apology. It also said they could keep using the name for the next one thousand years.

The museum worker says the king loved showing off. He would be very happy to know that billions of people say his name every day.
