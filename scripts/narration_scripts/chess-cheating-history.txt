This article is about cheating in chess. It is a very old problem. It started long before computers.

Let's begin with the earliest cheats. In the seventeen hundreds and eighteen hundreds, some clever tricksters built machines that seemed to play chess by themselves. They were called automatons. But they were fakes. A person was hidden inside or controlled them in secret. So the people who built them were maybe the first chess cheats we know of.

The next type of cheating is agreeing to a draw before the game. Players do this to save energy and to improve their chances in a big tournament. This is almost as old as chess tournaments. It was in the news in the year eighteen eighty. The most famous case was in nineteen sixty-two. A young American star finished fourth in a big event. Afterwards, he said that three Russian players had agreed to quick draws among themselves, so they could save their energy for playing against him. Most people today think he was probably right. Even then, the complaint was taken so seriously that the rules of the event were changed.

Then there is a rule called touch-move. If you touch a piece, you must move it. Some players broke this rule. In nineteen sixty-seven, one player made a bad move, took it back, and played a better one. He then got a draw. His opponent complained three times, but the judge had not seen the first part, so nothing happened. Many years later, the same thing happened again. A player touched one piece and then moved a different one.

Not all accusations are true. Some were just strange. At a world championship in nineteen seventy-eight, one side said the other was getting secret signals from a cup of yoghurt. The colour of the yoghurt was supposed to be a code. It is not clear if they were serious. In two thousand six, at another world championship, one player fell behind. His team said the other player was cheating from the toilet. This was not true. The other player went on to win. Winning alone is not proof of cheating, either. In two thousand fifteen, a woman started a big tournament with five wins in a row. People hunted her. But there was no evidence. The person who led the attacks even got a ban for it.

Then computers arrived, and cheating became much more direct. Instead of fixing results, players now use engines to find the best moves. One of the first known cases was in nineteen ninety-three. A man showed up at a tournament, stared at the ceiling most of the time, and still scored well. He did not seem to know chess at all. Someone even brought him a written move. When the organisers asked him to solve a very easy puzzle, he refused and ran away.

In two thousand nineteen, one grandmaster was photographed in a toilet using an engine on his phone. He lost his title and got a six-year ban. A few years before, another player was caught using a phone in a toilet too. A rival player had noticed it, and the judge found the phone with the game still on its screen. The strange part is that the player who spotted it was later caught cheating himself, in an online game.

Some cheats are cleverer. One team in two thousand ten used a plan with a computer outside the hall. A helper sent the moves by text message in a code. Another man stood in the hall and used his body position to tell the player what to play.

So cheating in chess has changed from fake machines to real ones. But the idea is the same. Someone wants to win more than they want to play fair.
