This article is about a jellyfish the size of a fingernail that seems to have found a way around ageing. It is called Turritopsis dohrnii, and scientists nickname it the immortal jellyfish.

Here is what makes it strange. When it is injured, starved or stressed, it does not die. Instead it sinks to the bottom of its tank and collapses into a ball of cells. A day or so later, that ball grows into a polyp, which is a young stage of the jellyfish's life, and the polyp grows into a colony that can produce hundreds of new, genetically identical jellyfish. One marine biologist, Maria Miglietta, has cut them, boiled them and starved them in her lab, and they keep doing it.

The trick was spotted by accident in 1992, when an Italian scientist forgot to feed his jellyfish over a weekend and came back expecting dead animals. Later work showed that some of its adult cells switch back into a stem cell state and then become a completely different type of cell. No other animal is known to turn an adult body back into a baby one in this way.

So why does a medical researcher care? Because if the control switch for this process can be found, perhaps something similar could be triggered in human cells. Nobody is talking about turning old people into babies. The hope is more modest: faster healing after strokes or heart attacks, or repairing damage from age-related vision loss, or even Alzheimer's disease. One researcher says it is a long way off, but possible at the level of tissues and organs.

The clues so far are about maintenance. Compared with a related jellyfish, this one has extra copies of genes that repair damaged DNA and protect telomeres, the little caps on the ends of our DNA that wear down as we age.

Now the twist. The jellyfish is not really immortal. Some animals in the lab cannot rejuvenate at all, there may be a limit to how many times it can reset itself, and in the wild, predators, hunger and algae still kill plenty of them.

As one scientist puts it, life and death are inseparable parts of the same reality. Even nature's most immortal animal has to face death.
