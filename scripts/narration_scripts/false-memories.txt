This article is about a strange fact. Your memory can show you things that never happened. And it feels completely real.

Think about courtroom shows on television. A witness says, I remember it clearly. But scientists say memories are not like videos. They are more like pictures that are blurred by what we imagine, what we believe, and how much time has passed. That is why good lawyers know that memory can be a weak kind of proof.

Here is one experiment. Scientists told a group of students a story about a man who left a restaurant without paying. Half the students were told the man was a bad person who liked to steal. The other half were told he left because he got an emergency phone call. A week later, they were asked about the bill. The first group remembered a bill that was ten to twenty-five percent higher than the real one. The second group remembered a slightly lower bill. So the way we feel about a person can change what we remember.

Here is another experiment. Volunteers looked at pictures, and also imagined other pictures. Later, many of them said they had seen the imagined ones. Brain scans showed why. The parts of the brain that see things and the parts that imagine things overlap. So an imagined picture can leave a trace that looks like a real memory.

A third study went even further. Scientists at a university in America worked with mice. They found brain cells that were active when a mouse got a small shock in one place. Later, in a different, safe place, they switched on those same cells. The mice froze in fear, as if the shock was happening again. Soon they froze even without any help from the scientists. The mice had a false memory of danger.

This shows that false memories and real memories use almost the same parts of the brain. That is why it is so hard to tell them apart.

So the next time you say, I remember it perfectly, be careful. Your brain may be telling you a story.
