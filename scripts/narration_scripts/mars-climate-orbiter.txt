This article tells the story of a very expensive mistake. A space probe cost one hundred and twenty-five million dollars. And it was lost because of a mix-up in units.

The probe was called the Mars Climate Orbiter. It was built by NASA. It weighed about three hundred and forty kilograms. It was launched on the eleventh of December, 1998. Its job was to study the planet Mars. It also had a second job. It was meant to pass messages from another NASA machine on Mars back to Earth.

The trip took nine months. On the twenty-third of September, 1999, the probe came near Mars. It began to burn its engine, to slow down and enter its orbit. Everything went as planned. The plan was that the probe would go behind the planet, and then come out again and call home.

But no signal came. The team on Earth waited. There was nothing. The probe had disappeared.

NASA started an investigation. Soon, they found the cause. It was a simple one. The team on Earth sent commands to the spacecraft in English units, like pounds. These are the units used in the United States. But the spacecraft needed the metric units, like newtons. No one changed the numbers.

The mistake was small each time, but it added up. The probe was meant to fly about one hundred and forty kilometres above Mars. In fact, it flew much lower, about fifty-seven kilometres above the surface. At that height, the air of Mars was too thick for the probe. It broke up and burned. The spacecraft was lost forever.

Many people were amazed. One of the project managers said the story became legend. He said it is in every kid's textbook. And everyone was surprised that nobody caught the mistake.

But he said there was a bigger problem. At that time, NASA had a famous saying: "Better, faster, cheaper." The team had to do a lot with very little money. They were pushed too far and too fast.

The lesson is clear. When you go to another planet, you cannot hurry. And you must always check your units.
