This article tells the story of the longest railway in the world. It is the Trans-Siberian Railway. It crosses Russia from Moscow in the west to Vladivostok in the east.

How long is it? About nine thousand three hundred kilometres. That is the longest single train journey on Earth. Today, it takes about one week. The train stops in big cities on the way. Some of them are Nizhny Novgorod, Yekaterinburg, Omsk, Novosibirsk, and Irkutsk.

The project was announced in 1891. It was part of a time of very fast change. In 1860, Russia had about sixteen hundred kilometres of railway. By 1917, it had about seventy-two thousand.

One man was the key to the project. He had worked his way up, step by step. First, he sold tickets. Then he became a stationmaster. Then he became a top manager, and finally a government minister. He was clever. He talked the ruler into making his own son the head of the railway committee. That way, the project always had strong support from the very top.

To pay for the railway, the government took loans, raised taxes, and even printed more money. The work went fast. Maybe too fast. Some parts of the track were not studied well. Workers often used wood that was still green. And a lot of money simply disappeared.

The work was hard. Builders crossed wide, flat lands, rivers, forests, and swamps. The weather was extreme. In the east, the ground stays frozen all year, and builders found it hard to work. There were also bandits, and sometimes even tigers.

The first passenger trains ran in the summer of 1903. At first, there was no track around the great Lake Baikal, so a ferry took the train across. Next year, the track around the lake was finished. It has thirty-three tunnels and more than one hundred bridges.

What was the first train like? For the rich, it was very fancy. The train had bathrooms with marble tiles, a piano in the music room, a library, and a gym. In the dining room, you could eat caviar and fish. But the train was slow, only about thirty-two kilometres an hour. The whole trip took nearly four weeks. In third class, the seats were crowded and not comfortable. And the line had been built so fast that there were many delays.

A modern travel guide has a good description. It says the trip is like being on a sea voyage, or having a beach holiday indoors.
