This article tells the story of the Pet Rock. It was a joke gift that made its inventor a millionaire. And the rock was the smallest part of the idea.

It started in 1975. A man in California worked in advertising, and he did not have much money. One night he was talking with friends. They were all complaining about their pets. Dogs and cats break things. They need walks. They need food. They need a lot of attention.

The man said, "I don't have that problem. I have a pet rock."

Everyone laughed. But he kept thinking about it. He went home and wrote a small guidebook for owning a pet rock. It explained how to look after it. It listed the tricks it could do. The most popular trick was "play dead." And it said the rock would be a loyal friend for a very, very long time.

Then he made the box. It looked like the little box a pet shop uses, with tiny air holes in the sides. Inside, the rock sat in soft wood shavings, like a real animal in a bed.

So the real product was not the rock. It was the joke. You could not just buy a stone. You bought a pet, with a box and a book.

The rocks cost almost nothing. They were smooth stones from a beach in Mexico. He paid less than one cent for each one. He sold them for almost four dollars.

He needed money to start, so two friends gave him some. One of them put in ten thousand dollars. That was a lot in 1975.

At first, he showed the rocks at a gift fair in San Francisco. People loved them. A big shop ordered a thousand rocks. A magazine ran a story with a picture. After that, the news spread fast.

By Christmas, he was selling up to a hundred thousand rocks every day. In only a few months, he sold more than a million. He earned over one million dollars. He bought each of his two friends a car. The friend who gave ten thousand dollars got two hundred thousand back.

But the fun did not last. Fads never do. Other people made copies. After a while, nobody wanted a pet rock. The two friends were also unhappy. They said they had not received enough money, and they took the inventor to court. They won, and he had to pay them more.

He tried new ideas. One was a kit for growing sand. Another was a can that jumped around on a table. Neither one sold well.

Later, he said the leftover rocks did not worry him. If they did not sell, he said, he would use them to cover his driveway.

The big lesson is simple. People did not pay for a stone. They paid for a funny idea, with a great box and a great story.
