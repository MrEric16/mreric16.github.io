This article is about the school years of a very famous scientist. Albert Einstein's old papers are now online. They show a teenager with big plans, and some surprises.

In the year two thousand fourteen, a university press and a research group put more than five thousand of his documents online for free. There are about thirty thousand in total. Before that, they were only in expensive books. The director of the project says she hopes teachers and students will read Einstein in his own words. She says no living scientist has the same place in people's hearts as he had.

Let's look at his teenage years. He left his school in Germany when he was fifteen. He did not have a diploma. But he had a plan. He would pass a school-leaving exam, and then go to a famous technical university in Switzerland. Normally, this university only took students who were eighteen and already had the exam. The head of the university wrote to a family friend. He said it was not a good idea to take a student out of school early, even a child genius. But in the end he allowed Einstein to take the exam.

Einstein's maths and science marks were good. But he was weak in other subjects. So the head told him to do one more year of school. Einstein went to a school in Switzerland. His records say he had private lessons in French, chemistry and natural history. He was excused from singing and gymnastics when he asked. A music teacher wrote that Einstein played the violin, and even sparkled when he played a slow piece by a famous composer, with deep understanding.

When he was seventeen, he got his final marks. In algebra and geometry, he got a six. This is the highest possible mark. In physics, he got five or six. In other subjects, he got fives. Then he took the school-leaving exams. There were seven written exams, and oral exams that were open to the public. His average mark was the best in his class. This gave him direct entry to the university.

One document is a French exam he wrote. In it, he wrote about his future. He said that young people like to think about bold projects. He said he planned to study maths and physics for four years in Zurich, if he was lucky and passed his exams. He wrote that he would probably become a teacher of these subjects, and choose the theory side of science.

He knew what he wanted. But his French had many mistakes. His project director says that in Switzerland, students study German and French for many years. They must be almost bilingual. And Einstein did not work hard at it.

So the great scientist was not a perfect student. But he knew his goal very early.
