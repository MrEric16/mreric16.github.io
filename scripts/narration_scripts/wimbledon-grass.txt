This article explains why the grass at Wimbledon, the famous tennis tournament, is so perfect. The answer is a mix of history, careful work, and a lot of science.

Each blade of grass is cut to eight millimetres. That is very short. And there are about fifty-four million plants on the courts. At the start of the tournament, the grass is thick and green. Two weeks later, hundreds of players have run, slid, and fallen on it. The grass is tired. But it is still alive.

How is that possible? Grass is very tough. The first grasses grew more than seventy million years ago. Over time, grass learned to live with grazing animals. Sheep and cows ate it every day. So grass learned to grow back quickly, even from a tiny green stump.

Tennis was once played on lawns, and for good reason. In Britain, towns often had a big grass field in the middle, where animals ate the grass. People later used these fields for sport. By 1877, the first big tennis tournament happened on a grass court.

But early grass was a mess. Weeds and flowers grew in it. It wore out fast. A horse even pulled the mower in those days!

Over the years, experts began to study the problem. They tested new soil, new plant food, and many types of seeds. They even built a machine that looked like a fake foot in a tennis shoe. It stamped and slid across the grass again and again. This showed them which grass could survive best.

Today, the courts use one plant only: ryegrass. It has short, fine blades and strong roots.

During the tournament, the groundskeepers check the grass every day. They test how hard the ground is, how wet it is, and how close the plants grow. One of them even crawls on the ground to count the stems by hand. They also call weather experts to check the rain, because a little change can matter.

Why do they care so much? Because the ball bounces lower on grass. Players have to move faster and bend lower. If the grass is uneven, the bounce is hard to guess. One former player said you can feel the tiniest change, and if you cannot get used to it, you will not win.

So when you watch a match, remember this. Under the players' feet, a whole team of people and a lot of science are working hard.
