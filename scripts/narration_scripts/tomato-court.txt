This article asks a very simple question. Is a tomato a fruit or a vegetable? In 1893, the highest court in the United States had to decide. And it was about a lot of money.

Here is the story. In 1883, the country passed a law about taxes on goods from other countries. Vegetables from abroad had to pay a tax. Fruit did not.

A family in New York ran a business that sold fruit and vegetables. They had been in business since 1839. They imported tomatoes, and they were made to pay the tax. They paid, but they were not happy. They said a tomato is a fruit, so the tax was not fair. So they took the tax collector of the port of New York to court.

The family had a strong point. Scientists say a tomato is a fruit. It grows from the flower of a plant, and it holds the seeds. The family brought dictionaries to court. They also brought two men who had worked in the food trade for about thirty years. Both agreed on what the words meant. Neither said that tomatoes counted as something special in the trade.

The tax collector had an answer too. His lawyers read out meanings of other foods, like peas, cucumbers, squash and peppers. All of them are botanical fruits. But people treat them as vegetables.

The judges decided that the tomato is a vegetable. The vote was unanimous. One judge wrote the decision. He said that in a case like this, the words in the law have their everyday meaning. In everyday life, people eat tomatoes with the main meal. They do not usually eat them as dessert. So they are vegetables.

The judge did not say that the scientists were wrong. He agreed that, as a plant, the tomato is a fruit. But the law was written for ordinary people, so it used ordinary words.

The case is still used as an example today. It shows that a word can mean one thing in science and another in normal life. And in the courtroom, normal life won.
