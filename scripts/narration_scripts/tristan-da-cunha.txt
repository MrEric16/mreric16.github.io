This article tells about the most remote place in the world where people live. It is a group of islands in the middle of the South Atlantic Ocean. It is called Tristan da Cunha.

About two hundred and fifty people live there. All of them live on one island, the biggest of the group. And all of them live in one village, which has a very long name: Edinburgh of the Seven Seas. Most of the people are farmers. They are citizens of Britain's overseas territories.

How far away is it? The nearest inhabited land is another island, called St Helena. It is about twenty-four hundred kilometres away. There is no easy way to get to Tristan. You cannot fly there. You must take a boat from South Africa, and the trip takes six days.

The main island is about twelve kilometres across. In the middle of it, there is a cone-shaped volcano, called Queen Mary's Peak. It is about two thousand metres high. At the top, inside the crater, there is a lake in the shape of a heart. In winter, it freezes. In summer, it melts again. People who climb up to the top can have a very cold swim.

The island is also a great place to watch animals. Seals and big sea birds called albatrosses live there. A kind of small penguin, called the northern rockhopper, has its babies on the shore.

Now, how did these islands appear in the middle of the ocean? The story is very old. It starts about one hundred and thirty million years ago. At that time, South America and Africa were not separate. They were joined together as one huge piece of land. But then the land began to break apart, and the two continents slowly moved away from each other. The Atlantic Ocean grew in the space between them.

Deep under the ground, there is a layer of very hot rock. In one place there, a huge stream of heat rises up. Scientists call this a hot spot. As the earth's crust slid over the hot spot, one volcano grew after another, in a long line under the sea. Each one grew taller and taller. At last, they came up above the water, and they became islands. After a time, each volcano stopped working, as it moved away from the hot spot.

The islands of Tristan are part of this long line. The main island is the second one from the east. Four other islands in the group are older. And one more island, called Gough, is the youngest.

So the six islands of Tristan da Cunha are like a diary of the breaking of the continents. They are the quiet leftovers of a very old, very big event. Today, only one of them has a village. If you ever want to get away from everyone, this is the place. Just be ready for a long trip on the sea.
