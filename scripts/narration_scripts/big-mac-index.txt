This article talks about the Big Mac index. It is a simple way to check if a country's money is strong or weak. And it uses a burger to do it.

Here is the big idea. A Big Mac is almost the same burger everywhere in the world. So if it costs much more in one country than in another, something about the money may be off.

A magazine called The Economist started the index in 1986. At first, it was just a joke. It was a light and fun way to explain a hard idea. The idea is called purchasing power. It asks one question: how much can the same money buy in different places? Today, the index covers more than fifty countries, and the magazine updates it regularly.

So how does it work? There are two steps.

Step one. Take the price of the burger in another country, and divide it by the price in the United States. This gives you a pretend exchange rate. It is the rate that would make the burger cost the same everywhere.

Step two. Compare the pretend rate with the real rate on the market. If the two rates are far apart, the money is probably too cheap or too expensive.

Let's try Japan. In the article's example, a burger there costs 480 yen. In the United States, it costs almost six dollars. That gives a pretend rate of about 83 yen for one dollar. But the real rate was over 150 yen. So the yen looked almost half its fair price. We call that undervalued. When money looks too expensive, we call it overvalued.

The article also shows where the burger costs the most and the least. Switzerland had the most expensive Big Mac. Taiwan had the cheapest.

What else can the index show? It can show how much people earn, and how costly it is to run a business. It can show if a government is pushing its exchange rate in one direction. And because people have tracked it for forty years, it also shows how prices go up over time.

But the index has limits. A burger is only one product. A real economy has thousands of things. The price of beef, bread, rent and workers is different in each place. Exchange rates also move for other reasons, like interest rates and politics. And a Big Mac means different things in different places. In some countries, it is cheap fast food. In others, it is a treat for a special day.

So nobody should use it to make big money decisions. But it is a clever and easy way to start thinking. Why does money buy more in some places than in others? That is why people still talk about it today.
