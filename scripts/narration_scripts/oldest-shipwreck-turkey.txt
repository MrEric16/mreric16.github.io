This article is about the oldest shipwreck ever found. It lies off the south coast of Turkey, near a small island called Kekova.

The ship is very, very old. It sank between four thousand four hundred and four thousand seven hundred years ago. That is about the time people were building the great pyramids in Egypt.

The wood of the ship is gone. But the clay jars it carried are still there. These jars are called amphorae. People used them to carry things like olive oil or wine. Experts say the clay came from workshops far away, in the Jordan Valley. And jars like these have been found in Egypt, in Troy and on the island of Crete. So this ship was part of a huge trade across the sea.

One jar is different. It has two holes in its side and nine marks. Experts think it may have been used to make cheese or butter. If that is true, sailors may have kept goats on board and milked them at sea.

One more thing. The Turkish minister called this the oldest shipwreck in the world. That is what he says, and the article does not prove it. But it is still a wonderful look at life almost five thousand years ago.
