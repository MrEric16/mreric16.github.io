This article is about the walls of Pompeii. Two thousand years ago, people there wrote on them all the time. And it sounds a lot like the internet.

First, a quick reminder. Pompeii was a busy Roman city. In the year 79, a nearby volcano blew up. Ash and rock fell on the city for hours. By the next night, Pompeii was buried under about six metres of it.

Today, people dig it up. They find art, houses and shops. But some of the best finds are small. They are words on walls.

Experts have counted more than eleven thousand of them in Pompeii. Shopkeepers wrote them. Workers wrote them. Soldiers wrote them. Even enslaved people wrote them. Some are quick scribbles. Some are neat, with real skill. Kids drew gladiators they saw fight in the arena. The drawings are rough, just like a kid's drawing today.

What did people write about? Almost everything. They said hello to friends. They wrote love notes. They wrote jokes, poems and insults. They wrote reviews of places to eat. One building near the arena has more than four hundred messages. It was the most popular spot for writing.

Some walls look like a comment thread. Two brothers left notes for each other on a friend's wall. On one staircase, people added their own funny lines to the same short poem, one after another. Almost everyone in the city could read. So a wall could be a place to talk.

There was also politics. Professional painters wrote big notices to support people in an election. They said a person was honest and good with money. They promised free bread and free shows. Sometimes they attacked a rival. They said he was friends with drunks and thieves. So the Romans argued about their city, just like we do.

And people loved to brag. One man wrote that a gladiator was the idol of all the girls. He was probably the gladiator! Two men even fought in writing over a woman who worked in a bar. They called each other names on the wall.

So we should be careful. Graffiti is not always true. It is only what one person wanted to say.

But there is a favourite joke. Many walls say the same thing. They say, "Wall, I am surprised you have not fallen down. You carry so many scribbles."

The tools are different now. We use phones, not sharp sticks. But the need is the same. People want to talk, to joke, to be seen and to feel close to others. That is why this old graffiti feels so close to us today.
