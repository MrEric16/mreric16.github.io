This article tells the story of the biggest wooden plane ever flown. People call it the Spruce Goose. It flew only once, for less than a minute. But it is still amazing.

The plane was huge. It was as tall as an eight-floor building. Its wings were as wide as a football field. And it was built to carry a great deal of cargo over the sea.

The idea started in the Second World War. Enemy submarines were sinking many cargo ships in the Atlantic Ocean. So someone had a wild idea. What if the cargo went through the air instead? What if giant flying boats carried it?

A famous ship builder liked this idea. But he did not know how to build planes. So he asked for help from a rich man who loved aeroplanes. This man was a record-breaking pilot, and he was also a film maker. Together, they agreed to build the biggest plane in the world.

There was a problem. During the war, metal like aluminium was hard to find. The army needed it for other planes. So the team used wood. They glued thin layers of wood together using heat and pressure. This made the wood very strong and light.

By the way, the name is wrong. There was very little spruce in it. Most of it was birch. And it was not a goose, but the name stuck.

The numbers are crazy. The plane had eight huge engines. It used about eight hundred gallons of fuel every hour. Eight tons of nails held the outside in place. And every glue line was checked by hand.

Many experts did not believe that it could fly. They thought the wooden joints could break. Then the war ended, and the plane was no longer needed. Even so, the rich man refused to give up.

Moving the plane was a big job. The parts travelled to the harbour at only two miles an hour. Roads were closed. Trees were cut back. And workers lifted thousands of power lines out of the way.

Finally, on the second of November, 1947, the test day came. The pilot sat in the front, with engineers, crew, and reporters on board. He said it was only a test on the water. But then he pushed the engines, and the huge plane sped across the water. At eighty miles an hour, it left the water. It flew for about thirty seconds, twenty-five feet above the sea.

One person remembered the radio that day. You can hear the engines, and the plane bouncing on the waves. Then, suddenly, everything goes quiet. Everyone realised it was in the air.

It never flew again. But for more than seventy years, it was the plane with the widest wings in history.

Today, you can see it in a museum in Oregon. Some experts say it might still be able to fly. Who knows?
