This article tells a strange true story. A young man tried to win a fighter jet from a soft drink company. Yes, really.

In 1995, a drink company ran a big prize game. You drank their cola, collected points, and traded the points for free things. A shirt cost seventy-five points. A leather jacket cost a few more.

Then they made a funny TV advert. A teenager goes to school. He looks at the prize catalogue, and he has a cold drink in his hand. Suddenly, a fighter jet flies over the school. It lands next to the bicycles. The wind is so strong that a teacher loses his clothes. At the end, the words on the screen say: "Fighter jet, seven million points."

The company thought it was only a joke.

One young man did not think so. He was a business student, twenty-one years old, and he did the maths. A jet like that costs about thirty-three million dollars. But seven million points is not so much money. The game had a rule in small print. You could buy extra points for ten cents each. So he worked out the price. It was about seven hundred thousand dollars. For a fighter jet, that is cheap.

He found friends who agreed to put in money. Then he sent his order. He sent the points, an order form, and a cheque. On the form he wrote one thing: "One fighter jet."

The company sent the cheque back. They said the jet was not part of the game. It was only a joke in the advert. They also sent some free coupons to say sorry.

The young man did not stop. His lawyer wrote a very serious letter. It said the advert clearly offered a jet, and he had followed all the rules. The company still said no. So he took them to court.

The case went on for three years. Many people thought he was right. After all, the advert really did say seven million points! But the judge disagreed. The judge said a normal, sensible person would not think the advert was a real offer.

There was another problem. Adverts are usually not promises. They are just invitations to buy something. And no contract existed, because the company never accepted his order.

So who knows what would have happened if he had won? The answer is simple. The army would not have allowed it. A fighter jet is a weapon. It is also very expensive to fly and to look after.

He tried one more time in the year 2000, but the court said no again.

After this, the company changed the advert. The jet now cost seven hundred million points.

The lesson is clear. Always read the small print. And be careful when an advert makes a joke.
