This article answers a question we all know. Why does a song get stuck in your head, and why will it not go away?

First, a surprising number. In some surveys, nine out of ten people said they have had this. For about one in three of them, it is annoying. There is a name for it: an earworm. The word comes from German. It means a musical itch. A doctor first used it in 1979.

An earworm is a small piece of a song, usually about twenty seconds long. It pops into your mind by itself. Then it plays again and again, like a short video on repeat.

Why do some songs get stuck more than others? The experts give a few reasons. The song repeats a lot. Some of its notes are held longer. The notes are close together, so the tune does not jump around. Also, a song gets stuck more easily if it makes you feel something strongly. The same goes for a song that reminds you of a moment in your life.

Now, what is going on inside the brain? Scientists have looked at brain scans. Three parts of the brain work together. One part hears music. Another part stores memories. A third part is about feelings, both good and bad. When these parts get linked together, the brain gets stuck in a loop. It plays the music again and again, without your choice.

There is also a reason from history. Long before people could write, they told their stories with music and rhymes. This helped them remember. So our brains learned to hold on to short pieces of tune. The earworm may be a leftover from that time.

So, how do you stop one? There are three ideas. The first is to give your brain something else to do. A puzzle or a hard task uses the same parts of the brain, and it pulls them away from the song. The second is to relax. Just let the song play and try not to think about it. And the third is to play a real song. A new tune can push out the old one.
