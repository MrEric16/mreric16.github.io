This article is about the long trade between ancient Rome and China. It starts with a strange visit, and it ends with a clue about who they really were.

In the year 166, the Chinese court got visitors. They said they came from the Roman emperor. China called Rome "the Great China." They saw it as an equal. So this was the first direct visit between the two empires.

But the Chinese were not happy. The visitors had come by sea, not on the usual road across land. And their gifts were small. They brought things like ivory, horns and turtle shells. So the Chinese officials thought they were just traders, not real messengers.

The article says they may have been right. To see why, we go back in time.

Long before, the Greeks and Romans wanted goods from the East. They wanted spices, jewels and silk. For a long time, trade went by land, which was slow and hard. Then someone found a faster way, by luck.

A patrol in the Red Sea found a sailor from India. He was almost dead. His ship had been lost at sea. He was taken to Egypt, and he learned Greek. Then he offered to show the Greeks the way back to India.

The secret was the wind. In summer, the winds there blow toward India. In winter, they blow back toward Egypt. A captain could use the winds to go and come back. The first trip took only weeks. The ship came home full of spices and jewels. After that, more traders followed.

Later, Rome took over Egypt. The big port city of Alexandria became the door to the East. Goods came by ship to the Red Sea. Then camels carried them across the hot desert to the Nile. The camels walked at night to avoid the heat. Soldiers guarded the water along the way. At the busiest port, up to one hundred and twenty ships left every year.

Then came the silk craze. Rich Romans loved it. Poets wrote about it. But some leaders were angry. They said silk was too soft, especially for men. They even tried to ban it. And silk was so costly that it drained Rome's gold.

The strange part is how little Romans knew. Few travelled farther than India. Many did not know silk came from worms. One famous poet even thought it was combed from the leaves of trees!

There were also routes over land. But another empire stood in the middle. It often kept the trade for itself. So Rome could not deal with China directly. One traveller did make a long trip towards the East. It may have taken almost two years, which was very rare. Most people only knew the East through the silk they saw in the market. And traders loved to make up stories to sell it.

Now back to the visitors in the year 166. Roman coins and jewels have been found in a port area in southern Vietnam. So the visitors may have been traders from there. They probably never saw the emperor at all.

The silk roads grew and fell many times after that. They were strong again hundreds of years later. And today, a high mountain road still links China and Pakistan. It follows a small part of this ancient web.
