This article is about an unusual link between two places far apart: Cornwall, in the south of England, and a part of Mexico called Hidalgo. The link is football, and also a pie.

Let's start with the stadium. In a Mexican football club called Pachuca, fans hold up a huge banner. It shows a miner. In one hand he has a pickaxe. In the other, he has a pasty, which is a pastry with a folded edge. Next to him are two black and white flags from Cornwall. The fans are saying thank you. Cornish miners helped bring football to Mexico.

The story starts in the year eighteen twenty-four. Mexico had just won its independence from Spain after a long war. Its mines were in ruins. They were broken and full of water. A mining engineer from England had already fixed some failing mines in Cornwall. He thought he could do the same in Mexico. So hundreds of Cornish miners travelled to Hidalgo. Over the next years, many went back and forth.

They brought their sports with them. But the first sport was not football. It was cricket. In the eighteen-fifties, a rich Cornish mine owner started a cricket team in Pachuca. The football clubs grew out of the cricket clubs. In fact, some of the players were the same people.

The first football team is mentioned in eighteen ninety-two. The team had a fight inside it. The miners from the town did not agree with the miners from the mountain. A historian laughed when she read this. She said it is very Cornish to love a fight like that. A few years later, the mine owner joined the teams into one stronger club. He gave the club some land. He had only one rule. No games on Sundays, because of his religion.

In nineteen-oh-two, Mexico had its first real football league. A club from another town won it first. They still say they were the first Mexican club, not Pachuca. Pachuca won the league a couple of years later.

Women also played a big part on match days. They came to games, and often wore the club colours. And they made pasties. A pasty has a thick crust. Miners could hold it with dirty hands. It was even strong enough to survive if it was dropped down a mine.

The two places became so close that people heard as much Spanish as English in some bars in Cornwall. One family brought Mexican habits back to England. They ate bread with every meal, and put hot pepper on everything.

In nineteen-oh-eight, the club got its first Mexican player. A Cornish player-manager invited him. People remember that man for breaking down walls between people.

The club stopped for a while after the Mexican Revolution. But it came back, and today it has won the league seven times. Its nickname is the Gophers. A gopher is a small animal that digs underground, just like the miners did. Fans say everything in the town is gopher this and gopher that, even the bus.

And the pasty is still very popular. In Mexico it is called a paste. It is usually filled with beef and vegetables, plus some chilli. There is even a pasty festival and a pasty museum.

So next time you eat a pie at a football game, remember. It might be a very old tradition.
