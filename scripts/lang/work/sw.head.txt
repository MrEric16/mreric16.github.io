INTRO: Swahili is spelled as it sounds. The stress is always on the second-last syllable. It has five pure vowels, like Spanish. Verbs are built from small pieces: who does it, when, then the verb stem. "Ninakula" is ni (I) + na (now) + kula (eat) = I am eating. Nouns are grouped into classes, and the class changes the words that agree with them.
a | like "a" in "father" | baba
e | like "e" in "bed" | nenda
i | like "ee" in "see" | simba
o | like "o" in "more", but short | moja
u | like "oo" in "moon" | kuku
ch | like "ch" in "church" | chai
dh | like "th" in "this" | dhahabu
gh | a rough throat sound | ghali
ng' | like "ng" in "singer", with no "g" sound | ng'ombe
ny | like "ny" in "canyon" | nyama
mb / nd / ng | said as one sound, with a quick "m" or "n" first | mbwa
NOTE: Words with the m- prefix at the start, like "mbwa" (dog) or "mtu" (person), have a short "m" sound before the next letter. Do not add a vowel.
NOTE: Swahili is spoken in Tanzania, Kenya and the region around them. The standard is Tanzanian, with "Kiswahili" the name of the language itself.
