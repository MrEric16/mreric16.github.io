[grammar]
## Pronouns
> Romani has separate subject and object forms. Subject: me, tu, vov / voj, amen, tumen, on. "Tu" is informal and "tumen" is the polite you, as well as the plural.
row: I | me | me
row: you (one) | tu | too
row: he | vov | vov
row: she | voj | voy
row: we | amen | A-men
row: you (plural, polite) | tumen | TOO-men
row: they | on | on
ex: I am a student | me som studento | me som stoo-DEN-to
ex: you are tall | tu san baro | too san BA-ro
ex: she is my older sister | voj si miri bari pheň | voy see MEE-ree BA-ree phen
ex: we are friends | amen sam amala | A-men sam a-MA-la
ex: they are teachers | on si sikavne | on see see-KAV-ne
## Singular and plural
> Nouns change their ending: o ćhavo (the boy), e ćhave (the boys); i ćhaj (the girl), e ćhaja (the girls). The article is o (masculine), i (feminine), e (plural).
row: the book | i kenvi | ee ken-VEE
row: the books | e kenvi | e ken-VEE
row: the child | o ćhavoro | o cha-VO-ro
row: the children | e ćhavore | e cha-VO-re
ex: one house, houses | jekh kher, kher | yekh kher, kher
ex: one book, books | jekh kenvi, kenvi | yekh ken-VEE, ken-VEE
ex: one cat, cats | jekh mačka, mačke | yekh MACH-ka, MACH-ke
ex: two books | duj kenvi | dooy ken-VEE
ex: the children | e ćhavore | e cha-VO-re
## To be: som, san, si
> Present: me som, tu san, vov / voj si, amen sam, tumen san, on si. Past: me sim, tu sian, vov sas, amen sam, tumen san, on sas.
row: I am | me som | me som
row: you are | tu san | too san
row: he / she is | vov / voj si | vov / voy see
row: we are | amen sam | A-men sam
ex: I am a student | me som studento | me som stoo-DEN-to
ex: you are tired | tu san thaklo | too san THAK-lo
ex: he is a doctor | vov si doktoro | vov see dok-TO-ro
ex: we are happy | amen sam bahtale | A-men sam bakh-TA-le
ex: they are at home | on si ando kher | on see AN-do kher
## Having: si man
> Romani says "there is to me": si man. I have: si man. You have: si tut. He has: si leske. She has: si laske. We have: si amen. To say not have, use "nane": nane man.
row: I have a car | si man mašina | see man ma-SHEE-na
row: you have a car | si tut mašina | see toot ma-SHEE-na
row: he has a car | si leske mašina | see LES-ke ma-SHEE-na
row: I do not have | nane man | NA-ne man
ex: I have an older brother | si man jekh baro phral | see man yekh BA-ro phral
ex: I have a car | si man mašina | see man ma-SHEE-na
ex: I do not have time | nane man vrama | NA-ne man VRA-ma
ex: there is a problem | si jekh problema | see yekh pro-BLE-ma
ex: there is no water | nane pani | NA-ne PA-nee
## Present tense
> Verbs end in -av (I), -es (you), -el (he / she), -as (we), -en (you plural / they). Example: xav, xas, xal, xas, xan. Add "kamav te" for want to.
row: I eat | xav | khav
row: you eat | xas | khas
row: he / she eats | xal | khal
row: we eat | xas | khas
ex: I am eating bread | xav maro | khav MA-ro
ex: you are reading a book | genes jekh kenvi | GE-nes yekh ken-VEE
ex: she works in a bank | voj kerel buti ando banka | voy KE-rel BOO-tee AN-do BAN-ka
ex: we drink tea | pijas ćaj | PEE-yas chay
ex: they are sleeping | on sovel | on SO-vel
## Basic sentences
> Word order is flexible. The usual pattern is subject, verb, object. Romani has no verb "to be" in some sentences when the meaning is clear.
ex: I read a book | genav jekh kenvi | GE-nav yekh ken-VEE
ex: he drinks tea | vov pijel ćaj | vov PEE-yel chay
ex: we go to school | džas ki škola | jas kee SHKO-la
ex: they eat bread | on xan maro | on khan MA-ro
ex: the teacher writes | o sikavno ćhinel | o see-KAV-no CHEE-nel
## Negation
> Put "na" before the verb: me na xav (I do not eat). For "there is not", use "nane". Never use na after a negative word: khanči (nothing), ni jekh (nobody).
row: I do not eat | me na xav | me na khav
row: I do not know | me na žanav | me na ZHA-nav
row: there is not | nane | NA-ne
row: nothing | khanči | KHAN-chee
ex: I want | kamav | KA-mav
ex: I do not want | na kamav | na KA-mav
ex: I speak English | rakirav anglikanes | ra-KEE-rav ang-LEE-ka-nes
ex: I do not speak English | na rakirav anglikanes | na ra-KEE-rav ang-LEE-ka-nes
ex: I like it | pasandzol man | pa-SAN-dzol man
ex: I do not like it | na pasandzol man | na pa-SAN-dzol man
ex: she is here | voj si kathe | voy see KA-te
ex: she is not here | voj nane kathe | voy NA-ne KA-te
ex: I have a car | si man mašina | see man ma-SHEE-na
ex: I do not have a car | nane man mašina | NA-ne man ma-SHEE-na
ex: I understand | haćarav | HA-cha-rav
ex: I do not understand | na haćarav | na HA-cha-rav
ex: we are tired | sam thakle | sam THAK-le
ex: we are not tired | na sam thakle | na sam THAK-le
ex: he works on Sundays | vov kerel buti duminika | vov KE-rel BOO-tee doo-MEE-nee-ka
ex: he does not work on Sundays | vov na kerel buti duminika | vov na KE-rel BOO-tee doo-MEE-nee-ka
## Asking questions
> Questions have no special word order. Raise your voice at the end. You can also start with "ma" or put a question word in front.
row: is this your book? | si kava ćiri kenvi? | see KA-va CHEE-ree ken-VEE
row: are they here? | si on kathe? | see on KA-te
ex: is this your book? | si kava ćiri kenvi? | see KA-va CHEE-ree ken-VEE
ex: are they here? | si on kathe? | see on KA-te
ex: do you like tea? | pasandzol tut o ćaj? | pa-SAN-dzol toot o chay
ex: is it cold? | si šilalo? | see shee-LA-lo
ex: do you speak English? | rakires anglikanes? | ra-KEE-res ang-LEE-ka-nes
## Question words
> so (what), kon (who), kaj (where), kana (when), sostar (why), sar (how), sode (how much), savo (which).
row: what | so | so
row: who | kon | kon
row: where | kaj | kay
row: when | kana | KA-na
row: why | sostar | SOS-tar
row: how | sar | sar
row: how much | sode | SO-de
row: which | savo | SA-vo
ex: what is your name? | sar akhardol tuke? | sar a-khar-DOL TOO-ke
ex: who is he? | kon si vov? | kon see vov
ex: where do you live? | kaj bešes? | kay BE-shes
ex: when is the lesson? | kana si i lekcia? | KA-na see ee LEK-tsi-a
ex: why are you late? | sostar san ko pheder? | SOS-tar san ko phe-DER
ex: how much is it? | sode kerel? | SO-de ke-REL
ex: which book? | savi kenvi? | SA-vee ken-VEE
## Possessives and liking
> Possessives agree with the thing owned: miro (my, masculine), miri (my, feminine), mire (my, plural). Same for tiro (your), leskero (his), lakero (her), amaro (our), tumaro (your plural), lengero (their).
row: my | miro / miri | MEE-ro / MEE-ree
row: your | tiro / tiri | TEE-ro / TEE-ree
row: his | leskero / leskeri | LES-ke-ro
row: our | amaro / amari | a-MA-ro
ex: my phone | miro telefono | MEE-ro te-le-FO-no
ex: your house (formal) | tumaro kher | too-MA-ro kher
ex: his sister | leskeri pheň | LES-ke-ree phen
ex: I like music | kamav i muzika | KA-mav ee MOO-zee-ka
ex: I love football | kamav o futbal | KA-mav o FOOT-bal
## Formal vs informal
> Romani uses "tu" for one friend and "tumen" for respect or many people. The verb also changes: tu san (you are), tumen san (you, polite). It is respectful to use tumen with elders.
row: you (informal) | tu | too
row: you (formal) | tumen | TOO-men
ex: how are you? (informal) | sar san? | sar san
ex: how are you? (formal) | sar san tumen? | sar san TOO-men
ex: where are you going? (informal) | kaj džas? | kay JAS
ex: where are you going? (formal) | kaj džan tumen? | kay jan TOO-men
ex: please sit down (formal) | beshen, mangav tumen | BE-shen, MAN-gav TOO-men
## Past and future (simple)
> Past: add -ahm, -an, -as, -am, -an, -e. Example: kerdem (I did), kerdan (you did), kerdas (he did). Future: add -a to the verb: kerava (I will do), kereha (you will).
row: I did | kerdem | KER-dem
row: you did | kerdan | KER-dan
row: he did | kerdas | KER-das
row: I will do | kerava | ke-RA-va
ex: yesterday I was tired | kerdivěs sim thaklo | ker-DEE-vyes seem THAK-lo
ex: I will study tomorrow | tehara sikljovava | te-HA-ra see-klyo-VA-va
ex: we went to the park | geleam ando parko | ge-LEAM AN-do PAR-ko
ex: I saw him | dikhlem les | DEEKH-lem les
ex: she wrote a letter | voj ćhindas jekh vudar | voy CHIN-das yekh VOO-dar
## Commands
> Command to one person: use the root of the verb (ač, dža). To several, add -en (ačen, džan). To forbid, say "ma" plus the verb: ma ač (do not stay).
row: come | av | av
row: go | dža | ja
row: sit | beš | besh
row: do not | ma | ma
ex: come here | av kathe | av KA-te
ex: do not come here | ma av kathe | ma av KA-te
ex: sit down | beš tele | besh TE-le
ex: do not sit down | ma beš tele | ma besh TE-le
ex: be quiet | ačh ćipo | ach CHEE-po
ex: do not be late | ma avel ko pheder | ma A-vel ko phe-DER
ex: open the door | putar o vudar | poo-TAR o VOO-dar
ex: close the window | phand o prozori | phand o pro-ZO-ree
ex: listen to me | šun man | shoon man
ex: do not worry | ma daraj | ma da-RAY
ex: speak slowly | rakir pomali | ra-KEER po-MA-lee
ex: wait a moment | ažutav tut jekh minuta | a-ZHOO-tav toot yekh mee-NOO-ta
## Can, must, want
> Can: šaj + verb. Cannot: našti + verb. Must: trubul te or mušaj te. Want: kamav te + verb.
row: can | šaj | shay
row: cannot | našti | NASH-tee
row: must | mušaj | MOO-shay
row: want to | kamav te | KA-mav te
ex: I can swim | šaj nuvav | shay noo-VAV
ex: I cannot swim | našti nuvav | NASH-tee noo-VAV
ex: can you help me? | šaj ažutis man? | shay a-ZHOO-tis man
ex: we must go | mušaj te džas | MOO-shay te jas
ex: we must not go | na mušaj te džas | na MOO-shay te jas
ex: I want to eat | kamav te xav | KA-mav te khav
ex: I do not want to eat | na kamav te xav | na KA-mav te khav
ex: you can come | šaj aves | shay A-ves
ex: you cannot come | našti aves | NASH-tee A-ves
ex: she wants to study | voj kamel te siklol | voy KA-mel te SEE-klol
ex: I must work today | mušaj te kerav buti adaleske | MOO-shay te KE-rav BOO-tee a-da-LES-ke
ex: you must not run | na mušaj te našes | na MOO-shay te NA-shes
## In, on, at
> Romani uses endings and little words: ando (in), pe (on), tel (under), paš (next to), pašal (near), ko (at / to), anglal (in front of).
row: in | ando | AN-do
row: on | pe | pe
row: under | tel | tel
row: next to | paš | pash
ex: the book is on the table | i kenvi si pe skamind | ee ken-VEE see pe ska-MIND
ex: the cat is under the table | i mačka si tel o skamind | ee MACH-ka see tel o ska-MIND
ex: I am at school | som ki škola | som kee SHKO-la
ex: I am at home | som ando kher | som AN-do kher
ex: the shop is next to the bank | o dukjani si paš e banka | o doo-KYA-nee see pash e BAN-ka
ex: the park is near the station | o parko si paš i stacia | o PAR-ko see pash ee STA-tsi-a
ex: we meet in the morning | dikhas amen ando tosara | DEE-khas A-men AN-do to-SA-ra
ex: the lesson is at nine | i lekcia si ko enja | ee LEK-tsi-a see ko EN-ya
ex: the key is in the bag | o ćavi si ando tasko | o CHA-vee see AN-do TAS-ko
## Comparing things
> Add "maj" (more) before an adjective: maj baro (bigger). The most is "sa maj" (maj baro anda sa). Than is "sar".
row: big | baro | BA-ro
row: bigger | maj baro | may BA-ro
row: the biggest | o maj baro | o may BA-ro
row: than | sar | sar
ex: big, bigger, the biggest | baro, maj baro, o maj baro | BA-ro, may BA-ro, o may BA-ro
ex: small, smaller, the smallest | tikno, maj tikno, o maj tikno | TEEK-no, may TEEK-no, o may TEEK-no
ex: this is better than that | kava si maj lačho sar kova | KA-va see may LA-cho sar KO-va
ex: this is worse than that | kava si maj bilačho sar kova | KA-va see may bee-LA-cho sar KO-va
ex: she is taller than me | voj si maj bari sar me | voy see may BA-ree sar me
ex: Tashkent is bigger than Samarkand | o Tashkent si maj baro sar o Samarkand | o tash-KENT see may BA-ro sar o sa-mar-KAND
ex: it is the cheapest | si o maj lešno | see o may LESH-no
ex: the same as | sar | sar
