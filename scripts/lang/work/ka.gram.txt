[grammar]
## Pronouns
> Georgian often drops the pronoun, because the verb shows who is speaking. There is no he / she difference: "is" means both.
row: I | me
row: you (informal) | shen
row: he / she / it | is
row: we | chven
row: you (formal or plural) | tkven
row: they | isini
ex: I am a student | me st'udent'i var | meh stoo-DEN-tee VAR
ex: you are tall | shen maghali khar | shen mah-GHAH-lee KHAR
ex: she is my older sister | is chemi didi da aris | ees CHEH-mee DEE-dee dah AH-ris
ex: we are friends | chven megobrebi vart | chven meh-GO-breh-bee VART
ex: they are teachers | isini mast'savleblebi arian | ee-SEE-nee mahs-tsahv-LEH-bleh-bee ah-ree-AHN
## Singular and plural
> Add -ebi to the noun. After a number, the noun stays singular: ori ts'igni (two book).
row: ts'igni (book) | ts'ignebi
row: sakhli (house) | sakhlebi
row: bavshvi (child) | bavshvebi
row: after a number: singular | ori ts'igni
ex: one house, houses | erti sakhli, sakhlebi | EHR-tee SAKH-lee sakh-LEH-bee
ex: one book, books | erti ts'igni, ts'ignebi | EHR-tee TSEEG-nee TSEEG-neh-bee
ex: one cat, cats | erti k'at'a, k'at'ebi | EHR-tee KAH-tah KAH-teh-bee
ex: two books | ori ts'igni | OH-ree TSEEG-nee
ex: the children | bavshvebi | BAHV-shveh-bee
## To be: var, khar, aris
> "To be" is a real verb. Put it after the noun or adjective.
row: I am | var
row: you are | khar
row: he / she is | aris
row: we are | vart
row: you are (formal) | khart
row: they are | arian
ex: I am a student | me st'udent'i var | meh stoo-DEN-tee VAR
ex: you are tired | shen daghlili khar | shen dah-GHLEE-lee KHAR
ex: he is a doctor | is ek'imi aris | ees eh-KEE-mee AH-ris
ex: we are happy | chven bednierebi vart | chven beh-dnee-EH-reh-bee VART
ex: they are at home | isini sakhlshi arian | ee-SEE-nee SAKH-lshee ah-ree-AHN
## Makvs: have
> Georgian says "to me there is": makvs (I have), gaqvs (you have), akvs (he or she has).
row: I have | makvs
row: you have | gaqvs
row: he / she has | akvs
row: we have | gvakvs
row: you have (formal) | gaqvt
row: I do not have | ar makvs
ex: I have an older brother | makvs didi dzma | MAHKVS DEE-dee DZMAH
ex: I have a car | makvs mankana | MAHKVS man-KAH-nah
ex: I do not have time | ar makvs dro | ar MAHKVS DRO
ex: there is a problem | aris p'roblema | AH-ris pro-BLEH-mah
ex: there is no water | ts'qali ar aris | TSKAH-lee ar AH-ris
## Present tense
> The verb carries a prefix for who does it: v- (I), nothing (you), -s (he), v-...-t (we). Cham (to eat): vch'am, ch'am, ch'ams, vch'amt, ch'amt, ch'amen.
row: I eat | vch'am
row: you eat | ch'am
row: he / she eats | ch'ams
row: we eat | vch'amt
row: you eat (formal) | ch'amt
row: they eat | ch'amen
ex: I am eating bread | p'uri vch'am | POO-ree vchahm
ex: you are reading a book | ts'igns k'itkhulob | TSEEG-ns KEET-khoo-lob
ex: she works in a bank | bankshi mushaobs | BAHN-kshee moo-SHAH-obs
ex: we drink tea | chai vsmt | CHAH-ee vsmt
ex: they are sleeping | isini dzinaven | ee-SEE-nee DZEE-nah-ven
## Basic sentences
> The verb often goes last, but the order is flexible. Georgian has no articles: "ts'igni" means "a book" or "the book".
row: I read a book | ts'igns k'itkhulob
row: she drinks tea | is chais svams
row: we go to school | chven sk'olashi vdivart
ex: I read a book | ts'igns k'itkhulob | TSEEG-ns KEET-khoo-lob
ex: he drinks tea | is chais svams | ees CHAH-ees SVAHMS
ex: we go to school | chven sk'olashi vdivart | chven SKO-lah-shee vdee-VART
ex: they eat bread | isini p'urs ch'amen | ee-SEE-nee POORS ch-AH-men
ex: the teacher writes | mast'savlebeli ts'ers | mahs-TSAHV-leh-beh-lee TSEHRS
## Negation
> Put "ar" before the verb: vts'er (I write), ar vts'er (I do not write). "Ar aris" means "is not".
row: I go | vdivar
row: I do not go | ar vdivar
row: is | aris
row: is not | ar aris
ex: I want | minda | MEEN-dah
ex: I do not want | ar minda | ar MEEN-dah
ex: I speak English | inglisurad vlaparak'ob | een-glee-SOO-rad vlah-pah-RAH-kob
ex: I do not speak English | inglisurad ar vlaparak'ob | een-glee-SOO-rad ar vlah-pah-RAH-kob
ex: I like it | miqvars | mee-KVARS
ex: I do not like it | ar miqvars | ar mee-KVARS
ex: she is here | is ak aris | ees AK AH-ris
ex: she is not here | is ak ar aris | ees AK ar AH-ris
ex: I have a car | makvs mankana | MAHKVS man-KAH-nah
ex: I do not have a car | ar makvs mankana | ar MAHKVS man-KAH-nah
ex: I understand | mesmis | MEHS-mees
ex: I do not understand | ar mesmis | ar MEHS-mees
ex: we are tired | chven daghlilebi vart | chven dah-GHLEE-leh-bee VART
ex: we are not tired | chven daghlilebi ar vart | chven dah-GHLEE-leh-bee ar VART
ex: he works on Sundays | is k'viraobit mushaobs | ees kvee-RAH-oh-beet moo-SHAH-obs
ex: he does not work on Sundays | is k'viraobit ar mushaobs | ees kvee-RAH-oh-beet ar moo-SHAH-obs
## Asking questions
> A yes or no question uses the same words, with a rising voice. You can also add the little word "tu" to soften it.
row: you are a student | shen st'udent'i khar
row: are you a student? | shen st'udent'i khar?
row: he is here | is ak aris
row: is he here? | is ak aris?
ex: is this your book? | es sheni ts'igni aris? | ES SHEH-nee TSEEG-nee ah-RIS
ex: are they here? | isini ak arian? | ee-SEE-nee AK ah-ree-AHN
ex: do you like tea? | chai gikvars? | CHAH-ee GEE-kvars
ex: is it cold? | tsivaa? | TSEE-vah-ah
ex: do you speak English? | inglisuri itsit? | een-glee-SOO-ree EE-tsit

row: what | ra
row: who | vin
row: where | sad

row: why | ratom
row: how | rogor
row: how much | ramdeni
row: which | romeli
ex: what is your name? | ra gkvia? | rah GKVEE-ah
ex: who is he? | vin aris is? | VEEN ah-ris EES
ex: where do you live? | sad tskhovrob? | SAD tskho-VROB

ex: how much is it? | ramdeni ghirs? | RAHM-deh-nee GHEERS
ex: which book? | romeli ts'igni? | ro-MEH-lee TSEEG-nee
## Possessives and liking
> Put the word before the noun: chemi (my), sheni (your), misi (his / her), chveni (our), tkveni (your, formal), mati (their). "Miqvars" means "I love" or "I like very much".
row: my book | chemi ts'igni
row: your book | sheni ts'igni
row: his / her book | misi ts'igni
row: our book | chveni ts'igni
row: your book (formal) | tkveni ts'igni
ex: my phone | chemi telepon'i | CHEH-mee teh-leh-PO-nee
ex: your house (formal) | tkveni sakhli | TKVEH-nee SAKH-lee
ex: his sister | misi da | MEE-see DAH
ex: I like music | musik'a miqvars | moo-SEE-kah mee-KVARS
ex: I love football | pekhburti miqvars | pekh-BOOR-tee mee-KVARS
## Formal vs informal
> shen (informal): friends, family, children. tkven (formal): strangers, older people, work. Add -t to the verb for formal: khar / khart.
row: how are you? | rogor khar? / rogor khart?
row: you speak | laparak'ob / laparak'obt
row: your name | sheni sakheli / tkveni sakheli
ex: how are you? (informal) | rogor khar? | RO-gor KHAR
ex: how are you? (formal) | rogor khart? | RO-gor KHART
ex: where are you going? (informal) | sad midikhar? | SAD mee-DEE-khar
ex: where are you going? (formal) | sad midikhart? | SAD mee-DEE-khart
ex: please sit down (formal) | dajdit, gtkhovt | DAH-jeet, GTKHOVT
## Past and future (simple)
> The past and the future change the verb's stem and its ending. Learn each common verb as a pair: ts'avedi (I went), ts'avalo (I will go).
row: I went | ts'avedi
row: you went | ts'axvedi
row: he went | ts'avida
row: we went | ts'avedit
ex: yesterday I was tired | gushin daghlili viq'avi | GOO-sheen dah-GHLEE-lee vee-KAH-vee
ex: I will study tomorrow | khval vists'avli | KHVAL vee-STSAHV-lee
ex: we went to the park | p'ark'shi ts'avedit | PAR-kshee tsah-veh-DEET
ex: I saw him | vnakhe is | VNAH-kheh EES
ex: she wrote a letter | is ts'erili dats'era | ees TSEH-ree-lee dah-TSEH-rah

row: come (informal) | modi
row: come (formal) | modit

row: sit down | dajdi

ex: sit down | dajdi | DAHJ-dee
ex: do not sit down | nu dajdebi | noo dahj-DEH-bee
ex: be quiet | chumad iq'avi | choo-MAD ee-KAH-vee
ex: do not be late | nu daghvianebi | noo dah-ghvee-AH-neh-bee
ex: open the door | gaghe k'ari | GAH-gheh KAH-ree
ex: close the window | daket'e panjara | dah-KEH-teh PAN-jah-rah

## Can, must, want
> Can: shemidzlia + noun form (shemidzlia tsurva). Must: unda + verb. Want: minda + verb.
row: I can swim | shemidzlia tsurva
row: I cannot swim | ar shemidzlia tsurva
row: I must go | unda ts'avide
row: I want to go | minda ts'avide
ex: I can swim | shemidzlia tsurva | sheh-MEE-dzlee-ah TSOOR-vah
ex: I cannot swim | ar shemidzlia tsurva | ar sheh-MEE-dzlee-ah TSOOR-vah
ex: can you help me? | shegidzliat damekhmarot? | sheh-gee-DZLEE-aht dah-meh-khmah-ROT
ex: we must go | unda ts'avidet | OON-dah tsah-vee-DET
ex: we must not go | ts'asvla ar sheidzleba | tsah-SVLAH ar sheh-ee-DZLEH-bah
ex: I want to eat | minda vch'amo | MEEN-dah vch-AH-mo
ex: I do not want to eat | ar minda vch'amo | ar MEEN-dah vch-AH-mo
ex: you can come | sheidzleba mokhvidet | sheh-ee-DZLEH-bah mo-khvee-DET
ex: you cannot come | ar sheidzleba mokhvidet | ar sheh-ee-DZLEH-bah mo-khvee-DET
ex: I must work today | dghes unda vimushao | DGHES OON-dah vee-moo-SHAH-o
ex: she wants to study | mas surs ists'avlos | mahs SOORS ee-STSAHV-los
ex: you must not run | sirbili ar sheidzleba | seer-BEE-lee ar sheh-ee-DZLEH-bah
## In, on, at
> Georgian uses endings, not words in front: -shi (in), -ze (on), -tan (at, near), -dan (from), -s (to).
row: in | -shi
row: on | -ze
row: at, near | -tan
row: from | -dan
ex: the book is on the table | ts'igni magidazea | TSEEG-nee mah-GEE-dah-zeh-ah
ex: the cat is under the table | k'at'a magidis kvesh aris | KAH-tah mah-GEE-dees KVESH ah-ris
ex: I am at school | sk'olashi var | SKO-lah-shee VAR
ex: I am at home | sakhlshi var | SAKH-lshee VAR
ex: the shop is next to the bank | maghazia bankis gverdzea | mah-ghah-ZEE-ah BAHN-kees gvehr-DZEH-ah
ex: the park is near the station | p'ark'i sadgurtan akhlosaa | PAR-kee sad-goor-TAN akh-LO-sah
ex: we meet in the morning | dilit shevkhvdebit | DEE-leet sheh-VKHVDEH-beet
ex: the lesson is at nine | gakvetili tskhra saatze | gah-kveh-TEE-lee TSKHRAH sah-AHT-zeh
ex: the key is in the bag | gasaghebi chantashi aris | gah-sah-GHEH-bee CHAN-tah-shee ah-ris
## Comparing things
> Put "ufro" before the adjective for "more": ufro didi. For "the most" use "q'velaze": q'velaze didi. "Than" is -ze.
row: big, bigger, the biggest | didi, ufro didi, q'velaze didi
row: small, smaller, the smallest | p'at'ara, ufro p'at'ara, q'velaze p'at'ara
ex: big, bigger, the biggest | didi, ufro didi, q'velaze didi | DEE-dee OO-fro DEE-dee KVEH-lah-zeh DEE-dee
ex: small, smaller, the smallest | p'at'ara, ufro p'at'ara, q'velaze p'at'ara | PAH-tah-rah OO-fro PAH-tah-rah KVEH-lah-zeh PAH-tah-rah
ex: this is better than that | es imaze k'argia | ES ee-MAH-zeh KAR-gee-ah
ex: this is worse than that | es imaze tsudia | ES ee-MAH-zeh TSOO-dee-ah
ex: she is taller than me | is chemze ufro maghalia | ees CHEM-zeh OO-fro mah-GHAH-lee-ah
ex: Tashkent is bigger than Samarkand | t'ashk'ent'i samarqandze ufro didia | tash-KEN-tee sah-mar-kahn-DZEH OO-fro DEE-dee-ah
ex: it is the cheapest | es q'velaze iapia | ES KVEH-lah-zeh EE-ah-pee-ah
ex: the same as | ise rogorts | EE-seh RO-gorts
