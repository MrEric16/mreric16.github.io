[grammar]
## Pronouns
> Hawaiian has no he / she difference: "ia" means both. It has a dual "we" (two people) and a plural "we" (three or more), and each comes in two kinds: "kāua / kākou" includes the listener, "māua / mākou" excludes the listener.
row: I | au | OW
row: you (one) | ʻoe | OH-eh
row: he / she | ia | EE-ah
row: we (with you) | kākou | KAH-koh
row: we (without you) | mākou | MAH-koh
row: you (plural) | ʻoukou | oh-OH-koh
row: they | lākou | LAH-koh
ex: I am a student | he haumāna au | heh how-MAH-nah OW
ex: you are tall | kiʻekiʻe ʻoe | kee-eh-kee-EH OH-eh
ex: she is my older sister | ʻo ia koʻu kaikuaʻana wahine | OH EE-ah KOH-oo kie-koo-ah-AH-nah wah-HEE-neh
ex: we are friends | he mau hoaaloha kākou | heh mow hoh-ah-ah-LOH-hah KAH-koh
ex: they are teachers | he mau kumu lākou | heh mow KOO-moo LAH-koh
## Singular and plural
> Nouns do not change. Use "ka" or "ke" (the) for one, and "nā" (the) for many: ka puke (the book), nā puke (the books). Use "he mau" before a noun for "some": he mau puke.
row: the book | ka puke | kah POO-keh
row: the books | nā puke | nah POO-keh
row: the child | ke keiki | keh KEH-ee-kee
row: the children | nā keiki | nah KEH-ee-kee
ex: one house, houses | hoʻokahi hale, nā hale | hoh-oh-KAH-hee HAH-leh, nah HAH-leh
ex: one book, books | hoʻokahi puke, nā puke | hoh-oh-KAH-hee POO-keh, nah POO-keh
ex: one cat, cats | hoʻokahi pōpoki, nā pōpoki | hoh-oh-KAH-hee poh-POH-kee, nah poh-POH-kee
ex: two books | ʻelua puke | eh-LOO-ah POO-keh
ex: the children | nā keiki | nah KEH-ee-kee
## To be: he, ʻo, aia
> Hawaiian has no verb "to be". Use "he" before a noun (he kumu au, a teacher I), "ʻo" for a name or identity, and "aia" for places (aia au ma ka hale). An adjective can stand first.
row: I am a teacher | he kumu au | heh KOO-moo OW
row: this is a book | he puke kēia | heh POO-keh KEH-ee-ah
row: I am at home | aia au ma ka hale | AH-ee-ah OW mah kah HAH-leh
row: I am tall | kiʻekiʻe au | kee-eh-kee-EH OW
ex: I am a student | he haumāna au | heh how-MAH-nah OW
ex: you are tired | luhi ʻoe | LOO-hee OH-eh
ex: he is a doctor | he kauka ʻo ia | heh KOW-kah OH EE-ah
ex: we are happy | hauʻoli kākou | how-OH-lee KAH-koh
ex: they are at home | aia lākou ma ka hale | AH-ee-ah LAH-koh mah kah HAH-leh
## Having: he ... koʻu
> To say "I have", Hawaiian says "a ... mine": he kaʻa koʻu (a car mine). Use "koʻu" for things you own that you do not control (o-class) and "kaʻu" for things you can give away or do (a-class).
row: I have a car | he kaʻa koʻu | heh KAH-ah KOH-oo
row: you have a car | he kaʻa kou | heh KAH-ah KOH
row: he / she has a car | he kaʻa kona | heh KAH-ah KOH-nah
row: I do not have | ʻaʻohe ... oʻu | ah-OH-heh ... OH-oo
ex: I have an older brother | he kaikuaʻana kāne koʻu | heh kie-koo-ah-AH-nah KAH-neh KOH-oo
ex: I have a car | he kaʻa koʻu | heh KAH-ah KOH-oo
ex: I do not have time | ʻaʻohe manawa oʻu | ah-OH-heh mah-NAH-vah OH-oo
ex: there is a problem | aia kekahi pilikia | AH-ee-ah keh-KAH-hee pee-LEE-kee-ah
ex: there is no water | ʻaʻohe wai | ah-OH-heh VAH-ee
## Present tense
> Verbs do not change for person. Put "ke" before the verb and "nei" after it for now: ke ʻai nei au (I am eating). Without "ke ... nei", it is simply "I eat".
row: I am eating | ke ʻai nei au | keh AH-ee neh-EE OW
row: you are eating | ke ʻai nei ʻoe | keh AH-ee neh-EE OH-eh
row: he / she is eating | ke ʻai nei ʻo ia | keh AH-ee neh-EE OH EE-ah
row: we are eating | ke ʻai nei kākou | keh AH-ee neh-EE KAH-koh
ex: I am eating bread | ke ʻai nei au i ka berena | keh AH-ee neh-EE OW ee kah beh-REH-nah
ex: you are reading a book | ke heluhelu nei ʻoe i ka puke | keh heh-loo-HEH-loo neh-EE OH-eh ee kah POO-keh
ex: she works in a bank | ke hana nei ʻo ia ma ka panakō | keh HAH-nah neh-EE OH EE-ah mah kah pah-nah-KOH
ex: we drink tea | ke inu nei mākou i ke kī | keh EE-noo neh-EE MAH-koh ee keh KEE
ex: they are sleeping | ke moe nei lākou | keh MOH-eh neh-EE LAH-koh
## Basic sentences
> The verb comes first. The order is verb, subject, object: "heluhelu au i ka puke" (read I the book). "I" marks the object.
row: I read a book | heluhelu au i ka puke | heh-loo-HEH-loo OW ee kah POO-keh
row: he drinks tea | inu ʻo ia i ke kī | EE-noo OH EE-ah ee keh KEE
row: a big house | he hale nui | heh HAH-leh NOO-ee
row: a new car | he kaʻa hou | heh KAH-ah HOH
ex: I read a book | heluhelu au i ka puke | heh-loo-HEH-loo OW ee kah POO-keh
ex: he drinks tea | inu ʻo ia i ke kī | EE-noo OH EE-ah ee keh KEE
ex: we go to school | hele mākou i ke kula | HEH-leh MAH-koh ee keh KOO-lah
ex: they eat bread | ʻai lākou i ka berena | AH-ee LAH-koh ee kah beh-REH-nah
ex: the teacher writes | kākau ke kumu | kah-KOW keh KOO-moo
## Negation
> Put "ʻaʻole" before the verb or sentence: ʻaʻole au makemake (I do not want). For "there is none", use "ʻaʻohe".
row: I do not go | ʻaʻole au hele | ah-OH-leh OW HEH-leh
row: you do not go | ʻaʻole ʻoe hele | ah-OH-leh OH-eh HEH-leh
row: he / she does not go | ʻaʻole ʻo ia hele | ah-OH-leh OH EE-ah HEH-leh
row: it is not good | ʻaʻole maikaʻi | ah-OH-leh my-KAH-ee
ex: I want | makemake au | mah-keh-MAH-keh OW
ex: I do not want | ʻaʻole au makemake | ah-OH-leh OW mah-keh-MAH-keh
ex: I speak English | ʻōlelo Pelekānia au | oh-LEH-loh peh-leh-KAH-nee-ah OW
ex: I do not speak English | ʻaʻole au ʻōlelo Pelekānia | ah-OH-leh OW oh-LEH-loh peh-leh-KAH-nee-ah
ex: I like it | makemake au iā ia | mah-keh-MAH-keh OW ee-AH EE-ah
ex: I do not like it | ʻaʻole au makemake iā ia | ah-OH-leh OW mah-keh-MAH-keh ee-AH EE-ah
ex: she is here | aia ʻo ia ma ʻaneʻi | AH-ee-ah OH EE-ah mah ah-neh-EE
ex: she is not here | ʻaʻole ʻo ia ma ʻaneʻi | ah-OH-leh OH EE-ah mah ah-neh-EE
ex: I have a car | he kaʻa koʻu | heh KAH-ah KOH-oo
ex: I do not have a car | ʻaʻohe kaʻa oʻu | ah-OH-heh KAH-ah OH-oo
ex: I understand | maopopo iaʻu | mah-oh-POH-poh ee-AH-oo
ex: I do not understand | ʻaʻole maopopo iaʻu | ah-OH-leh mah-oh-POH-poh ee-AH-oo
ex: we are tired | luhi kākou | LOO-hee KAH-koh
ex: we are not tired | ʻaʻole kākou luhi | ah-OH-leh KAH-koh LOO-hee
ex: he works on Sundays | hana ʻo ia i ka Lāpule | HAH-nah OH EE-ah ee kah lah-POO-leh
ex: he does not work on Sundays | ʻaʻole ʻo ia hana i ka Lāpule | ah-OH-leh OH EE-ah HAH-nah ee kah lah-POO-leh
## Asking questions
> Raise your voice at the end, or add "anei" after the first word: "Makemake anei ʻoe?" (Do you want?). To answer, repeat the verb or say "ʻae" (yes) or "ʻaʻole" (no).
row: Do you go? | hele anei ʻoe? | HEH-leh ah-NEH-ee OH-eh
row: Is it good? | maikaʻi anei? | my-KAH-ee ah-NEH-ee
row: Is it right? | pololei anei? | poh-loh-LEH-ee ah-NEH-ee
ex: is this your book? | ʻo kāu puke kēia? | OH KAH-oo POO-keh KEH-ee-ah
ex: are they here? | aia lākou ma ʻaneʻi? | AH-ee-ah LAH-koh mah ah-neh-EE
ex: do you like tea? | makemake ʻoe i ke kī? | mah-keh-MAH-keh OH-eh ee keh KEE
ex: is it cold? | anuanu anei? | ah-noo-AH-noo ah-NEH-ee
ex: do you speak English? | ʻōlelo Pelekānia ʻoe? | oh-LEH-loh peh-leh-KAH-nee-ah OH-eh

> Question words usually come at the start or in the place of the answer.
row: who | wai | VAH-ee

row: why | no ke aha | noh keh AH-hah
row: how | pehea | peh-HEH-ah
row: how much | ehia | eh-HEE-ah
ex: what is your name? | ʻo wai kou inoa? | OH VAH-ee koh ee-NOH-ah
ex: who is he? | ʻo wai ia? | OH VAH-ee EE-ah
ex: where do you live? | noho ʻoe ma hea? | NOH-hoh OH-eh mah HEH-ah

## Possessives and liking
> There are two kinds of "my": "koʻu" (things you have, can't choose, like family and body) and "kaʻu" (things you choose or do, like a pencil or a plan). Your: kou / kāu. His, her: kona / kāna. Our: ko mākou / kā mākou.
row: my body (o-class) | koʻu kino | KOH-oo KEE-noh
row: my book (a-class) | kaʻu puke | KAH-oo POO-keh
row: your book | kāu puke | KAH-oo POO-keh
row: his / her book | kāna puke | KAH-nah POO-keh
ex: my phone | koʻu kelepona | KOH-oo keh-leh-POH-nah
ex: your house (formal) | ko ʻoukou hale | koh oh-OH-koh HAH-leh
ex: his sister | kona kaikuahine | KOH-nah kie-koo-ah-HEE-neh
ex: I like music | makemake au i ke mele | mah-keh-MAH-keh OW ee keh MEH-leh
ex: I love football | aloha au i ka pōpeku | ah-LOH-hah OW ee kah poh-PEH-koo
## Formal vs informal
> Hawaiian shows respect with "ʻoukou" (you, plural) for one person, and with "e ʻoluʻolu" (please). There is no formal verb form.
row: sir | e ke kāne | eh keh KAH-neh
row: madam | e ka wahine | eh kah wah-HEE-neh
ex: how are you? (informal) | pehea ʻoe? | peh-HEH-ah OH-eh
ex: how are you? (formal) | pehea ʻoukou? | peh-HEH-ah oh-OH-koh
ex: where are you going? (informal) | e hele ana ʻoe i hea? | eh HEH-leh AH-nah OH-eh ee HEH-ah
ex: where are you going? (formal) | e hele ana ʻoukou i hea? | eh HEH-leh AH-nah oh-OH-koh ee HEH-ah
ex: please sit down (formal) | e noho, e ʻoluʻolu | eh NOH-hoh, eh oh-loo-OH-loo
## Past and future (simple)
> Verbs do not change. Past: put "ua" before the verb (ua ʻai au, I ate). Future: put "e" before the verb and "ana" after (e ʻai ana au, I will eat).
row: I ate | ua ʻai au | OO-ah AH-ee OW
row: I will eat | e ʻai ana au | eh AH-ee AH-nah OW
row: I will go tomorrow | e hele ana au ʻapōpō | eh HEH-leh AH-nah OW ah-poh-POH
ex: yesterday I was tired | i nehinei, ua luhi au | ee neh-hee-NEH-ee, OO-ah LOO-hee OW
ex: I will study tomorrow | e aʻo ana au ʻapōpō | eh AH-oh AH-nah OW ah-poh-POH
ex: we went to the park | ua hele mākou i ka pāka | OO-ah HEH-leh MAH-koh ee kah PAH-kah
ex: I saw him | ua ʻike au iā ia | OO-ah EE-keh OW ee-AH EE-ah
ex: she wrote a letter | ua kākau ʻo ia i leka | OO-ah kah-KOW OH EE-ah ee LEH-kah
## Commands
> Put "e" before the verb: e hele mai (come). To forbid, use "mai" before the verb: mai hele (do not go).
row: come! | e hele mai! | eh HEH-leh MY
row: wait! | e kali! | eh KAH-lee
row: do not go | mai hele | my HEH-leh
row: please wait | e kali, e ʻoluʻolu | eh KAH-lee, eh oh-loo-OH-loo
ex: come here | e hele mai ʻaneʻi | eh HEH-leh MY ah-neh-EE
ex: do not come here | mai hele mai ʻaneʻi | my HEH-leh MY ah-neh-EE
ex: sit down | e noho iho | eh NOH-hoh EE-hoh
ex: do not sit down | mai noho | my NOH-hoh
ex: be quiet | e noho mālie | eh NOH-hoh MAH-lee-eh
ex: do not be late | mai lohi | my LOH-hee
ex: open the door | e wehe i ka puka | eh VEH-heh ee kah POO-kah
ex: close the window | e pani i ka puka makani | eh PAH-nee ee kah POO-kah mah-KAH-nee
ex: listen to me | e hoʻolohe mai iaʻu | eh hoh-oh-LOH-heh MY ee-AH-oo
ex: do not worry | mai hopohopo | my hoh-poh-HOH-poh
ex: speak slowly | e ʻōlelo mālie | eh oh-LEH-loh MAH-lee-eh
ex: wait a moment | e kali i kekahi manawa | eh KAH-lee ee keh-KAH-hee mah-NAH-vah
## Can, must, want
> "Hiki" (can), "pono" (must) and "makemake" (want) come first. Hiki iaʻu ke... (it is possible for me to...), pono iaʻu e... (it is needed for me to...).
row: I can swim | hiki iaʻu ke ʻau | HEE-kee ee-AH-oo keh OW
row: I must go | pono iaʻu e hele | POH-noh ee-AH-oo eh HEH-leh
row: I want to go | makemake au e hele | mah-keh-MAH-keh OW eh HEH-leh
row: may I? | hiki iaʻu? | HEE-kee ee-AH-oo
ex: I can swim | hiki iaʻu ke ʻau | HEE-kee ee-AH-oo keh OW
ex: I cannot swim | ʻaʻole hiki iaʻu ke ʻau | ah-OH-leh HEE-kee ee-AH-oo keh OW
ex: can you help me? | hiki iā ʻoe ke kōkua mai iaʻu? | HEE-kee ee-AH OH-eh keh koh-KOO-ah MY ee-AH-oo
ex: we must go | pono kākou e hele | POH-noh KAH-koh eh HEH-leh
ex: we must not go | ʻaʻole pono kākou e hele | ah-OH-leh POH-noh KAH-koh eh HEH-leh
ex: I want to eat | makemake au e ʻai | mah-keh-MAH-keh OW eh AH-ee
ex: I do not want to eat | ʻaʻole au makemake e ʻai | ah-OH-leh OW mah-keh-MAH-keh eh AH-ee
ex: you can come | hiki iā ʻoe ke hele mai | HEE-kee ee-AH OH-eh keh HEH-leh MY
ex: you cannot come | ʻaʻole hiki iā ʻoe ke hele mai | ah-OH-leh HEE-kee ee-AH OH-eh keh HEH-leh MY
ex: she wants to study | makemake ʻo ia e aʻo | mah-keh-MAH-keh OH EE-ah eh AH-oh
ex: I must work today | pono iaʻu e hana i kēia lā | POH-noh ee-AH-oo eh HAH-nah ee KEH-ee-ah LAH
ex: you must not run | ʻaʻole pono ʻoe e holo | ah-OH-leh POH-noh OH-eh eh HOH-loh
## In, on, at
> Hawaiian uses small words: "ma" (at, in), "i" (to), "mai" (from), "ma luna o" (on), "ma lalo o" (under), "ma ka ʻaoʻao o" (next to), "kokoke i" (near).
row: in the house | ma loko o ka hale | mah LOH-koh oh kah HAH-leh
row: on the table | ma luna o ka pākaukau | mah LOO-nah oh kah pah-kow-KOW
row: under the table | ma lalo o ka pākaukau | mah LAH-loh oh kah pah-kow-KOW
row: at school | ma ke kula | mah keh KOO-lah
ex: the book is on the table | aia ka puke ma luna o ka pākaukau | AH-ee-ah kah POO-keh mah LOO-nah oh kah pah-kow-KOW
ex: the cat is under the table | aia ka pōpoki ma lalo o ka pākaukau | AH-ee-ah kah poh-POH-kee mah LAH-loh oh kah pah-kow-KOW
ex: I am at school | aia au ma ke kula | AH-ee-ah OW mah keh KOO-lah
ex: I am at home | aia au ma ka hale | AH-ee-ah OW mah kah HAH-leh
ex: the shop is next to the bank | aia ka hale kūʻai ma ka ʻaoʻao o ka panakō | AH-ee-ah kah HAH-leh koo-AH-ee mah kah ah-OH-ah-oh oh kah pah-nah-KOH
ex: the park is near the station | kokoke ka pāka i ke kahua kaʻaahi | koh-KOH-keh kah PAH-kah ee keh kah-HOO-ah kah-ah-AH-hee
ex: we meet in the morning | hui kākou i ke kakahiaka | HOO-ee KAH-koh ee keh kah-kah-hee-AH-kah

## Comparing things
> Hawaiian says "surpasses": ʻoi aku ka nui o kēia ma mua o kēlā (this surpasses that in size). Or "ʻoi" alone: ʻoi aku ka maikaʻi (is better). For "the most", use "ʻoi loa".
row: big, bigger | nui, ʻoi aku ka nui | NOO-ee, OY AH-koo kah NOO-ee
row: good, better | maikaʻi, ʻoi aku ka maikaʻi | my-KAH-ee, OY AH-koo kah my-KAH-ee
row: the biggest | ʻoi loa ka nui | OY LOH-ah kah NOO-ee
ex: big, bigger, the biggest | nui, ʻoi aku ka nui, ʻoi loa ka nui | NOO-ee, OY AH-koo kah NOO-ee, OY LOH-ah kah NOO-ee
ex: small, smaller, the smallest | liʻiliʻi, ʻoi aku ka liʻiliʻi, ʻoi loa ka liʻiliʻi | lee-ee-LEE-ee, OY AH-koo kah lee-ee-LEE-ee, OY LOH-ah kah lee-ee-LEE-ee
ex: this is better than that | ʻoi aku ka maikaʻi o kēia ma mua o kēlā | OY AH-koo kah my-KAH-ee oh KEH-ee-ah mah MOO-ah oh KEH-lah
ex: this is worse than that | ʻoi aku ka ʻino o kēia ma mua o kēlā | OY AH-koo kah EE-noh oh KEH-ee-ah mah MOO-ah oh KEH-lah
ex: she is taller than me | ʻoi aku ka kiʻekiʻe o ia ma mua oʻu | OY AH-koo kah kee-eh-kee-EH oh EE-ah mah MOO-ah OH-oo
ex: Tashkent is bigger than Samarkand | ʻoi aku ka nui o Tashkent ma mua o Samarkand | OY AH-koo kah NOO-ee oh tahsh-KENT mah MOO-ah oh sa-mar-KAND
ex: it is the cheapest | ʻo ia ka mea kūʻai make loa | OH EE-ah kah MEH-ah koo-AH-ee MAH-keh LOH-ah
ex: the same as | e like me | eh LEE-keh meh
