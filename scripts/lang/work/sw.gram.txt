[grammar]
## Pronouns
> Swahili has no he / she difference: "yeye" means both. The pronoun is also built into the verb, so it is often left out. Learn the verb prefixes: ni- (I), u- (you), a- (he, she), tu- (we), m- (you plural), wa- (they).
row: I | mimi | MEE-mee
row: you | wewe | WEH-weh
row: he / she | yeye | YEH-yeh
row: we | sisi | SEE-see
row: you (plural) | ninyi | NEE-nyee
row: they | wao | WAH-oh
ex: I am a student | mimi ni mwanafunzi | MEE-mee nee mwah-nah-FOON-zee
ex: you are tall | wewe ni mrefu | WEH-weh nee m-REH-foo
ex: she is my older sister | yeye ni dada yangu mkubwa | YEH-yeh nee DAH-dah YAHN-goo m-KOO-bwah
ex: we are friends | sisi ni marafiki | SEE-see nee mah-rah-FEE-kee
ex: they are teachers | wao ni walimu | WAH-oh nee wah-LEE-moo
## Singular and plural
> The plural changes the first part of the noun, according to its class. Person: m- to wa- (mtu, watu). Thing: ki- to vi- (kitabu, vitabu). Many nouns: n- stays the same (nyumba, nyumba). Tree: m- to mi- (mti, miti).
row: person, people | mtu, watu | m-TOO, WAH-too
row: book, books | kitabu, vitabu | kee-TAH-boo, vee-TAH-boo
row: house, houses | nyumba, nyumba | NYOOM-bah, NYOOM-bah
row: tree, trees | mti, miti | m-TEE, MEE-tee
ex: one house, houses | nyumba moja, nyumba nyingi | NYOOM-bah MOH-jah, NYOOM-bah NYEEN-gee
ex: one book, books | kitabu kimoja, vitabu vingi | kee-TAH-boo kee-MOH-jah, vee-TAH-boo VEEN-gee
ex: one cat, cats | paka mmoja, paka wengi | PAH-kah m-MOH-jah, PAH-kah WEN-gee
ex: two books | vitabu viwili | vee-TAH-boo vee-WEE-lee
ex: the children | watoto | wah-TOH-toh
## To be: ni
> "Ni" is "is / am / are" for all persons. In the negative it becomes "si". For places use "-ko" with a prefix: niko (I am at).
row: I am a teacher | mimi ni mwalimu | MEE-mee nee mwah-LEE-moo
row: this is a book | hiki ni kitabu | HEE-kee nee kee-TAH-boo
row: I am at home | niko nyumbani | NEE-koh nyoom-BAH-nee
row: I am tall | mimi ni mrefu | MEE-mee nee m-REH-foo
ex: I am a student | mimi ni mwanafunzi | MEE-mee nee mwah-nah-FOON-zee
ex: you are tired | umechoka | oo-meh-CHOH-kah
ex: he is a doctor | yeye ni daktari | YEH-yeh nee dahk-TAH-ree
ex: we are happy | tuna furaha | TOO-nah foo-RAH-hah
ex: they are at home | wako nyumbani | WAH-koh nyoom-BAH-nee
## Having: -na
> Swahili says "I am with": ni- + na = nina (I have), una (you have), ana (he has), tuna (we have), mna (you have), wana (they have).
row: I have | nina | NEE-nah
row: you have | una | OO-nah
row: he / she has | ana | AH-nah
row: we have | tuna | TOO-nah
row: I do not have | sina | SEE-nah
ex: I have an older brother | nina kaka mkubwa | NEE-nah KAH-kah m-KOO-bwah
ex: I have a car | nina gari | NEE-nah GAH-ree
ex: I do not have time | sina muda | SEE-nah MOO-dah
ex: there is a problem | kuna tatizo | KOO-nah tah-TEE-zoh
ex: there is no water | hakuna maji | hah-KOO-nah MAH-jee
## Present tense
> Verb = who + tense + stem. Present: -na-. Ni-na-soma (I am reading), u-na-soma (you), a-na-soma (he, she), tu-na-soma (we), m-na-soma (you plural), wa-na-soma (they).
row: I read | ninasoma | nee-nah-SOH-mah
row: you read | unasoma | oo-nah-SOH-mah
row: he / she reads | anasoma | ah-nah-SOH-mah
row: we read | tunasoma | too-nah-SOH-mah
ex: I am eating bread | ninakula mkate | nee-nah-KOO-lah m-KAH-teh
ex: you are reading a book | unasoma kitabu | oo-nah-SOH-mah kee-TAH-boo
ex: she works in a bank | anafanya kazi benki | ah-nah-FAHN-yah KAH-zee BEHN-kee
ex: we drink tea | tunakunywa chai | too-nah-KOON-wah CHAH-ee
ex: they are sleeping | wanalala | wah-nah-LAH-lah

## Negation
> Add "ha-" at the front and change the tense marker. Present negative: ha- + prefix + verb ending in -i: sisomi (I do not read), husomi (you do not), hasomi (he does not).

row: you do not go | huendi | hoo-EN-dee
row: he / she does not go | haendi | hah-EN-dee
row: it is not good | si nzuri | see n-ZOO-ree

## Asking questions
> The word order does not change. Raise your voice at the end. Or add "je" at the front, or "au" (or) at the end.
row: Do you go? | unaenda? | oo-nah-EHN-dah
row: Is it good? | ni nzuri? | nee n-ZOO-ree
row: Is it right? | si kweli? | see KWEH-lee
ex: is this your book? | hiki ni kitabu chako? | HEE-kee nee kee-TAH-boo CHAH-koh
ex: are they here? | wako hapa? | WAH-koh HAH-pah
ex: do you like tea? | unapenda chai? | oo-nah-PEHN-dah CHAH-ee
ex: is it cold? | kuna baridi? | KOO-nah bah-REE-dee
ex: do you speak English? | unazungumza Kiingereza? | oo-nah-zoon-GOOM-zah kee-een-geh-REH-zah

## Possessives and liking
> The possessive agrees with the noun class: kitabu changu (my book), vitabu vyangu (my books), nyumba yangu (my house), mtoto wangu (my child). Your: -ako, his / her: -ake, our: -etu.
row: my book | kitabu changu | kee-TAH-boo CHAHN-goo
row: your book | kitabu chako | kee-TAH-boo CHAH-koh
row: his / her book | kitabu chake | kee-TAH-boo CHAH-keh
ex: my phone | simu yangu | SEE-moo YAHN-goo
ex: your house (formal) | nyumba yako | NYOOM-bah YAH-koh
ex: his sister | dada yake | DAH-dah YAH-keh
ex: I like music | ninapenda muziki | nee-nah-PEHN-dah moo-ZEE-kee
ex: I love football | ninapenda mpira wa miguu | nee-nah-PEHN-dah m-PEE-rah wah mee-GOO
## Formal vs informal
> Swahili shows respect with greetings and titles: "shikamoo" to elders, "mzee" for an older man, "mama" for a woman, "bwana" for sir. Use "tafadhali" when you ask for things.
row: respectful greeting to an elder | shikamoo | shee-kah-MOH
row: sir | bwana | BWAH-nah
row: madam | bibi | BEE-bee
ex: how are you? (informal) | hujambo? | hoo-JAHM-boh
ex: how are you? (formal) | habari za asubuhi? | hah-BAH-ree zah ah-soo-BOO-hee
ex: where are you going? (informal) | unaenda wapi? | oo-nah-EHN-dah WAH-pee
ex: where are you going? (formal) | unaelekea wapi, mzee? | oo-nah-eh-leh-KEH-ah WAH-pee, m-ZEH-eh
ex: please sit down (formal) | tafadhali keti | tah-fah-DAH-lee KEH-tee
## Past and future (simple)
> Past: -li-. Future: -ta-. Present perfect: -me-. Ni-li-soma (I read), ni-ta-soma (I will read), ni-me-soma (I have read).

row: I will eat | nitakula | nee-tah-KOO-lah
row: I have eaten | nimekula | nee-meh-KOO-lah
row: I will go tomorrow | nitaenda kesho | nee-tah-EHN-dah KEH-shoh
ex: yesterday I was tired | jana nilikuwa nimechoka | JAH-nah nee-lee-KOO-wah nee-meh-CHOH-kah
ex: I will study tomorrow | nitasoma kesho | nee-tah-SOH-mah KEH-shoh
ex: we went to the park | tulikwenda bustanini | too-lee-KWEN-dah boos-tah-NEE-nee
ex: I saw him | nilimwona | nee-lee-m-WOH-nah
ex: she wrote a letter | aliandika barua | ah-lee-ahn-DEE-kah bah-ROO-ah
## Commands

## Can, must, want
> "Kuweza" (can), "kutaka" (want) and "lazima" (must) go before the main verb. Swahili uses the infinitive: naweza kuogelea (I can swim).

ex: I do not want to eat | sitaki kula | see-TAH-kee KOO-lah

## In, on, at
> Swahili uses "-ni" at the end of a noun for "in / at": nyumbani (at home), shuleni (at school). Or use "juu ya" (on), "chini ya" (under), "karibu na" (near), "kando ya" (next to).
row: at home | nyumbani | nyoom-BAH-nee
row: at school | shuleni | shoo-LEH-nee
row: on the table | juu ya meza | JOO yah MEH-zah
row: under the table | chini ya meza | CHEE-nee yah MEH-zah
ex: the book is on the table | kitabu kiko juu ya meza | kee-TAH-boo KEE-koh JOO yah MEH-zah
ex: the cat is under the table | paka yuko chini ya meza | PAH-kah YOO-koh CHEE-nee yah MEH-zah
ex: I am at school | niko shuleni | NEE-koh shoo-LEH-nee
ex: I am at home | niko nyumbani | NEE-koh nyoom-BAH-nee
ex: the shop is next to the bank | duka liko kando ya benki | DOO-kah LEE-koh KAHN-doh yah BEHN-kee
ex: the park is near the station | bustani iko karibu na kituo | boos-TAH-nee EE-koh kah-REE-boo nah kee-TOH-oh
ex: we meet in the morning | tunakutana asubuhi | too-nah-koo-TAH-nah ah-soo-BOO-hee
ex: the lesson is at nine | somo ni saa tatu | SOH-moh nee SAH-ah TAH-too
ex: the key is in the bag | ufunguo uko mfukoni | oo-foon-GOO-oh OO-koh m-foo-KOH-nee
## Comparing things
> There is no -er ending. Use "zaidi ya" (more than) after the adjective: kubwa zaidi ya (bigger than). For "the most", say "kuliko wote" (than all).
row: big, bigger | kubwa, kubwa zaidi | KOO-bwah, KOO-bwah ZAH-ee-dee
row: good, better | nzuri, nzuri zaidi | n-ZOO-ree, n-ZOO-ree ZAH-ee-dee
row: the biggest | kubwa kuliko wote | KOO-bwah koo-LEE-koh WOH-teh
ex: big, bigger, the biggest | kubwa, kubwa zaidi, kubwa kuliko zote | KOO-bwah, KOO-bwah ZAH-ee-dee, KOO-bwah koo-LEE-koh ZOH-teh
ex: small, smaller, the smallest | ndogo, ndogo zaidi, ndogo kuliko zote | N-doh-goh, N-doh-goh ZAH-ee-dee, N-doh-goh koo-LEE-koh ZOH-teh
ex: this is better than that | hii ni bora kuliko ile | HEE nee BOH-rah koo-LEE-koh EE-leh
ex: this is worse than that | hii ni mbaya kuliko ile | HEE nee M-bah-yah koo-LEE-koh EE-leh
ex: she is taller than me | yeye ni mrefu kuliko mimi | YEH-yeh nee m-REH-foo koo-LEE-koh MEE-mee
ex: Tashkent is bigger than Samarkand | Tashkent ni kubwa kuliko Samarkand | tahsh-KENT nee KOO-bwah koo-LEE-koh sah-mar-KAHND
ex: it is the cheapest | ni nafuu kuliko zote | nee nah-FOO koo-LEE-koh ZOH-teh
ex: the same as | sawa na | SAH-wah nah
