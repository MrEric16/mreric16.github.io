[grammar]
## Pronouns
> Latvian has "tu" (you, informal) and "jūs" (you, formal or plural). Verb forms change for each person. There is no he / she difference in the plural except for gender in the past: "viņi" (they, men) and "viņas" (they, women).
row: I | es | es
row: you (informal) | tu | too
row: he | viņš | veensh
row: she | viņa | VEE-nyah
row: we | mēs | mehs
row: you (formal / plural) | jūs | yoos
row: they (m) | viņi | VEE-nyee
row: they (f) | viņas | VEE-nyas
ex: I am a student | es esmu students | es ES-moo STOO-dents
ex: you are tall | tu esi garš | too EH-see garsh
ex: she is my older sister | viņa ir mana vecākā māsa | VEE-nyah ir MAH-nah VEH-tsah-kah MAH-sah
ex: we are friends | mēs esam draugi | mehs EH-sam DROW-gee
ex: they are teachers | viņi ir skolotāji | VEE-nyee ir SKOH-loh-tah-yee
## Singular and plural
> Latvian has no "the" or "a". Nouns end in -s, -š, -is, -us (masculine) or -a, -e (feminine). Plural masculine: -i (grāmata, grāmatas). Memorise the plural of each noun.
row: the book | grāmata | GRAH-mah-tah
row: the books | grāmatas | GRAH-mah-tas
row: the child | bērns | behrns
row: the children | bērni | BEHR-nee
ex: one house, houses | māja, mājas | MAH-yah, MAH-yas
ex: one book, books | grāmata, grāmatas | GRAH-mah-tah, GRAH-mah-tas
ex: one cat, cats | kaķis, kaķi | KAH-tyis, KAH-tyee
ex: two books | divas grāmatas | DEE-vas GRAH-mah-tas
ex: the children | bērni | BEHR-nee
## To be: būt
> The verb "būt" (to be) changes for each person, and is also used for "there is".
row: I am | es esmu | es ES-moo
row: you are (informal) | tu esi | too EH-see
row: he / she is | viņš / viņa ir | veensh / VEE-nyah ir
row: we are | mēs esam | mehs EH-sam
row: you are (formal / plural) | jūs esat | yoos EH-sat
row: they are | viņi ir | VEE-nyee ir
ex: I am a student | es esmu students | es ES-moo STOO-dents
ex: you are tired | tu esi noguris | too EH-see NOH-goo-ris
ex: he is a doctor | viņš ir ārsts | veensh ir arsts
ex: we are happy | mēs esam laimīgi | mehs EH-sam LIE-mee-gee
ex: they are at home | viņi ir mājās | VEE-nyee ir MAH-yas
## Having: man ir
> Latvian says "to me is": man ir mašīna (I have a car). The thing owned comes after "ir". For "I do not have", use "man nav".
row: I have a car | man ir mašīna | man ir MAH-shee-nah
row: you have a car | tev ir mašīna | tev ir MAH-shee-nah
row: he / she has a car | viņam / viņai ir mašīna | VEE-nyam / VEE-nyai ir MAH-shee-nah
row: I do not have | man nav | man nav
ex: I have an older brother | man ir vecāks brālis | man ir VEH-tsahks BRAH-lis
ex: I have a car | man ir mašīna | man ir MAH-shee-nah
ex: I do not have time | man nav laika | man nav LAI-kah
ex: there is a problem | ir problēma | ir proh-BLEH-mah
ex: there is no water | nav ūdens | nav OO-dens
## Present tense
> Verbs change for person. Regular verbs (like "lasīt", to read) take -u, -i, -a for I, you, he / she: es lasu, tu lasi, viņš lasa.
row: I read | es lasu | es LAH-soo
row: you read | tu lasi | too LAH-see
row: he / she reads | viņš / viņa lasa | veensh / VEE-nyah LAH-sah
row: we read | mēs lasām | mehs LAH-sahm
ex: I am eating bread | es ēdu maizi | es EH-doo MIE-zee
ex: you are reading a book | tu lasi grāmatu | too LAH-see GRAH-mah-too
ex: she works in a bank | viņa strādā bankā | VEE-nyah STRAH-dah BAN-kah
ex: we drink tea | mēs dzeram tēju | mehs DZEH-ram TEH-yoo
ex: they are sleeping | viņi guļ | VEE-nyee goolyh
## Basic sentences
> The usual order is subject, verb, object: "es lasu grāmatu" (I read a book). The object ends change (a "case" ending): grāmata becomes grāmatu.
row: I read a book | es lasu grāmatu | es LAH-soo GRAH-mah-too
row: he drinks tea | viņš dzer tēju | veensh dzer TEH-yoo
row: a big house | liela māja | lee-EH-lah MAH-yah
row: a new car | jauna mašīna | YOW-nah MAH-shee-nah
ex: I read a book | es lasu grāmatu | es LAH-soo GRAH-mah-too
ex: he drinks tea | viņš dzer tēju | veensh dzer TEH-yoo
ex: we go to school | mēs ejam uz skolu | mehs EH-yam ooz SKOH-loo
ex: they eat bread | viņi ēd maizi | VEE-nyee ehd MIE-zee
ex: the teacher writes | skolotājs raksta | SKOH-loh-tahys RAK-stah
## Negation
> Put "ne" before the verb: es nezinu (I do not know). Verbs become "ne" + verb. With "būt", "nav" replaces "ir": man nav (I do not have). Nouns after "nav" take the genitive.
row: I do not go | es neeju | es NEH-ay-oo
row: you do not go | tu neej | too nay
row: he / she does not go | viņš / viņa neiet | veensh / VEE-nyah NAY-et
row: it is not good | tas nav labs | tas nav labs
ex: I want | es gribu | es GREE-boo
ex: I do not want | es negribu | es NEH-gree-boo
ex: I speak English | es runāju angliski | es ROO-nah-yoo ANG-lis-kee
ex: I do not speak English | es nerunāju angliski | es NEH-roo-nah-yoo ANG-lis-kee
ex: I like it | tas man patīk | tas man pah-TEEK
ex: I do not like it | tas man nepatīk | tas man NEH-pah-teek
ex: she is here | viņa ir šeit | VEE-nyah ir shayt
ex: she is not here | viņas šeit nav | VEE-nyas shayt nav
ex: I have a car | man ir mašīna | man ir MAH-shee-nah
ex: I do not have a car | man nav mašīnas | man nav MAH-shee-nas
ex: I understand | es saprotu | es SAH-proh-too
ex: I do not understand | es nesaprotu | es NEH-sah-proh-too
ex: we are tired | mēs esam noguruši | mehs EH-sam NOH-goo-roo-shee
ex: we are not tired | mēs neesam noguruši | mehs NEH-eh-sam NOH-goo-roo-shee
ex: he works on Sundays | viņš strādā svētdienās | veensh STRAH-dah SVEHT-dee-eh-nahs
ex: he does not work on Sundays | viņš nestrādā svētdienās | veensh NEH-strah-dah SVEHT-dee-eh-nahs
## Asking questions
> Add "vai" at the start to turn a sentence into a yes / no question: Vai tu esi students? (Are you a student?). Answer "jā" (yes) or "nē" (no).
row: Do you go? | vai tu ej? | vai too ay
row: Is it good? | vai tas ir labi? | vai tas ir LAH-bee
row: Is it right? | vai tas ir pareizi? | vai tas ir pah-RAY-zee
ex: is this your book? | vai šī ir tava grāmata? | vai shee ir TAH-vah GRAH-mah-tah
ex: are they here? | vai viņi ir šeit? | vai VEE-nyee ir shayt
ex: do you like tea? | vai tev patīk tēja? | vai tev pah-TEEK TEH-yah
ex: is it cold? | vai ir auksts? | vai ir owksts
ex: do you speak English? | vai tu runā angliski? | vai too ROO-nah ANG-lis-kee

## Possessives and liking
> Latvian possessives change with the gender and number of the noun: mans (m), mana (f), mani (m pl), manas (f pl). Your: tavs, tava. His, her: viņa, viņas (no change). "Patikt" means "to please": man patīk tēja (tea pleases me).
row: my book | mana grāmata | MAH-nah GRAH-mah-tah
row: my father | mans tēvs | mans tehvs
row: your book | tava grāmata | TAH-vah GRAH-mah-tah
row: his / her book | viņa grāmata | VEE-nyah GRAH-mah-tah
ex: my phone | mans telefons | mans TEH-leh-fons
ex: your house (formal) | jūsu māja | YOO-soo MAH-yah
ex: his sister | viņa māsa | VEE-nyah MAH-sah
ex: I like music | man patīk mūzika | man pah-TEEK MOO-zee-kah
ex: I love football | es mīlu futbolu | es MEE-loo FOOT-boh-loo
## Formal vs informal
> Use "jūs" (and "jūs esat") for one older person, a stranger or in a formal setting. Use "tu" with friends, family and children.
row: sir | kungs | koongs
row: madam | kundze | KOON-dzeh
ex: how are you? (informal) | kā tev iet? | kah tev ee-ET
ex: how are you? (formal) | kā jums iet? | kah yooms ee-ET
ex: where are you going? (informal) | kurp tu ej? | koorp too ay
ex: where are you going? (formal) | kurp jūs ejat? | koorp yoos EH-yat
ex: please sit down (formal) | lūdzu, apsēdieties | LOO-dzoo, AP-seh-dee-eh-tee-es
## Past and future (simple)
> Past: past forms of verbs (es lasīju, I read). Future: end in -šu (es lasīšu, I will read). A form changes by gender in the past of "būt": es biju, tu biji, viņš bija.
row: I ate | es ēdu | es EH-doo
row: I will eat | es ēdīšu | es EH-dee-shoo
row: I will go tomorrow | es iešu rīt | es EE-eh-shoo reet
ex: yesterday I was tired | vakar es biju noguris | VAH-kar es BEE-yoo NOH-goo-ris
ex: I will study tomorrow | rīt es mācīšos | reet es MAH-tsee-shos
ex: we went to the park | mēs gājām uz parku | mehs GAH-yahm ooz PAR-koo
ex: I saw him | es viņu redzēju | es VEE-nyoo REH-dzeh-yoo
ex: she wrote a letter | viņa uzrakstīja vēstuli | VEE-nyah OOZ-rak-stee-yah VEHS-too-lee

## Can, must, want
> "Varēt" (can), "vajadzēt" or "drīkstēt" (must / may) and "gribēt" (want) change for person. They are followed by the "to" form of the verb: es varu peldēt (I can swim).

row: I must go | man jāiet | man YAH-ee-et
row: I want to go | es gribu iet | es GREE-boo ee-et
row: may I? | vai drīkstu? | vai DREEK-stoo
ex: I can swim | es varu peldēt | es VAH-roo PEL-det
ex: I cannot swim | es nevaru peldēt | es NEH-vah-roo PEL-det
ex: can you help me? | vai tu vari man palīdzēt? | vai too VAH-ree man pah-LEED-zet
ex: we must go | mums jāiet | moomz YAH-ee-et
ex: we must not go | mums nav jāiet | moomz nav YAH-ee-et
ex: I want to eat | es gribu ēst | es GREE-boo ehst
ex: I do not want to eat | es negribu ēst | es NEH-gree-boo ehst
ex: you can come | tu vari nākt | too VAH-ree nahkt
ex: you cannot come | tu nevari nākt | too NEH-vah-ree nahkt

## In, on, at

row: under the table | zem galda | zem GAL-dah
row: at school | skolā | SKOH-lah

## Comparing things
> Add "-āks" to an adjective for "more": liels, lielāks (big, bigger). For "the most", use "visvairāk" or "vislielākais". "Nekā" means "than".
row: big, bigger | liels, lielāks | lee-ELS, lee-EH-lahks
row: good, better | labs, labāks | labs, LAH-bahks
row: the biggest | vislielākais | vis-lee-EH-lah-kice
ex: big, bigger, the biggest | liels, lielāks, vislielākais | lee-ELS, lee-EH-lahks, vis-lee-EH-lah-kice
ex: small, smaller, the smallest | mazs, mazāks, vismazākais | mazs, MAH-zahks, vis-MAH-zah-kice
ex: this is better than that | šis ir labāks nekā tas | shis ir LAH-bahks NEH-kah tas
ex: this is worse than that | šis ir sliktāks nekā tas | shis ir SLIK-tahks NEH-kah tas
ex: she is taller than me | viņa ir garāka par mani | VEE-nyah ir GAH-rah-kah par MAH-nee
ex: Tashkent is bigger than Samarkand | Taškenta ir lielāka nekā Samarkanda | TASH-ken-tah ir lee-EH-lah-kah NEH-kah SAH-mar-kan-dah
ex: it is the cheapest | tas ir lētākais | tas ir LEH-tah-kice
