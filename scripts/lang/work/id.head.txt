INTRO: Indonesian is spelled almost exactly as it sounds, with no tones, no genders and no verb endings. Stress usually falls on the second-last syllable. Words are made longer by adding prefixes and suffixes, not by changing the verb for the person or the time.
c | like "ch" in "chair" | cinta
j | like "j" in "jam" | jalan
y | like "y" in "yes" | ya
r | rolled, as in Spanish | rumah
e | usually a short "uh" (like "a" in "about"), sometimes like "e" in "bed" | enak
ny | like "ny" in "canyon" | nyanyi
ng | like "ng" in "singer" | pinggir
ngg | like "ng-g" in "finger" | tinggi
kh | a rough "h" or "kh", like Scottish "loch" | khusus
NOTE: Indonesian has a formal "Anda" for you and a friendly "kamu". In speech, people often use "Bapak" (sir) and "Ibu" (madam) in place of "you" to show respect.
