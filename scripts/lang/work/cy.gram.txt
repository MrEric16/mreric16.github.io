## Pronouns
> Welsh has no he / she difference for verbs, but the pronoun does: "e" or "fe" (he) and "hi" (she). The little form "ti" is for friends, "chi" for strangers, elders and groups.
row: I | fi / i | vee / ee
row: you (informal) | ti | tee
row: he | e / fe | eh / veh
row: she | hi | hee
row: we | ni | nee
row: you (formal or plural) | chi | chee
row: they | nhw | nhoo
ex: I am a student | dw i'n fyfyriwr | doo een vuh-VUH-ri-oor
ex: you are tall | rwyt ti'n dal | ROO-it teen dal
ex: she is my older sister | hi yw fy chwaer hŷn | hee yoo vuh chwire heen
ex: we are friends | ffrindiau ydyn ni | FRIND-yeye UH-din nee
ex: they are teachers | athrawon ydyn nhw | ath-ROW-on UH-din nhoo
## Singular and plural
> Many nouns add -au or -on: ci, cŵn (irregular), llyfr, llyfrau. After a number, the noun stays singular: dau lyfr (two book).
row: book | llyfr | hluvr
row: books | llyfrau | HLUV-reye
row: house | tŷ | tee
row: houses | tai | tie
ex: one house, houses | un tŷ, tai | een tee, tie
ex: one book, books | un llyfr, llyfrau | een hluvr, HLUV-reye
ex: one cat, cats | un gath, cathod | een gath, KATH-od
ex: two books | dau lyfr | dai hluvr
ex: the children | y plant | uh plant
## To be: bod
> Welsh uses "bod" (to be). Present: dw i (I am), rwyt ti (you are), mae e / hi (he, she is), dyn ni (we are), dych chi (you are), maen nhw (they are). Before a noun, use "yn" or the word order of "ydy".
row: I am | dw i | doo ee
row: you are | rwyt ti | ROO-it tee
row: he / she is | mae e / mae hi | mine eh / mine hee
row: we are | dyn ni | din nee
row: you are (formal) | dych chi | dikh chee
row: they are | maen nhw | mine nhoo
ex: I am a student | dw i'n fyfyriwr | doo een vuh-VUH-ri-oor
ex: you are tired | rwyt ti wedi blino | ROO-it tee WED-ee BLIN-oh
ex: he is a doctor | meddyg yw e | METH-ig yoo eh
ex: we are happy | dyn ni'n hapus | din neen HAP-is
ex: they are at home | maen nhw gartref | mine nhoo GAR-trev
## Having: gan, gyda
> Welsh says "with me": mae gen i (I have), mae gen ti (you have), mae ganddo fe (he has), mae ganddi hi (she has), mae gynnon ni (we have), mae gynnoch chi (you have), mae ganddyn nhw (they have).
row: I have | mae gen i | mine gen ee
row: you have | mae gen ti | mine gen tee
row: he has | mae ganddo fe | mine GAN-tho veh
row: she has | mae ganddi hi | mine GAN-thee hee
row: I do not have | does dim ... gen i | doys dim ... gen ee
ex: I have an older brother | mae gen i frawd hŷn | mine gen ee vrowd heen
ex: I have a car | mae gen i gar | mine gen ee gar
ex: I do not have time | does dim amser gen i | doys dim AM-ser gen ee
ex: there is a problem | mae problem | mine PROB-lem
ex: there is no water | does dim dŵr | doys dim door
## Present tense
> Use "bod" + "yn" + the verb-noun. "Dw i'n bwyta" is I am eating (or I eat). The "yn" often joins the word before it: dw i'n, rwyt ti'n, mae e'n.
row: I eat | dw i'n bwyta | doo een BOOIT-a
row: you eat | rwyt ti'n bwyta | ROO-it teen BOOIT-a
row: he / she eats | mae e'n bwyta | mine eh'n BOOIT-a
row: we eat | dyn ni'n bwyta | din neen BOOIT-a
ex: I am eating bread | dw i'n bwyta bara | doo een BOOIT-a BAR-ah
ex: you are reading a book | rwyt ti'n darllen llyfr | ROO-it teen DAR-hlen hluvr
ex: she works in a bank | mae hi'n gweithio mewn banc | mine heen GOOITH-yo mehwn bank
ex: we drink tea | dyn ni'n yfed te | din neen UV-ed teh
ex: they are sleeping | maen nhw'n cysgu | mine nhoon KUS-gee
## Basic sentences
> The verb comes first. Welsh order is verb, subject, object. The adjective follows the noun.
row: I read a book | dw i'n darllen llyfr | doo een DAR-hlen hluvr
row: he drinks tea | mae e'n yfed te | mine eh'n UV-ed teh
row: a big house | tŷ mawr | tee mowr
row: a new car | car newydd | kar NEW-ith
ex: I read a book | dw i'n darllen llyfr | doo een DAR-hlen hluvr
ex: he drinks tea | mae e'n yfed te | mine eh'n UV-ed teh
ex: we go to school | dyn ni'n mynd i'r ysgol | din neen meend eer US-gol
ex: they eat bread | maen nhw'n bwyta bara | mine nhoon BOOIT-a BAR-ah
ex: the teacher writes | mae'r athro'n ysgrifennu | mire ATH-ron us-grif-EN-ee
## Negation
> In the present, "dim" follows the subject: dw i ddim yn gweld (I am not seeing). The "dd" is a mutation of "dim". In the past, use "ches i ddim" (I did not get).
row: I do not go | dw i ddim yn mynd | doo ee thim un meend
row: you do not go | dwyt ti ddim yn mynd | DOO-it tee thim un meend
row: he / she does not go | dyw e ddim yn mynd | dew eh thim un meend
row: it is not good | dyw e ddim yn dda | dew eh thim un thah
ex: I want | dw i eisiau | doo ee ICE-ya
ex: I do not want | dw i ddim eisiau | doo ee thim ICE-ya
ex: I speak English | dw i'n siarad Saesneg | doo een SHAR-ad SIGHS-neg
ex: I do not speak English | dw i ddim yn siarad Saesneg | doo ee thim un SHAR-ad SIGHS-neg
ex: I like it | dw i'n ei hoffi | doo een ay HOF-ee
ex: I do not like it | dw i ddim yn ei hoffi | doo ee thim un ay HOF-ee
ex: she is here | mae hi yma | mine hee UH-ma
ex: she is not here | dyw hi ddim yma | dew hee thim UH-ma
ex: I have a car | mae gen i gar | mine gen ee gar
ex: I do not have a car | does dim car gen i | doys dim kar gen ee
ex: I understand | dw i'n deall | doo een DEH-ahlh
ex: I do not understand | dw i ddim yn deall | doo ee thim un DEH-ahlh
ex: we are tired | dyn ni wedi blino | din nee WED-ee BLIN-oh
ex: we are not tired | dyn ni ddim wedi blino | din nee thim WED-ee BLIN-oh
ex: he works on Sundays | mae e'n gweithio ar ddydd Sul | mine eh'n GOOITH-yo ar theeth seel
ex: he does not work on Sundays | dyw e ddim yn gweithio ar ddydd Sul | dew eh thim un GOOITH-yo ar theeth seel
## Asking questions
> Swap the order: put "wyt" or "ydy" first and add the subject. "Wyt ti'n..." = are you...? A short answer repeats the verb: "Ydw" (I am), "Nac ydw" (I am not).
row: Do you go? | wyt ti'n mynd? | ooit teen meend
row: Is it good? | ydy e'n dda? | UH-dee eh'n thah
row: Is it right? | ydy hynny'n iawn? | UH-dee HUN-ee'n yown
ex: is this your book? | ai dy lyfr di yw hwn? | ie duh hluvr dee yoo hoon
ex: are they here? | ydyn nhw yma? | UH-din nhoo UH-ma
ex: do you like tea? | wyt ti'n hoffi te? | ooit teen HOF-ee teh
ex: is it cold? | ydy hi'n oer? | UH-dee hee'n oyr
ex: do you speak English? | wyt ti'n siarad Saesneg? | ooit teen SHAR-ad SIGHS-neg

row: who | pwy | pooi
row: what | beth | beth
row: where | ble | bleh
row: when | pryd | preed
row: why | pam | pam
row: how | sut | sit
row: how much | faint | vine-t
ex: what is your name? | beth yw dy enw di? | beth yoo duh EN-oo dee
ex: who is he? | pwy yw e? | pooi yoo eh
ex: where do you live? | ble wyt ti'n byw? | bleh ooit teen biw

ex: how much is it? | faint yw e? | vine-t yoo eh
ex: which book? | pa lyfr? | pah hluvr
## Possessives and liking
> The possessive comes before the noun and changes its first letter: fy llyfr i (my book, with a nasal sound in speech), dy lyfr di (your book), ei lyfr e (his book), ei llyfr hi (her book).
row: my book | fy llyfr i | vuh hluvr ee
row: your book | dy lyfr di | duh hluvr dee
row: his book | ei lyfr e | ay hluvr eh
row: her book | ei llyfr hi | ay hluvr hee
ex: my phone | fy ffôn i | vuh fohn ee
ex: your house (formal) | eich tŷ chi | ikh tee chee
ex: his sister | ei chwaer e | ay chwire eh
ex: I like music | dw i'n hoffi cerddoriaeth | doo een HOF-ee ker-thor-YATH
ex: I love football | dw i wrth fy modd â phêl-droed | doo ee oorth vuh moth ah fayl-droyd
## Formal vs informal
> "Ti" is for friends and children. "Chi" is for strangers, older people and groups. The verb ending changes: wyt ti / ydych chi.
row: you (informal) | ti | tee
row: you (formal) | chi | chee
row: excuse me, sir | esgusodwch fi, syr | es-gis-OD-wookh vee, seer
ex: how are you? (informal) | sut wyt ti? | sit ooit tee
ex: how are you? (formal) | sut ydych chi? | sit UH-dikh chee
ex: where are you going? (informal) | i ble wyt ti'n mynd? | ee bleh ooit teen meend
ex: where are you going? (formal) | i ble ydych chi'n mynd? | ee bleh UH-dikh cheen meend
ex: please sit down (formal) | eisteddwch, os gwelwch yn dda | ice-TETH-ookh, os GWEL-ookh un thah
## Past and future (simple)
> Past: add endings to the stem: es i (I went), est ti (you went), aeth e (he went). Future: bydda i + yn + the verb-noun: bydda i'n mynd (I will go).
row: I went | es i | ehs ee
row: I ate | bwyteais i | BOO-it-ay-ees ee
row: I will go | bydda i'n mynd | BUH-tha een meend
row: I will go tomorrow | bydda i'n mynd yfory | BUH-tha een meend uh-VOR-ee
ex: yesterday I was tired | ddoe, roeddwn i wedi blino | thoy, ROYTH-oon ee WED-ee BLIN-oh
ex: I will study tomorrow | bydda i'n astudio yfory | BUH-tha een as-TID-yo uh-VOR-ee
ex: we went to the park | aethon ni i'r parc | IGH-thon nee eer park
ex: I saw him | gwelais i e | GOO-el-ice ee eh
ex: she wrote a letter | ysgrifennodd hi lythyr | us-grif-EN-oth hee HLUTH-eer
## Commands
> Use the stem for "ti": dere (come), arhosa (wait). Add -wch for "chi": dewch (come), arhoswch (wait). To forbid, use "paid" (ti) or "peidiwch" (chi) + "â" + the verb-noun.
row: come! | dere! | DEH-reh
row: come! (formal) | dewch! | DEH-ookh
row: wait! | arhosa! | ar-HOS-a
row: do not go | paid â mynd | pide ah meend
ex: come here | dere yma | DEH-reh UH-ma
ex: do not come here | paid â dod yma | pide ah dohd UH-ma
ex: sit down | eistedd | ICE-teth
ex: do not sit down | paid ag eistedd | pide ag ICE-teth
ex: be quiet | bydd yn dawel | beeth un DAH-wel
ex: do not be late | paid â bod yn hwyr | pide ah bod un hooir
ex: open the door | agor y drws | AG-or uh droos
ex: close the window | cau'r ffenestr | KIGH-r fen-EST-r
ex: listen to me | gwranda arna i | GWRAN-da AR-na ee
ex: do not worry | paid â phoeni | pide ah FOY-nee
ex: speak slowly | siarada'n araf | shar-AD-an AR-av
ex: wait a moment | arhosa funud | ar-HOS-a VIN-id
## Can, must, want
> "Gallu" (can): alla i (I can), alli di (you can). "Mae'n rhaid i" (must): mae'n rhaid i fi fynd. "Eisiau" (want): dw i eisiau mynd.
row: I can swim | alla i nofio | AHLH-a ee NOV-yo
row: I must go | mae'n rhaid i fi fynd | mine rhide ee vee veend
row: I want to go | dw i eisiau mynd | doo ee ICE-ya meend
row: may I? | ga i? | gah ee
ex: I can swim | alla i nofio | AHLH-a ee NOV-yo
ex: I cannot swim | alla i ddim nofio | AHLH-a ee thim NOV-yo
ex: can you help me? | alli di fy helpu i? | AHLH-ee dee vuh HEL-pee ee
ex: we must go | mae'n rhaid i ni fynd | mine rhide ee nee veend
ex: we must not go | does dim hawl gyda ni i fynd | doys dim howl GUH-da nee ee veend
ex: I want to eat | dw i eisiau bwyta | doo ee ICE-ya BOOIT-a
ex: I do not want to eat | dw i ddim eisiau bwyta | doo ee thim ICE-ya BOOIT-a
ex: you can come | gelli di ddod | GEHLH-ee dee thohd
ex: you cannot come | allet ti ddim dod | AHLH-et tee thim dohd
ex: she wants to study | mae hi eisiau astudio | mine hee ICE-ya as-TID-yo
ex: I must work today | mae'n rhaid i fi weithio heddiw | mine rhide ee vee WEETH-yo HETH-yoo
ex: you must not run | does dim hawl gyda ti i redeg | doys dim howl GUH-da tee ee RED-eg
## In, on, at
> Welsh uses short words before the place: "yn" (in), "ar" (on), "wrth" (at, by), "o dan" (under), "wrth ymyl" (next to), "ger" (near), "i" (to), "o" (from). Many make the next word change its first letter.
row: in the house | yn y tŷ | un uh tee
row: on the table | ar y bwrdd | ar uh boorth
row: under the table | o dan y bwrdd | oh dan uh boorth
row: at school | yn yr ysgol | un ur US-gol
ex: the book is on the table | mae'r llyfr ar y bwrdd | mire hluvr ar uh boorth
ex: the cat is under the table | mae'r gath o dan y bwrdd | mire gath oh dan uh boorth
ex: I am at school | dw i yn yr ysgol | doo ee un ur US-gol
ex: I am at home | dw i gartref | doo ee GAR-trev
ex: the shop is next to the bank | mae'r siop wrth ymyl y banc | mire shop oorth UM-il uh bank
ex: the park is near the station | mae'r parc ger yr orsaf | mire park ger ur OR-sav
ex: we meet in the morning | dyn ni'n cwrdd yn y bore | din neen koordh un uh BOR-eh
ex: the lesson is at nine | mae'r wers am naw o'r gloch | mire wers am now or glokh
ex: the key is in the bag | mae'r allwedd yn y bag | mire AHLH-weth un uh bag
## Comparing things
> Add -ach for "more" and -af for "the most": mawr, mwy (irregular), mwyaf. Regular: cyflym, cyflymach, cyflymaf. "Than" is "na". "As ... as" is "mor ... â".
row: big, bigger | mawr, mwy | mowr, mooee
row: good, better | da, gwell | dah, gwehlh
row: small, smaller | bach, llai | bakh, hlie
row: the biggest | y mwyaf | uh MOO-ee-av
ex: big, bigger, the biggest | mawr, mwy, mwyaf | mowr, mooee, MOO-ee-av
ex: small, smaller, the smallest | bach, llai, lleiaf | bakh, hlie, HLAY-av
ex: this is better than that | mae hwn yn well na hwnna | mine hoon un wehlh nah HOON-a
ex: this is worse than that | mae hwn yn waeth na hwnna | mine hoon un wighth nah HOON-a
ex: she is taller than me | mae hi'n dalach na fi | mine heen DAL-akh nah vee
ex: Tashkent is bigger than Samarkand | mae Tashkent yn fwy na Samarkand | mine tash-KENT un vooee nah sa-mar-KAND
ex: it is the cheapest | hwn yw'r rhataf | hoon yoor RHAT-av
ex: the same as | yr un fath â | ur een vath ah
