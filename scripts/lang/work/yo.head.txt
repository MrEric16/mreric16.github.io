INTRO: Yoruba is a tonal language of Nigeria and Benin. It has three tones: high (á), mid (a, unmarked) and low (à). The tone changes the meaning. Dots under letters (ẹ, ọ, ṣ) are separate letters. Words never change for person or time, and nouns have no gender.
ẹ | like "e" in "bed" | ẹja
ọ | like "o" in "more" or "thought" | ọmọ
ṣ | like "sh" in "shop" | ṣé
gb | said as one sound, a "g" and "b" together | gbà
kp | a "k" and "p" said together, like a kiss with a click | kpa
p | said as "kp" in many words, a sharp "p" | pa
an / en / in / on / un | a nasal vowel, with the "n" not said as a consonant | ń
á / à / a | high, low, mid tone on a vowel | bàbá
NOTE: The tones are shown here as in standard writing, but people often drop them when they type quickly. Learn them from a speaker, because a wrong tone can change the word.
NOTE: Yoruba shows respect with the plural "ẹ" for one older person. Use "ẹ" to a parent, an elder or a stranger and "o" to a friend or a child.
