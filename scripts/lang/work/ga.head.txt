INTRO: Irish spelling looks hard but follows rules. A consonant has a "broad" or "slender" sound, depending on the vowels next to it: a, o, u make it broad; e, i make it slender. The accent mark (fada) over a vowel makes it long. The sound system is different in Munster, Connacht and Ulster. The Latin line here follows the standard form with a simple, general-Irish pronunciation.
bh / mh | like "w" or "v" | bhfuil
ch | like "ch" in Scottish "loch" | cheol
dh / gh | a rough sound at the back of the throat, or silent | dhá
fh | silent | fhios
th | like "h" | thú
ph | like "f" | phost
sh | like "h" | sé
ao | like "ee" or "ay" | gaoth
ái | like "aw" in "paw" | cáis
NOTE: Irish has no word for "yes" or "no". You repeat the verb: "An bhfuil tú go maith?" "Tá" (am), or "Níl" (am not).
NOTE: A word's first sound often changes (lenition and eclipsis): bád, an bád, ar an mbád. The first letter after "mo" (my) takes a "h"-like change: mo cheann, mo mháthair.
