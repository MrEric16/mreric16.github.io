INTRO: Māori has five vowels (a, e, i, o, u) and ten consonants (h, k, m, n, ng, p, r, t, w, wh). Every vowel is said, and a bar over a vowel (macron: ā, ē, ī, ō, ū) makes it about twice as long. The macron changes the meaning, so it matters.
a | ah, like "far" | tahi
e | eh, like "bed" | rewera
i | ee, like "see" | rima
o | oh, like "more" | ono
u | oo, like "food" | tūru
ā ē ī ō ū | the same vowel, held about twice as long | whā
wh | most speakers say "f"; some say a soft "w" | whare
ng | like "ng" in "singer", never "g" as in "finger" | ngeru
r | a quick tap of the tongue, between "r" and "d" | rua
NOTE: Words get stress on the first long vowel, or the first syllable if there is none. There are no consonant clusters, so every syllable ends in a vowel.
NOTE: Māori sentences begin with the verb marker. "Kei te" shows something happening now: "Kei te kai ahau" (I am eating). The pool is unreviewed and a few rare words are best guesses.
