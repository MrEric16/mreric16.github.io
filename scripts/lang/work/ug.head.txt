INTRO: Uyghur is written right to left in an Arabic-based alphabet that marks every vowel. The Latin shown is the Uyghur Latin spelling. There are no genders and no articles. The stress is usually on the last syllable.
a / e | "a" like "father" and "e" like "bed" | ata, ete
é | a close "ay", like "day" without the "y" | élip
i | a short "ih", like "sit" | ikki
o / u | rounded back vowels | ot, ulugh
ö | say "oh" with rounded lips, like German "ö" | öy
ü | say "ee" with rounded lips, like German "ü" | yüz
gh | a gargled French "r" | tagh
q | a "k" made far back in the throat | qiz
x | kh, like "ch" in Scottish "loch" | xeyr
ng | like "ng" in "sing" | ming
zh | like "s" in "measure" | zhurnal
NOTE: Vowel harmony: back vowels (a, i, o, u) and front vowels (e, é, ö, ü) are not mixed in one word. Suffixes follow the same rule.
NOTE: The Arabic-script line is written by rule from the Latin. Where a word starts with a vowel, the script shows the letter hamza first.
