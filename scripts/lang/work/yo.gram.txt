[grammar]
## Pronouns
> There is no he / she difference: "ó" means both. The short forms come before a verb: mo (I), o (you), ó (he, she), a (we), ẹ (you plural or polite), wọ́n (they). The long forms stand alone: èmi, ìwọ, òun, àwa, ẹ̀yin, àwọn.
row: I | mo / èmi | moh / EH-mee
row: you | o / ìwọ | oh / ee-WAW
row: he / she | ó / òun | oh / AW-oon
row: we | a / àwa | ah / AH-wah
row: you (plural or polite) | ẹ / ẹ̀yin | eh / EH-yeen
row: they | wọ́n / àwọn | WAWN / ah-WAWN
ex: I am a student | akẹ́kọ̀ọ́ ni mí | ah-keh-KAW-aw nee MEE
ex: you are tall | o ga | oh GAH
ex: she is my older sister | ẹ̀gbọ́n mi obìnrin ni | eh-GBAWN mee oh-bee-REEN nee
ex: we are friends | ọ̀rẹ́ ni wá | AW-reh nee WAH
ex: they are teachers | olùkọ́ ni wọ́n | oh-loo-KAW nee WAWN
## Singular and plural
> Nouns do not change. Put "àwọn" (the) before the noun for a plural: àwọn ọmọ (the children). With a number, the noun stays the same.
row: child | ọmọ | aw-MAW
row: children | àwọn ọmọ | ah-WAWN aw-MAW
row: book | ìwé | ee-WEH
row: two books | ìwé méjì | ee-WEH MEH-jee
ex: one house, houses | ilé kan, àwọn ilé | ee-LEH KAHN, ah-WAWN ee-LEH
ex: one book, books | ìwé kan, àwọn ìwé | ee-WEH KAHN, ah-WAWN ee-WEH
ex: one cat, cats | ológbò kan, àwọn ológbò | oh-LAW-gbaw KAHN, ah-WAWN oh-LAW-gbaw
ex: two books | ìwé méjì | ee-WEH MEH-jee
ex: the children | àwọn ọmọ | ah-WAWN aw-MAW
## To be: jẹ́, ni, wà
> "Ni" links a person or noun: mo jẹ́ akẹ́kọ̀ọ́ or akẹ́kọ̀ọ́ ni mí. "Wà" is used for places: mo wà nílé (I am at home). Adjectives are verbs: ó ga (he is tall).
row: I am a teacher | olùkọ́ ni mí | oh-loo-KAW nee MEE
row: this is a book | ìwé ni èyí | ee-WEH nee EH-yee
row: I am at home | mo wà nílé | moh WAH nee-LEH
row: I am tall | mo ga | moh GAH
ex: I am a student | akẹ́kọ̀ọ́ ni mí | ah-keh-KAW-aw nee MEE
ex: you are tired | àárẹ̀ mú ọ | AH-ah-REH moo AW
ex: he is a doctor | dókítà ni ó | doh-kee-TAH nee OH
ex: we are happy | inú wa dùn | ee-NOO wah DOON
ex: they are at home | wọ́n wà nílé | WAWN WAH nee-LEH
## Having: ní
> "Ní" means to have. Put it after the person: mo ní (I have), o ní (you have), ó ní (he has), a ní (we have), ẹ ní (you have), wọ́n ní (they have).
row: I have | mo ní | moh NEE
row: you have | o ní | oh NEE
row: he / she has | ó ní | oh NEE
row: I do not have | n kò ní | n kaw NEE
ex: I have an older brother | mo ní ẹ̀gbọ́n ọkùnrin | moh NEE eh-GBAWN aw-koo-REEN
ex: I have a car | mo ní ọkọ̀ | moh NEE AW-kaw
ex: I do not have time | n kò ní àkókò | n kaw NEE ah-KAW-kaw
ex: there is a problem | ìṣòro kan wà | ee-shaw-ROH KAHN WAH
ex: there is no water | kò sí omi | kaw SEE OH-mee
## Present tense
> Verbs never change. Add "ń" before the verb for an action in progress: mo ń jẹun (I am eating).
row: I eat | mo jẹun | moh jeh-OON
row: you eat | o jẹun | oh jeh-OON
row: he / she eats | ó jẹun | oh jeh-OON
row: I am eating | mo ń jẹun | moh n jeh-OON
ex: I am eating bread | mo ń jẹ búrẹ́dì | moh n JEH boo-REH-dee
ex: you are reading a book | o ń ka ìwé | oh n KAH ee-WEH
ex: she works in a bank | ó ń ṣiṣẹ́ ní báńkì | oh n shee-SHEH nee BAHN-kee
ex: we drink tea | a ń mu tíì | ah n MOO TEE-ee
ex: they are sleeping | wọ́n ń sùn | WAWN n SOON
## Basic sentences
> The order is subject, verb, object, just as in English. Adjectives come after the noun.
row: I read a book | mo ka ìwé | moh KAH ee-WEH
row: he drinks tea | ó mu tíì | oh MOO TEE-ee
row: a big house | ilé ńlá | ee-LEH n-LAH
row: a new car | ọkọ̀ tuntun | AW-kaw toon-TOON
ex: I read a book | mo ka ìwé | moh KAH ee-WEH
ex: he drinks tea | ó mu tíì | oh MOO TEE-ee
ex: we go to school | a lọ sí ilé ìwé | ah LAW see ee-LEH ee-WEH
ex: they eat bread | wọ́n jẹ búrẹ́dì | WAWN JEH boo-REH-dee
ex: the teacher writes | olùkọ́ ń kọ̀wé | oh-loo-KAW n kaw-WEH
## Negation
> Put "kò" (or "ò" after "mi") before the verb. After "mo" it becomes "n kò": n kò lọ (I do not go). Others: o kò, kò, a kò, ẹ kò, wọn kò.
row: I do not go | n kò lọ | n kaw LAW
row: you do not go | o kò lọ | oh kaw LAW
row: he / she does not go | kò lọ | kaw LAW
row: it is not good | kò dára | kaw dah-RAH
ex: I want | mo fẹ́ | moh FEH
ex: I do not want | n kò fẹ́ | n kaw FEH
ex: I speak English | mo ń sọ Gẹ̀ẹ́sì | moh n SAW GEH-eh-SEE
ex: I do not speak English | n kò sọ Gẹ̀ẹ́sì | n kaw SAW GEH-eh-SEE
ex: I like it | mo fẹ́ràn rẹ̀ | moh feh-RAHN REH
ex: I do not like it | n kò fẹ́ràn rẹ̀ | n kaw feh-RAHN REH
ex: she is here | ó wà níbí | oh WAH nee-BEE
ex: she is not here | kò sí níbí | kaw SEE nee-BEE
ex: I have a car | mo ní ọkọ̀ | moh NEE AW-kaw
ex: I do not have a car | n kò ní ọkọ̀ | n kaw NEE AW-kaw
ex: I understand | ó yé mi | oh YEH mee
ex: I do not understand | kò yé mi | kaw YEH mee
ex: we are tired | àárẹ̀ mú wa | AH-ah-REH moo WAH
ex: we are not tired | àárẹ̀ kò mú wa | AH-ah-REH kaw moo WAH
ex: he works on Sundays | ó ń ṣiṣẹ́ ní Ọjọ́ Àìkú | oh n shee-SHEH nee aw-JAW ah-ee-KOO
ex: he does not work on Sundays | kò ṣiṣẹ́ ní Ọjọ́ Àìkú | kaw shee-SHEH nee aw-JAW ah-ee-KOO
## Asking questions
> Start with "ṣé" (is it true that). Reply with the verb or "bẹ́ẹ̀ ni" (yes) or "rárá" (no).
row: Do you go? | ṣé o lọ? | sheh oh LAW
row: Is it good? | ṣé ó dára? | sheh oh dah-RAH
row: Is it right? | ṣé ó tọ̀nà? | sheh oh taw-NAH
ex: is this your book? | ṣé ìwé rẹ ni èyí? | sheh ee-WEH reh nee EH-yee
ex: are they here? | ṣé wọ́n wà níbí? | sheh WAWN wah nee-BEE
ex: do you like tea? | ṣé o fẹ́ràn tíì? | sheh oh feh-RAHN TEE-ee
ex: is it cold? | ṣé òtútù ń mú? | sheh aw-too-TOO n MOO
ex: do you speak English? | ṣé o ń sọ Gẹ̀ẹ́sì? | sheh oh n SAW GEH-eh-SEE
## Question words
> Question words usually come at the end of the sentence or in the place of the answer.
row: who | ta ni | TAH nee
row: what | kí ni | kee NEE
row: where | níbo | nee-BOH
row: when | ìgbà wo | ee-GBAH woh
row: why | kí ló dé | kee LOH deh
row: how | báwo | BAH-woh
row: how much | èló | eh-LOH
ex: what is your name? | kí ni orúkọ rẹ? | kee nee oh-ROO-kaw REH
ex: who is he? | ta ni ó? | TAH nee OH
ex: where do you live? | níbo ni o ń gbé? | nee-BOH nee oh n GBEH
ex: when is the lesson? | ìgbà wo ni ẹ̀kọ́? | ee-GBAH woh nee eh-KAW
ex: why are you late? | kí ló dé tí o fi pẹ́? | kee LOH deh tee oh fee PEH
ex: how much is it? | èló ni? | eh-LOH nee
ex: which book? | ìwé wo? | ee-WEH WOH
## Possessives and liking
> Put the owner after the thing: ìwé mi (book my), ìwé rẹ (your book), ìwé rẹ̀ (his book), ìwé wa (our book), ìwé yín (your book, plural), ìwé wọn (their book).
row: my book | ìwé mi | ee-WEH MEE
row: your book | ìwé rẹ | ee-WEH REH
row: his / her book | ìwé rẹ̀ | ee-WEH REH
ex: my phone | fóònù mi | foh-OH-noo MEE
ex: your house (formal) | ilé yín | ee-LEH YEEN
ex: his sister | arábìnrin rẹ̀ | ah-rah-bee-REEN REH
ex: I like music | mo fẹ́ràn orin | moh feh-RAHN OH-reen
ex: I love football | mo nífẹ̀ẹ́ bọ́ọ̀lù | moh nee-FEH-eh baw-AW-loo
## Formal vs informal
> Show respect with "ẹ" (plural) for one older person, and add "ẹ kú" (well done) greetings. "Sir" is "ọ̀gá" or "bàbá". "Madam" is "ìyá" or "ma". Use "o" with a friend or a child.
row: sir | sà | SAH
row: madam | ma | MAH
row: well done (greeting) | ẹ kú iṣẹ́ | eh KOO ee-SHEH
ex: how are you? (informal) | báwo ni? | BAH-woh nee
ex: how are you? (formal) | báwo ni ẹ ṣe wà? | BAH-woh nee eh sheh WAH
ex: where are you going? (informal) | níbo ni o ń lọ? | nee-BOH nee oh n LAW
ex: where are you going? (formal) | níbo ni ẹ ń lọ? | nee-BOH nee eh n LAW
ex: please sit down (formal) | ẹ jọ̀ọ́ jókòó | eh JAW-aw jaw-KAW-aw
## Past and future (simple)
> There is no past tense. Context shows it. Use "ti" for done: mo ti jẹun (I have eaten). For the future, add "yóò" or "á": mo á jẹun (I will eat).
row: I ate | mo ti jẹun | moh tee jeh-OON
row: I will eat | mo á jẹun | moh ah jeh-OON
row: I will go tomorrow | mo á lọ ní ọ̀la | moh ah LAW nee AW-lah
ex: yesterday I was tired | àná, àárẹ̀ mú mi | ah-NAH, AH-ah-REH moo MEE
ex: I will study tomorrow | mo á kẹ́kọ̀ọ́ ní ọ̀la | moh ah keh-KAW-aw nee AW-lah
ex: we went to the park | a lọ sí ọgbà ìtura | ah LAW see aw-GBAH ee-too-RAH
ex: I saw him | mo rí i | moh REE ee
ex: she wrote a letter | ó kọ lẹ́tà kan | oh KAW leh-TAH KAHN
## Commands
> Use the bare verb. Add "jọ̀ọ́" (please) before it. To forbid, put "má" in front: má lọ (do not go).
row: come! | wá! | WAH
row: wait! | dúró! | doo-ROH
row: do not go | má lọ | mah LAW
row: please wait | jọ̀ọ́ dúró | JAW-aw doo-ROH
ex: come here | wá síbí | WAH see-BEE
ex: do not come here | má wá síbí | mah WAH see-BEE
ex: sit down | jókòó | jaw-KAW-aw
ex: do not sit down | má jókòó | mah jaw-KAW-aw
ex: be quiet | dákẹ́ | dah-KEH
ex: do not be late | má pẹ́ | mah PEH
ex: open the door | ṣí ilẹ̀kùn | SHEE ee-leh-KOON
ex: close the window | tì fèrèsé | TEE feh-reh-SEH
ex: listen to me | gbọ́ tèmi | GBAW teh-MEE
ex: do not worry | má ṣàníyàn | mah shah-nee-YAHN
ex: speak slowly | sọ̀rọ̀ díẹ̀díẹ̀ | SAW-raw DEE-eh DEE-eh
ex: wait a moment | dúró díẹ̀ | doo-ROH DEE-eh
## Can, must, want
> "Lè" (can) and "fẹ́" (want) go before the main verb. "Gbọ́dọ̀" (must) goes before the verb too. "Kò lè" means cannot.
row: I can swim | mo lè wẹ̀ | moh LEH WEH
row: I must go | mo gbọ́dọ̀ lọ | moh gbaw-DAW LAW
row: I want to go | mo fẹ́ lọ | moh FEH LAW
row: may I? | ṣé mo lè? | sheh moh LEH
ex: I can swim | mo lè wẹ̀ | moh LEH WEH
ex: I cannot swim | n kò lè wẹ̀ | n kaw LEH WEH
ex: can you help me? | ṣé o lè ràn mí lọ́wọ́? | sheh oh LEH rahn MEE LAW-woh
ex: we must go | a gbọ́dọ̀ lọ | ah gbaw-DAW LAW
ex: we must not go | a kò gbọ́dọ̀ lọ | ah kaw gbaw-DAW LAW
ex: I want to eat | mo fẹ́ jẹun | moh FEH jeh-OON
ex: I do not want to eat | n kò fẹ́ jẹun | n kaw FEH jeh-OON
ex: you can come | o lè wá | oh LEH WAH
ex: you cannot come | o kò lè wá | oh kaw LEH WAH
ex: she wants to study | ó fẹ́ kẹ́kọ̀ọ́ | oh FEH keh-KAW-aw
ex: I must work today | mo gbọ́dọ̀ ṣiṣẹ́ lónìí | moh gbaw-DAW shee-SHEH law-NEE-ee
ex: you must not run | o kò gbọ́dọ̀ sá | oh kaw gbaw-DAW SAH
## In, on, at
> Yoruba uses little words: "ní" (in, at), "lórí" (on), "lábẹ́" (under), "lẹ́bàá" (next to), "nítòsí" (near), "sí" (to), "láti" (from).
row: in the house | nínú ilé | nee-NOO ee-LEH
row: on the table | lórí tábìlì | law-REE tah-bee-LEE
row: under the table | lábẹ́ tábìlì | lah-BEH tah-bee-LEE
row: at school | ní ilé ìwé | nee ee-LEH ee-WEH
ex: the book is on the table | ìwé wà lórí tábìlì | ee-WEH WAH law-REE tah-bee-LEE
ex: the cat is under the table | ológbò wà lábẹ́ tábìlì | oh-LAW-gbaw WAH lah-BEH tah-bee-LEE
ex: I am at school | mo wà ní ilé ìwé | moh WAH nee ee-LEH ee-WEH
ex: I am at home | mo wà nílé | moh WAH nee-LEH
ex: the shop is next to the bank | ṣọ́ọ̀bù wà lẹ́bàá báńkì | shaw-AW-boo WAH leh-BAH-ah BAHN-kee
ex: the park is near the station | ọgbà ìtura wà nítòsí ibùdó | aw-GBAH ee-too-RAH WAH nee-taw-SEE ee-boo-DOH
ex: we meet in the morning | a ó pàdé ní òwúrọ̀ | ah oh pah-DEH nee oh-woo-RAW
ex: the lesson is at nine | ẹ̀kọ́ jẹ́ aago mẹ́sàn-án | eh-KAW JEH AH-ah-goh meh-SAHN-ahn
ex: the key is in the bag | kọ́kọ́rọ́ wà nínú àpò | kaw-kaw-RAW WAH nee-NOO ah-PAW
## Comparing things
> Use "ju" (than) after the adjective: tóbi ju (bigger than). For the most, say "ju gbogbo wọn lọ" (more than all of them).
row: big, bigger | tóbi, tóbi jù | taw-BEE, taw-BEE JOO
row: good, better | dára, dára jù | dah-RAH, dah-RAH JOO
row: the biggest | tóbi jù gbogbo wọn lọ | taw-BEE joo GBOH-gboh WAWN LAW
ex: big, bigger, the biggest | tóbi, tóbi jù, tóbi jù gbogbo wọn lọ | taw-BEE, taw-BEE JOO, taw-BEE joo GBOH-gboh WAWN LAW
ex: small, smaller, the smallest | kékeré, kékeré jù, kékeré jù gbogbo wọn lọ | keh-keh-REH, keh-keh-REH JOO, keh-keh-REH joo GBOH-gboh WAWN LAW
ex: this is better than that | èyí dára ju ìyẹn lọ | EH-yee dah-RAH joo EE-yehn LAW
ex: this is worse than that | èyí burú ju ìyẹn lọ | EH-yee boo-ROO joo EE-yehn LAW
ex: she is taller than me | ó ga jù mí lọ | oh GAH joo MEE LAW
ex: Tashkent is bigger than Samarkand | Tashkent tóbi ju Samarkand lọ | tahsh-KENT taw-BEE joo sah-mar-KAHND LAW
ex: it is the cheapest | òun ni ó wọ́n jù | AW-oon nee oh WAWN JOO
ex: the same as | bákan náà pẹ̀lú | bah-KAHN NAH-ah peh-LOO
