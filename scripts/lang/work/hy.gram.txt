[grammar]
## Pronouns
> Armenian often drops the pronoun, because the verb shows who is speaking. There is no he / she difference: "na" means both.
row: I | yes
row: you (informal) | du
row: he / she / it | na
row: we | menk'
row: you (formal or plural) | duk'
row: they | nrank'
ex: I am a student | yes usanogh em | yes oo-sah-NOGH em
ex: you are tall | du bardzr es | doo BAHRDZR es
ex: she is my older sister | na im mets k'uyrn ê | nah eem mets kooy-RUHN ê
ex: we are friends | menk' ənkernerenk' | mek-NK un-ker-neh-REH-nk
ex: they are teachers | nrank' usuts'ich'ner en | nrahnk oo-soo-tseech-NER en
## Singular and plural
> Add -er after a one-syllable word, and -ner after a longer word. After a number, the noun stays singular: erku girk' (two book).
row: girk' (book) | girk'er
row: tun (house) | tuner
row: erekha (child) | erekhaner
row: after a number: singular | erku girk'
ex: one house, houses | mek tun, tuner | mek TOON too-NER
ex: one book, books | mek girk', girk'er | mek GEERK geer-KER
ex: one cat, cats | mek katu, katuner | mek kah-TOO kah-too-NER
ex: two books | erku girk' | er-KOO GEERK
ex: the children | erekhanerə | eh-reh-khah-neh-RUH
## To be: em, es, ê
> "To be" is a real verb. It comes after the noun or adjective: em, es, ê, enk', ek', en.
row: I am | em
row: you are | es
row: he / she is | ê
row: we are | enk'
row: you are (formal) | ek'
row: they are | en
ex: I am a student | yes usanogh em | yes oo-sah-NOGH em
ex: you are tired | du hognats es | doo hog-NAHTS es
ex: he is a doctor | na bzhishk ê | nah bzheeshk ê
ex: we are happy | menk' erjanik enk' | mek-NK er-jah-NEEK eh-nk
ex: they are at home | nrank' tann en | nrahnk tahnn en
## Unem: have
> Armenian says "I have": unem, unes, uni, unenk', unek', unen. "Ka" means "there is" and "chka" means "there is not".
row: I have | unem
row: you have | unes
row: he / she has | uni
row: we have | unenk'
row: there is | ka
row: there is not | ch'ka
ex: I have an older brother | mets eghbayr unem | mets egh-BAYR oo-NEM
ex: I have a car | mek'ena unem | meh-keh-NAH oo-NEM
ex: I do not have time | zhamanak ch'unem | zhah-mah-NAHK choo-NEM
ex: there is a problem | khndir ka | khn-DEER kah
ex: there is no water | jur ch'ka | joor chkah
## Present tense: -um em
> Verb stem + -um + a form of "to be". Utel (to eat): utum em, utum es, utum ê.
row: I eat | utum em
row: you eat | utum es
row: he / she eats | utum ê
row: we eat | utum enk'
row: you eat (formal) | utum ek'
row: they eat | utum en
ex: I am eating bread | hats' utum em | hahts oo-TOOM em
ex: you are reading a book | girk' kardum es | geerk kahr-DOOM es
ex: she works in a bank | na bankum ashkhatum ê | nah bahn-KOOM ahsh-khah-TOOM ê
ex: we drink tea | t'ey enk' khmum | tey eh-nk khmoom
ex: they are sleeping | nrank' k'num en | nrahnk koo-NOOM en
## Basic sentences
> Order: who, where or when, what, verb. Armenian has no articles: "girk'" means "a book" or "the book".
row: I read a book | yes girk' kardum em
row: she drinks tea | na t'ey khmum ê
row: we go to school | menk' dprots' enk' gnum
ex: I read a book | yes girk' kardum em | yes geerk kahr-DOOM em
ex: he drinks tea | na t'ey khmum ê | nah tey khmoom ê
ex: we go to school | menk' dprots' enk' gnum | mek-NK dprots eh-nk gnoom
ex: they eat bread | nrank' hats' utum en | nrahnk hahts oo-TOOM en
ex: the teacher writes | usuts'ich'ə grum ê | oo-soo-tsee-CHUH groom ê
## Negation
> Replace "em" with "ch'em", "es" with "ch'es", and so on. "Ch'ka" means "there is not".
row: I go | gnum em
row: I do not go | ch'em gnum
row: is | ê
row: is not | ch'i
ex: I want | uzum em | oo-ZOOM em
ex: I do not want | ch'em uzum | chem oo-ZOOM
ex: I speak English | angleren khosum em | ahn-gleh-REN kho-SOOM em
ex: I do not speak English | angleren ch'em khosum | ahn-gleh-REN chem kho-SOOM
ex: I like it | indz dur ê galis | eendz door ê gah-LEES
ex: I do not like it | indz dur ch'i galis | eendz door chee gah-LEES
ex: she is here | na aystegh ê | nah ays-TEGH ê
ex: she is not here | na aystegh ch'i | nah ays-TEGH chee
ex: I have a car | mek'ena unem | meh-keh-NAH oo-NEM
ex: I do not have a car | mek'ena ch'unem | meh-keh-NAH choo-NEM
ex: I understand | haskanum em | hahs-kah-NOOM em
ex: I do not understand | ch'em haskanum | chem hahs-kah-NOOM
ex: we are tired | hognats enk' | hog-NAHTS eh-nk
ex: we are not tired | hognats ch'enk' | hog-NAHTS cheh-nk
ex: he works on Sundays | kirakin ashkhatum ê | kee-rah-KEEN ahsh-khah-TOOM ê
ex: he does not work on Sundays | kirakin ch'i ashkhatum | kee-rah-KEEN chee ahsh-khah-TOOM
## Asking questions
> A yes or no question uses the same words as the statement, with a rising voice on the word you ask about.
row: you are a student | du usanogh es
row: are you a student? | du usanogh es?
row: he is here | na aystegh ê
row: is he here? | na aystegh ê?
ex: is this your book? | sa k'o girk'ə ê? | sah ko geer-KUH ê
ex: are they here? | nrank' aystegh en? | nrahnk ays-TEGH en
ex: do you like tea? | t'ey dur ê galis? | tey door ê gah-LEES
ex: is it cold? | ts'urt ê? | tsoort ê
ex: do you speak English? | angleren khosum ek'? | ahn-gleh-REN kho-SOOM ek
## Question words
> Question words stand just before the verb.
row: what | inch'
row: who | ov
row: where | ur
row: when | erb
row: why | inch'u
row: how | inch'pes
row: how much | inch'k'an
row: which | or
ex: what is your name? | inch' ê k'o anunə? | eench ê ko ah-NOO-nuh
ex: who is he? | ov ê na? | ov ê nah
ex: where do you live? | ur es aprum? | oor es ahp-ROOM
ex: when is the lesson? | erb ê dasə? | erb ê dah-SUH
ex: why are you late? | inch'u es ushanum? | een-CHOO es oo-shah-NOOM
ex: how much is it? | inch'k'an ê? | een-chkahn ê
ex: which book? | or girk'ə? | or geer-KUH
## Possessives and liking
> Put the word before the noun: im (my), k'o (your), nra (his / her), mer (our), dzer (your, formal), nrants' (their). To like something, say "it pleases me": indz dur ê galis.
row: my book | im girk'ə
row: your book | k'o girk'ə
row: his / her book | nra girk'ə
row: our book | mer girk'ə
row: your book (formal) | dzer girk'ə
ex: my phone | im herrakhosə | eem heh-rah-kho-SUH
ex: your house (formal) | dzer tunə | dzer too-NUH
ex: his sister | nra k'uyrə | nrah kooy-RUH
ex: I like music | indz yerazhshtut'yun dur ê galis | eendz yeh-rahzh-shtoot-YOON door ê gah-LEES
ex: I love football | futbolə sirum em | foot-bo-LUH see-ROOM em
## Formal vs informal
> du (informal): friends, family, children. duk' (formal): strangers, older people, work. The verb changes: es (informal), ek' (formal).
row: how are you? | inch'pes es? / inch'pes ek'?
row: you speak | khosum es / khosum ek'
row: your name | k'o anun / dzer anun
ex: how are you? (informal) | inch'pes es? | een-CHPES es
ex: how are you? (formal) | inch'pes ek'? | een-CHPES ek
ex: where are you going? (informal) | ur es gnum? | oor es gnoom
ex: where are you going? (formal) | ur ek' gnum? | oor ek gnoom
ex: please sit down (formal) | nstek', khndrum em | nstek, khn-DROOM em
## Past and future (simple)
> The past adds -ts' or -ets' to the stem (gnats'i, I went). The future puts k- in front of the present: kgnam (I will go).
row: I went | gnats'i
row: you went | gnats'ir
row: he went | gnats'
row: we went | gnats'ink'
ex: yesterday I was tired | erek hognats ei | eh-REK hog-NAHTS ei
ex: I will study tomorrow | vaghə ksovorem | vah-GHUH kso-vo-REM
ex: we went to the park | park gnats'ink' | pahrk gnah-tsee-NK
ex: I saw him | tesa nran | teh-SAH nrahn
ex: she wrote a letter | na namak grets' | nah nah-MAHK grets
## Commands
> Informal: the short form (ari). Formal: add -k' (arek'). To forbid, put "mi" in front.
row: come (informal) | ari
row: come (formal) | arek'
row: do not come | mi ari
row: sit down | nstir
ex: come here | ari aystegh | ah-REE ays-TEGH
ex: do not come here | mi ari aystegh | mee ah-REE ays-TEGH
ex: sit down | nstir | nsteer
ex: do not sit down | mi nstir | mee nsteer
ex: be quiet | hangist eghir | hahn-GEEST eh-GHEER
ex: do not be late | mi ushats'ir | mee oo-shah-TSEER
ex: open the door | bats'ir durrə | bah-TSEER doo-RRUH
ex: close the window | p'akir patuhanə | pah-KEER pah-too-hah-NUH
ex: listen to me | lsir indz | lseer eendz
ex: do not worry | mi anhangstats'ir | mee ahn-hahn-gstah-TSEER
ex: speak slowly | dandagh khosir | dahn-DAHGH kho-SEER
ex: wait a moment | spasir mi rrope | spah-SEER mee rro-PEH
## Can, must, want
> Can: karogh em + verb. Must: petk' ê + verb. Want: uzum em + verb.
row: I can swim | karogh em loghal
row: I cannot swim | ch'em karogh loghal
row: I must go | petk' ê gnam
row: I want to go | uzum em gnal
ex: I can swim | karogh em loghal | kah-ROGH em lo-GHAHL
ex: I cannot swim | ch'em karogh loghal | chem kah-ROGH lo-GHAHL
ex: can you help me? | karogh ek' ôgnel indz? | kah-ROGH ek ogh-NEL eendz
ex: we must go | petk' ê gnank' | petk ê gnahnk
ex: we must not go | ch'i kareli gnal | chee kah-reh-LEE gnahl
ex: I want to eat | uzum em utel | oo-ZOOM em oo-TEL
ex: I do not want to eat | ch'em uzum utel | chem oo-ZOOM oo-TEL
ex: you can come | karogh ek' gal | kah-ROGH ek gahl
ex: you cannot come | ch'ek' karogh gal | chek kah-ROGH gahl
ex: she wants to study | na uzum ê sovorel | nah oo-ZOOM ê so-vo-REL
ex: I must work today | aysôr petk' ê ashkhatem | ay-SOR petk ê ahsh-khah-TEM
ex: you must not run | ch'i kareli vazel | chee kah-reh-LEE vah-ZEL
## In, on, at
> Armenian uses endings and small words after the noun: -um (in), -i vra (on), -i mot (near), -its' (from), -in (to).
row: in | -um
row: on | -i vra
row: near | -i mot
row: from | -its'
ex: the book is on the table | girk'ə seghani vra ê | geer-KUH seh-gha-NEE vrah ê
ex: the cat is under the table | katun seghani tak ê | kah-TOON seh-gha-NEE tahk ê
ex: I am at school | dprots'um em | dprots-OOM em
ex: I am at home | tann em | tahnn em
ex: the shop is next to the bank | khanut'ə banki mot ê | khah-noo-TUH bahn-KEE mot ê
ex: the park is near the station | parkə kayarani mot ê | pahr-KUH kah-yah-rah-NEE mot ê
ex: we meet in the morning | arravotyan khandipenk' | ah-rah-vot-YAHN khahn-dee-peh-NK
ex: the lesson is at nine | dasə inn zhamin ê | dah-SUH een zhah-MEEN ê
ex: the key is in the bag | banalin payusaki mej ê | bah-nah-LEE-nuh pah-yoo-sah-KEE mej ê
## Comparing things
> Put "aveli" before the adjective for "more": aveli mets. For "the most" use "amenaveli": amenaveli mets. "Than" is "k'an".
row: big, bigger, the biggest | mets, aveli mets, amenaveli mets
row: small, smaller, the smallest | p'ok'r, aveli p'ok'r, amenaveli p'ok'r
ex: big, bigger, the biggest | mets, aveli mets, amenaveli mets | mets ah-veh-LEE mets ah-meh-nah-veh-LEE mets
ex: small, smaller, the smallest | p'ok'r, aveli p'ok'r, amenaveli p'ok'r | pok-R ah-veh-LEE pok-R ah-meh-nah-veh-LEE pok-R
ex: this is better than that | sa aveli lav ê k'an ayn | sah ah-veh-LEE lahv ê kahn ayn
ex: this is worse than that | sa aveli vat ê k'an ayn | sah ah-veh-LEE vaht ê kahn ayn
ex: she is taller than me | na indzanits' aveli bardzr ê | nah een-dzah-NEETS ah-veh-LEE bahrdzr ê
ex: Tashkent is bigger than Samarkand | T'ashk'endə aveli mets ê k'an Samarkandə | tahsh-ken-DUH ah-veh-LEE mets ê kahn sah-mahr-kahn-DUH
ex: it is the cheapest | sa amenaveli êzhann ê | sah ah-meh-nah-veh-LEE ê-ZHAHN-nuh ê
ex: the same as | nuynə | nooy-NUH
