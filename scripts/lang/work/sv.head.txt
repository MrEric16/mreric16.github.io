INTRO: Swedish is written in the Latin alphabet with three extra letters at the end: å, ä, ö. Nouns are "en" or "ett" words. The language has a sing-song pitch, and long and short vowels matter.
å | like "aw" in "saw" | å, båt
ä | like "e" in "bed" (like "air" before r) | äpple, är
ö | say "ur" with rounded lips, like "ur" in "fur" | öl, öga
y | say "ee" with rounded lips, like German "ü" | by, ny
u | a rounded "ew", like a short "oo" said with the lips forward | hus
sj / skj / stj | a hushing sound, between English "sh" and German "ch" | sju, skjorta
tj / k (before e, i, y, ä, ö) | like a soft "ch" in "chin" | tjugo, kista
g / j | "y", as in "yes", before e, i, y, ä, ö | gärna, jag
NOTE: Double consonants make the vowel before them short. A single consonant after a stressed vowel means the vowel is long.
