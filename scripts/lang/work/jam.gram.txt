[grammar]
## Pronouns
> Patois pronouns do not change for subject or object. "Mi" is both I and me, and "wi" is we and us. "Unu" (or "yuh") can mean you (plural). Only he / she / it have forms: im, shi, it. The plural is "dem".
row: I / me | mi | mee
row: you (one) | yuh | yuh
row: he / him | im | eem
row: she / her | shi | shee
row: we / us | wi | wee
row: you (plural) | unu | OO-noo
row: they / them | dem | dem
ex: I am a student | mi a student | mee a STOO-dent
ex: you are tall | yuh tall | yuh tawl
ex: she is my older sister | shi a mi big sista | shee a mee big SIS-ta
ex: we are friends | wi a fren dem | wee a fren dem
ex: they are teachers | dem a tiicha dem | dem a TEE-cha dem
## Singular and plural
> Nouns do not change. Add "dem" after the noun for many: di buk dem (the books). Use a number and the noun stays the same: tuu buk.
row: the book | di buk | dee buk
row: the books | di buk dem | dee buk dem
row: the child | di pickney | dee PIK-nee
row: the children | di pickney dem | dee PIK-nee dem
ex: one house, houses | wan hous, hous dem | wun hows, hows dem
ex: one book, books | wan buk, buk dem | wun buk, buk dem
ex: one cat, cats | wan kyat, kyat dem | wun kyat, kyat dem
ex: two books | tuu buk | too buk
ex: the children | di pickney dem | dee PIK-nee dem
## To be: a, deh, ier
> Patois has no verb "to be". "A" links two nouns: mi a tiicha (I am a teacher). Adjectives stand on their own: shi tall. "Deh" says where: mi deh a skuul (I am at school).
row: I am a teacher | mi a tiicha | mee a TEE-cha
row: this is a book | dis a buk | dis a buk
row: I am at home | mi deh a yaad | mee deh a yard
row: I am tall | mi tall | mee tawl
ex: I am a student | mi a student | mee a STOO-dent
ex: you are tired | yuh tai'd | yuh tied
ex: he is a doctor | im a daktar | eem a DOK-ta
ex: we are happy | wi hapi | wee HAP-ee
ex: they are at home | dem deh a yaad | dem deh a yard
## Having: mi av
> "Av" (or "hav") means have: mi av a kyar (I have a car). Say "mi nuh av" for I do not have. "Deh" with "a" can also say "there is": a deh wan problem.
row: I have a car | mi av a kyar | mee av a kyar
row: you have a car | yuh av a kyar | yuh av a kyar
row: he / she has a car | im av a kyar | eem av a kyar
row: I do not have | mi nuh av | mee nuh av
ex: I have an older brother | mi av wan big breda | mee av wun big BRED-a
ex: I have a car | mi av a kyar | mee av a kyar
ex: I do not have time | mi nuh av taim | mee nuh av time
ex: there is a problem | wan problem deh | wun PROB-lem deh
ex: there is no water | nuh wata nuh deh | nuh WAH-ta nuh deh
## Present tense
> Verbs do not change. Put "a" before the verb for something happening now: mi a nyam (I am eating). Without "a", it is a habit or a plain fact.
row: I am eating | mi a nyam | mee a nyam
row: you are eating | yuh a nyam | yuh a nyam
row: he / she is eating | im a nyam | eem a nyam
row: we are eating | wi a nyam | wee a nyam
ex: I am eating bread | mi a nyam bred | mee a nyam bred
ex: you are reading a book | yuh a riid a buk | yuh a reed a buk
ex: she works in a bank | shi wok inna bangk | shee wok in-a bank
ex: we drink tea | wi drink tii | wee drink tee
ex: they are sleeping | dem a sliip | dem a sleep
## Basic sentences
> The order is subject, verb, object, like English: "mi riid di buk" (I read the book). Adjectives come before the noun: big hous.
row: I read a book | mi riid a buk | mee reed a buk
row: he drinks tea | im drink tii | eem drink tee
row: a big house | a big hous | a big hows
row: a new car | a nyuu kyar | a nyoo kyar
ex: I read a book | mi riid a buk | mee reed a buk
ex: he drinks tea | im drink tii | eem drink tee
ex: we go to school | wi go a skuul | wee go a skool
ex: they eat bread | dem nyam bred | dem nyam bred
ex: the teacher writes | di tiicha a rait | dee TEE-cha a rite
## Negation
> Put "nuh" before the verb: mi nuh nuo (I do not know). For the past, use "neva": mi neva si im (I did not see him). For "can't", use "kyaan": mi kyaan go.
row: I do not go | mi nuh go | mee nuh go
row: you do not go | yuh nuh go | yuh nuh go
row: he / she does not go | im nuh go | eem nuh go
row: it is not good | it nuh gud | it nuh gud
ex: I want | mi waan | mee wahn
ex: I do not want | mi nuh waan | mee nuh wahn
ex: I speak English | mi taak Inglish | mee tak ING-glish
ex: I do not speak English | mi nuh taak Inglish | mee nuh tak ING-glish
ex: I like it | mi laik it | mee like it
ex: I do not like it | mi nuh laik it | mee nuh like it
ex: she is here | shi deh yah | shee deh yah
ex: she is not here | shi nuh deh yah | shee nuh deh yah
ex: I have a car | mi av a kyar | mee av a kyar
ex: I do not have a car | mi nuh av no kyar | mee nuh av noh kyar
ex: I understand | mi andastan | mee an-da-STAN
ex: I do not understand | mi nuh andastan | mee nuh an-da-STAN
ex: we are tired | wi tai'd | wee tied
ex: we are not tired | wi nuh tai'd | wee nuh tied
ex: he works on Sundays | im wok pan Sundeh | eem wok pan SUN-day
ex: he does not work on Sundays | im nuh wok pan Sundeh | eem nuh wok pan SUN-day
## Asking questions
> Raise your voice at the end, or start with a question word. There is no word order change: "Yuh a go?" (Are you going?). Answer "yes" or "nuh".
row: Do you go? | yuh go? | yuh go
row: Is it good? | it gud? | it gud
row: Is it right? | it rait? | it rite
ex: is this your book? | dis a yuh buk? | dis a yuh buk
ex: are they here? | dem deh yah? | dem deh yah
ex: do you like tea? | yuh laik tii? | yuh like tee
ex: is it cold? | it kuol? | it kohl
ex: do you speak English? | yuh taak Inglish? | yuh tak ING-glish
## Question words
> Question words come at the start. "Weh" means where, "ow" means how.
row: who | huu | hoo
row: what | wah | wah
row: where | weh | weh
row: when | wen | wen
row: why | fi wah | fee wah
row: how | ow | ow
row: how much | ow much | ow much
ex: what is your name? | wah yuh niem? | wah yuh NYAME
ex: who is he? | huu im a? | hoo eem a
ex: where do you live? | weh yuh liv? | weh yuh liv
ex: when is the lesson? | wen di klaas deh? | wen dee klas deh
ex: why are you late? | fi wah yuh late? | fee wah yuh layt
ex: how much is it? | ow much it kos? | ow much it kos
ex: which book? | wich buk? | wich buk
## Possessives and liking
> Possessives do not change: mi (my), yuh (your), im (his), shi (her), wi (our), unu (your, plural), dem (their). Put them before the noun: mi buk, yuh buk.
row: my book | mi buk | mee buk
row: my father | mi fada | mee FAD-a
row: your book | yuh buk | yuh buk
row: his / her book | im buk | eem buk
ex: my phone | mi fuon | mee fone
ex: your house (formal) | yuh hous, sa | yuh hows sah
ex: his sister | im sista | eem SIS-ta
ex: I like music | mi laik myuuzik | mee like MYOO-zik
ex: I love football | mi luv fuutbaal | mee luv FOOT-bawl
## Formal vs informal
> Patois has no formal verb form. Show respect with "sa" (sir) or "ma'am", and by speaking in a gentler, more English way.
row: sir | sa | sah
row: madam | ma'am | mam
ex: how are you? (informal) | ow yuh du? | ow yuh DOO
ex: how are you? (formal) | ow yuh du, sa? | ow yuh DOO sah
ex: where are you going? (informal) | weh yuh a go? | weh yuh a go
ex: where are you going? (formal) | weh yuh a go, sa? | weh yuh a go sah
ex: please sit down (formal) | pliiz, sit dong, sa | pleez sit dong sah
## Past and future (simple)
> Past: "did" before the verb (mi did go, I went) or the verb alone. Future: "wi" or "a go" before the verb: mi a go kom (I will come).
row: I ate | mi did nyam | mee did nyam
row: I will eat | mi a go nyam | mee a go nyam
row: I will go tomorrow | mi a go go tumaara | mee a go go too-MAR-ah
ex: yesterday I was tired | yestideh mi did tai'd | YES-tee-day mee did tied
ex: I will study tomorrow | mi a go stodi tumaara | mee a go STUD-ee too-MAR-ah
ex: we went to the park | wi did go a di paak | wee did go a dee park
ex: I saw him | mi did si im | mee did see eem
ex: she wrote a letter | shi did rait a leta | shee did rite a LET-a
## Commands
> A command is the plain verb: "kom yah" (come here). To forbid, use "nuh" first: "nuh go" (do not go).
row: come! | kom! | kom
row: wait! | wiet! | wayt
row: do not go | nuh go | nuh go
row: please wait | wiet, pliiz | wayt pleez
ex: come here | kom yah | kom yah
ex: do not come here | nuh kom yah | nuh kom yah
ex: sit down | sit dong | sit dong
ex: do not sit down | nuh sit dong | nuh sit dong
ex: be quiet | kiip kwaiyet | keep KWY-et
ex: do not be late | nuh late | nuh layt
ex: open the door | uopn di duo | OH-pn dee doh
ex: close the window | kluoz di windo | kloz dee WIN-doh
ex: listen to me | lisn tu mi | LIS-n too mee
ex: do not worry | nuh worid | nuh WUR-id
ex: speak slowly | taak sloo | tak sloo
ex: wait a moment | wiet a minit | wayt a MIN-it
## Can, must, want
> "Kyan" (can), "mos" (must) and "waan" (want) come before the verb: mi kyan swim, mi mos go, mi waan nyam.
row: I can swim | mi kyan swim | mee kyan swim
row: I must go | mi mos go | mee mos go
row: I want to go | mi waan go | mee wahn go
row: may I? | mi kyan? | mee kyan
ex: I can swim | mi kyan swim | mee kyan swim
ex: I cannot swim | mi kyaan swim | mee kyan swim
ex: can you help me? | yuh kyan help mi? | yuh kyan help mee
ex: we must go | wi mos go | wee mos go
ex: we must not go | wi mussi go | wee MUS-ee go
ex: I want to eat | mi waan nyam | mee wahn nyam
ex: I do not want to eat | mi nuh waan nyam | mee nuh wahn nyam
ex: you can come | yuh kyan kom | yuh kyan kom
ex: you cannot come | yuh kyaan kom | yuh kyan kom
ex: she wants to study | shi waan stodi | shee wahn STUD-ee
ex: I must work today | mi mos wok tideh | mee mos wok tee-DAY
ex: you must not run | yuh mussi run | yuh MUS-ee run
## In, on, at
> Patois uses small words: "a" or "inna" (in, at), "pan" (on), "anda" (under), "nex tu" (next to), "nier" (near).
row: in the house | inna di hous | in-a dee hows
row: on the table | pan di tiebl | pan dee TAY-bl
row: under the table | anda di tiebl | UN-da dee TAY-bl
row: at school | a skuul | a skool
ex: the book is on the table | di buk deh pan di tiebl | dee buk deh pan dee TAY-bl
ex: the cat is under the table | di kyat deh anda di tiebl | dee kyat deh UN-da dee TAY-bl
ex: I am at school | mi deh a skuul | mee deh a skool
ex: I am at home | mi deh a yaad | mee deh a yard
ex: the shop is next to the bank | di shap deh nex tu di bangk | dee shap deh neks too dee bank
ex: the park is near the station | di paak deh nier di steshan | dee park deh neer dee STAY-shan
ex: we meet in the morning | wi mit inna di mawnin | wee mit in-a dee MAW-nin
ex: the lesson is at nine | di klaas a nain a klaak | dee klas a nine a klok
ex: the key is in the bag | di kii deh inna di bag | dee kee deh in-a dee bag
## Comparing things
> Add "-a" to a short adjective or use "mo" for "more": big, bigga. Use "pas" or "ova" for "than": dis bigga pas dat. For "the most", say "di mos" or "di bigges".
row: big, bigger | big, bigga | big, BIG-a
row: good, better | gud, beta | gud, BET-a
row: the biggest | di bigges | dee BIG-es
ex: big, bigger, the biggest | big, bigga, bigges | big, BIG-a, BIG-es
ex: small, smaller, the smallest | likkle, likkla, likklis | LIK-l, LIK-la, LIK-lis
ex: this is better than that | dis beta pas dat | dis BET-a pas dat
ex: this is worse than that | dis waas pas dat | dis wos pas dat
ex: she is taller than me | shi tala pas mi | shee TAW-la pas mee
ex: Tashkent is bigger than Samarkand | Tashkent bigga pas Samarkand | tash-KENT BIG-a pas sa-mar-KAND
ex: it is the cheapest | it di chiipis | it dee CHEEP-is
ex: the same as | di siem laik | dee saym like
