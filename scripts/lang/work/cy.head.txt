INTRO: Welsh is spelled very regularly. The stress usually falls on the second-last syllable. The vowels "w" and "y" can be vowels. A word's first letter often changes (a "mutation") after some words, so you will see the same word in two spellings: cath, ei chath, fy nghath.
w | "oo" as in "moon", or "w" before a vowel | cwm
y | "uh" in most words, "ee" in the last syllable | ysgol
u | like "ee" in "see" (north) or a short "i" | un
ll | blow air past the sides of the tongue, an "hl" sound | llaeth
dd | like "th" in "this" | dydd
ch | like "ch" in Scottish "loch" | bach
rh | a breathy "r", like "hr" | rhad
f | like English "v" | afon
ff | like English "f" | ffrind
ae / ai | like "eye" | cae
au | like "eye" or "ay", depending on the region | pethau
NOTE: Welsh has two words for "you": "ti" for a friend or child and "chi" for strangers, elders and groups.
NOTE: Most sentences use "mae" (is / are) in front of the subject: "Mae'r ci yn fawr" (the dog is big). "Dw i" is "I am", short for "rydw i".
