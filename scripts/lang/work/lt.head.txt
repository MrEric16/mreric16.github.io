INTRO: Lithuanian is a Baltic language, one of the oldest living Indo-European languages, closely related to Latvian. It uses the Latin alphabet with extra marks. Vowels with a hook (ą, ę, į, ų) or a bar (ū) are long. The dot over ė also makes a long, closed "e". Stress can fall on any syllable and moves between forms of the same word.
ą ę į ų | long vowels, "ah", "eh", "ee", "oo" | ąžuolas
ė | long, closed "e", like the "ay" in "say" without the glide | ėjau
y | long "ee" (the same as į) | mylėti
č | "ch" as in "church" | čia
š | "sh" as in "ship" | šuo
ž | "s" as in "measure" | žuvis
ie / uo | a glide: "ie" like "ye" in "yet" and "uo" like "wo" in "word" | kiek, šuo
NOTE: Lithuanian has seven cases and no articles. The words change their endings to show their job in the sentence.
NOTE: Marks on vowels (accents) show stress in dictionaries but are not written in normal text. The "say" line shows the usual stress. The pool is unreviewed and a few rare words are best guesses.
