INTRO: Urdu is written right to left in an Arabic-based script. Short vowels are usually not written, so the Latin shown is how it is said. Spoken Urdu is almost the same as spoken Hindi. Every noun is masculine or feminine.
a | a short "u", like "cup" | kitab
aa | a long "ah", like "father" | paani
i | ee, like "bit" | kitab
ee | a long "ee", like "see" | teen
u | oo, like "put" | kuch
oo | a long "oo", like "food" | doosra
kh | like "ch" in Scottish "loch" | khush
gh | a gargled French "r" | ghar
q | a "k" made far back in the throat | qalam
z | like English "z" | zaroorat
dh / bh / ph | aspirated: a puff of air after the sound | dhoop
n | a nasal at the end of a word: the vowel is said through the nose | haan
NOTE: Verbs and adjectives change for a man or a woman: -a for a man, -i for a woman (thaka = tired, man; thaki = tired, woman). This book shows the man's form.
NOTE: The Latin is a common spelling for learners. Urdu has no standard Latin spelling.
