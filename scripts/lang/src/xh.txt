NAME: Xhosa
ID: xh
FLAG: za

[sounds]
INTRO: isiXhosa is a South African Bantu language famous for its three click sounds: c, q and x. Stress falls on the second-last syllable. Every word ends in a vowel.
c | dental click: the "tsk-tsk" of disapproval | cela (CE-la)
q | palatal click: like a cork popping | qonda (QO-nda)
x | lateral click: like calling a horse, "tchick" from the side of the mouth | xola (XO-la)
nc / ngc / gc | the c click with a nasal or voiced start | ncedo (NCE-do), ngcono (NGCO-no), gcina (GCI-na)
nq / ngq / gq | the q click with a nasal or voiced start | nqanda, ngqo, gqirha
nx / ngx / gx | the x click with a nasal or voiced start | nxiba, ngxola, gxotha
hl | breathy "l", like Welsh "ll" | hlala (HLA-la)
dl | voiced version of hl | dlala (DLA-la)
rh | rough, like the Scottish "ch" in loch | rhoqo (RHO-qo)
ny | like "ny" in "canyon" | nyaka (NYA-ka)
tsh | strong "ch" with a puff of air | tshintsha (TSHI-ntsha)
kh / ph / th | aspirated k, p, t with a puff of air | khaya, phi, thetha
b | a soft, sucked-in b (implosive) | bona (BO-na)
NOTE: Nouns belong to classes (like genders). The class decides the prefix, the plural and the agreement on adjectives and verbs.
NOTE: The verb carries the subject: ndi- (I), u- (you / he / she), si- (we), ni- (you all), ba- (they). There are no articles.

## Formal and informal
> Xhosa shows respect with the plural. Use ni- and -ni (Molweni, Ninjani, Nithetha) to an elder or a stranger. Use the singular (Molo, Unjani) to a friend or a child.

## Pronouns
> The verb already shows the subject, so the pronoun is for emphasis. The verb prefixes are: ndi- (I), u- (you, he, she), si- (we), ni- (you all), ba- (they). Xhosa has no gender.
row: I (ndi-) | mna | mna
row: you (u-) | wena | WE-na
row: he / she (u-) | yena | YE-na
row: we (si-) | thina | THI-na
row: you all (ni-) | nina | NI-na
row: they (ba-) | bona | BO-na
ex: I am a student | Ndingumfundi | ndi-ngu-MFU-ndi
ex: you are tall | Ude | U-de
ex: she is my sister | Ngudade wam | ngu-DA-de wam
ex: we are friends | Singabahlobo | si-nga-ba-HLO-bo
ex: they are teachers | Bangootitshala | ba-ngo-o-ti-TSHA-la
ex: I am happy | Ndonwabile | ndo-nwa-BI-le
ex: he is a doctor | Ngugqirha | ngu-GQI-rha
ex: you all are late | Nihlwile | ni-HLWI-le
## Noun classes
> Every noun has a class. The prefix tells you the class and the plural: um- / aba- (people), um- / imi- (trees, things), i- / ama- , isi- / izi- , in- / iin- (or izin-). Adjectives and verbs agree with the class.
row: um- → aba- (people) | umntu → abantu | u-MNTU → a-BA-ntu
row: um- → imi- | umthi → imithi | u-MTHI → i-MI-thi
row: i- → ama- | ikati → amakati | i-KA-ti → a-ma-KA-ti
row: isi- → izi- | isikolo → izikolo | i-si-KO-lo → i-zi-KO-lo
row: in- → iin- / izin- | inja → izinja | I-nja → i-ZI-nja
ex: person, people | umntu, abantu | u-MNTU, a-BA-ntu
ex: tree, trees | umthi, imithi | u-MTHI, i-MI-thi
ex: thing, things | into, izinto | I-nto, i-ZI-nto
ex: language, languages | ulwimi, iilwimi | u-LWI-mi, i-i-LWI-mi
ex: day, days | usuku, iintsuku | u-SU-ku, i-i-NTSU-ku
ex: book, books | incwadi, iincwadi | i-NCWA-di, i-i-NCWA-di
