[sounds]
INTRO: Vietnamese uses the Latin alphabet with many accent marks. It has six tones in the north (the south merges some). The mark shows the tone: ma (mid), má (rising), mà (low falling), mả (dipping), mã (broken rising), mạ (low heavy). The same letters with a different tone are different words. Words are short and never change form.
đ | like a hard English "d" | đi
d | "y" in the north, "z" in the north-west, "y" in the south | dạ
gi | like "z" in the north | giá
x | like "s" | xin
ch | like "ch", at the end of a word like "k" | chào
ng / ngh | like "ng" in "singer", also at the start of a word | nghe
nh | like "ny" in "canyon" | nhà
ă | short "a", like "u" in "cut" | ăn
â | short "uh" sound | cân
ơ | long "uh" sound, like "ur" in "fur" | cơm
ư | "oo" with spread lips | tư
NOTE: Tones matter more than anything else. "Ma" (ghost), "má" (mother or cheek), "mà" (but), "mả" (tomb), "mã" (horse), "mạ" (rice seedling).
NOTE: Politeness is built into the pronouns. Choose by age: anh (older man), chị (older woman), em (younger person), ông (old man), bà (old woman), bạn (friend of the same age).

## Pronouns
> Vietnamese pronouns depend on age and relationship. "Tôi" is a neutral I. "Bạn" is you for a friend of the same age. For older people use kinship words: anh (older brother, any young man), chị (older sister), ông (grandfather, old man), bà (grandmother, old woman). Younger people are "em".
row: I (neutral) | tôi | toy
row: you (friend) | bạn | bahn
row: he | anh ấy | ahn AY
row: she | cô ấy | koh AY
row: we | chúng tôi | choong TOY
row: you (plural) | các bạn | kahk BAHN
row: they | họ | haw
ex: I am a student | tôi là học sinh | toy lah hawk SING
ex: you are tall | bạn cao | bahn KOW
ex: she is my older sister | cô ấy là chị gái của tôi | koh ay lah chee gie koo-ah TOY
ex: we are friends | chúng tôi là bạn | choong toy lah BAHN
ex: they are teachers | họ là giáo viên | haw lah zow VIEN

> Nouns never change. Use "những" or "các" before the noun for a plural: những cuốn sách (the books). After a number, a classifier word goes between the number and the noun.
