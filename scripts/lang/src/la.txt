[sounds]
INTRO: This is classical Latin pronunciation, as spoken in Cicero's time. Latin is spelled almost exactly as it sounds. Modern objects (phone, wifi) use the modern Latin words coined by the Vatican and by schools.
a | ah, like "father" | casa
e | eh, like "bed" | mensa
i | ee, like "see" | via
o | oh, like "more" | domus
u | oo, like "food" | luna
c | always hard "k", never "s" | Caesar (KAI-sar)
g | always hard "g", like "go" | gens
v | "w", like English "w" | vita (WEE-tah)
j / i | "y" at the start of a word before a vowel | iam (yahm)
ae | like "eye" | aqua
au | like "ow" in "cow" | aurum
oe | like "oy" in "boy" | poena
ch, ph, th | k + h, p + h, t + h (breathy, not "f") | pulcher
qu | "kw" | quid
r | rolled, like Italian | rosa
NOTE: Long vowels are held twice as long as short ones. The stress falls on the second-last syllable if it is long, and otherwise on the third-last.
NOTE: Latin has no word for "the" or "a". It has six cases that change the ending of a noun.

## Formal and informal
> Latin has tu (one person) and vos (several). There is no polite "you" for one person in classical Latin. Show respect with domine (sir), domina (madam) and quaeso.

## Comparing things
> Add -ior to the adjective for "more" (maior = bigger). Add -issimus for "most" (maximus = the biggest). Than = "quam". Irregular: bonus, melior, optimus (good, better, best).
