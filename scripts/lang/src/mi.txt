NAME: Māori (Te Reo Māori)
ID: mi
FLAG: nz

[sounds]
INTRO: Māori has five vowels (a, e, i, o, u) and ten consonants (h, k, m, n, ng, p, r, t, w, wh). Every vowel is said, and a bar over a vowel (macron: ā, ē, ī, ō, ū) makes it about twice as long. The macron changes the meaning, so it matters.
a | ah, like "far" | tahi
e | eh, like "bed" | rewera
i | ee, like "see" | rima
o | oh, like "more" | ono
u | oo, like "food" | tūru
ā ē ī ō ū | the same vowel, held about twice as long | whā
wh | most speakers say "f"; some say a soft "w" | whare
ng | like "ng" in "singer", never "g" as in "finger" | ngeru
r | a quick tap of the tongue, between "r" and "d" | rua
NOTE: Words get stress on the first long vowel, or the first syllable if there is none. There are no consonant clusters, so every syllable ends in a vowel.
NOTE: Māori sentences begin with the verb marker. "Kei te" shows something happening now: "Kei te kai ahau" (I am eating). The pool is unreviewed and a few rare words are best guesses.

## Pronouns
> Māori has a dual "we" (two people) and a plural "we" (three or more), and each comes in two kinds: "tāua / tātou" includes the listener, "māua / mātou" excludes the listener. There is no he / she difference: "ia" means both.
row: I | ahau (au) | AH-how (OW)
row: you (one) | koe | KWAY
row: he / she | ia | EE-ah
row: we two (with you) | tāua | TAH-oo-ah
row: we (with you) | tātou | TAH-toh
row: we (without you) | mātou | MAH-toh
row: you (plural) | koutou | KOH-toh
row: they | rātou | RAH-toh
ex: I am a student | he akonga ahau | heh ah-KOH-ngah AH-how
ex: you are tall | kei te teitei koe | kay teh TAY-tay KWAY
ex: she is my older sister | ko tōku tuakana wahine ia | koh TOH-koo too-ah-KAH-nah wah-HEE-neh EE-ah
ex: we are friends | he hoa tātou | heh HOH-ah TAH-toh
ex: they are teachers | he kaiako rātou | heh kie-AH-koh RAH-toh
## Singular and plural
> Nouns do not change. Use "te" (the) for one and "ngā" (the) for many: te pukapuka (the book), ngā pukapuka (the books). Use "ētahi" for "some": ētahi pukapuka.
row: the book | te pukapuka | teh poo-kah-POO-kah
row: the books | ngā pukapuka | ngah poo-kah-POO-kah
row: the child | te tamaiti | teh tah-MIE-tee
row: the children | ngā tamariki | ngah tah-mah-REE-kee
ex: one house, houses | kotahi te whare, ngā whare | KOH-tah-hee teh FAH-reh, ngah FAH-reh
ex: one book, books | kotahi te pukapuka, ngā pukapuka | KOH-tah-hee teh poo-kah-POO-kah, ngah poo-kah-POO-kah
ex: one cat, cats | kotahi te ngeru, ngā ngeru | KOH-tah-hee teh NGEH-roo, ngah NGEH-roo
ex: two books | e rua ngā pukapuka | eh ROO-ah ngah poo-kah-POO-kah
ex: the children | ngā tamariki | ngah tah-mah-REE-kee
## To be: he, ko, kei
> Māori has no verb "to be". Use "he" before a noun (he kaiako ahau, a teacher I), "ko" for a name or identity, and "kei" for places (kei te kura ahau). An adjective can stand first with "kei te".

## Having: he ... tāku
> To say "I have", Māori says "a ... mine": he motokā tāku (a car mine). Use "tōku" for things that are part of you, like family and body (o-class), and "tāku" for things you own or do (a-class).

## Asking questions
> Raise your voice at the end, or begin with "he": "He pai tēnei?" (Is this good?). To answer, say "āe" (yes) or "kao" (no), or repeat the verb.
row: Do you go? | kei te haere koe? | kay teh HIGH-reh KWAY
row: Is it good? | he pai? | heh PIE
row: Is it right? | he tika? | heh TEE-kah
ex: is this your book? | nāu tēnei pukapuka? | NAH-oo TEH-nay poo-kah-POO-kah

## Possessives and liking
> Two kinds of "my": "tōku" (parts of you, family, home) and "taku" (things you own or choose, like a pencil or a plan). Your: tō / tāu. His, her: tōna / tāna. Their: tō rātou / ā rātou.
row: my body (o-class) | tōku tinana | TOH-koo tee-NAH-nah

## Formal vs informal
> Māori shows respect with "koutou" (you, plural) for one person, with "e te rangatira" (sir, chief) and with "tēnā koa" (please). There is no formal verb form.
row: sir | e te rangatira | eh teh rah-ngah-TEE-rah
row: madam | e kui | eh KOO-ee
ex: how are you? (informal) | kei te pēhea koe? | kay teh PEH-heh-ah KWAY

ex: where are you going? (informal) | kei te haere koe ki hea? | kay teh HIGH-reh KWAY kee HEH-ah
ex: where are you going? (formal) | kei te haere koutou ki hea? | kay teh HIGH-reh KOH-toh kee HEH-ah
ex: please sit down (formal) | noho, tēnā koa | NOH-hoh, TEH-nah KOH-ah
## Past and future (simple)
> Verbs do not change. Past: put "i" before the verb (i kai ahau, I ate). Future: put "ka" before the verb (ka kai ahau, I will eat).
row: I ate | i kai ahau | ee KIE AH-how
row: I will eat | ka kai ahau | kah KIE AH-how
row: I will go tomorrow | ka haere ahau āpōpō | kah HIGH-reh AH-how ah-poh-POH

## Can, must, want
> "Ka taea" (can), "me" (must) and "hiahia" (want) are the key words. Ka taea e au te... (it is possible for me to...), me haere ahau (I must go).

## In, on, at

## Comparing things
> Māori adds "ake" for "more": nui ake (bigger), pai ake (better). For "than", use "i": pai ake tēnei i tērā. For "the most", use "rawa atu": nui rawa atu.

row: the biggest | nui rawa atu | NOO-ee RAH-wah AH-too

ex: small, smaller, the smallest | iti, iti ake, iti rawa atu | EE-tee, EE-tee AH-keh, EE-tee RAH-wah AH-too

ex: she is taller than me | teitei ake ia i ahau | TAY-tay AH-keh EE-ah ee AH-how
ex: Tashkent is bigger than Samarkand | nui ake a Tashkent i a Samarkand | NOO-ee AH-keh ah tash-KENT ee ah sa-mar-KAND
ex: it is the cheapest | ko tēnei te mea utu iti rawa atu | koh TEH-nay teh MEH-ah OO-too EE-tee RAH-wah AH-too
