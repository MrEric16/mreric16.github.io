[sounds]
INTRO: Uzbek is written in the Latin alphabet and is spelled almost exactly as it sounds. There are no genders and no articles. The stress is usually on the last syllable. Learn the special letters first.
a | ah, between "cat" and "father" | ota
e | eh, like "bed" | eshik
i | a short "ih", like "sit" | ikki
o | a rounded "o", like British "hot" | olma
o' | oh, like "more" | o'g'il
u | oo, like "food" | uy
g' | gh, like a gargled French "r" | g'oya
q | a "k" made far back in the throat | qo'y
x | kh, like "ch" in Scottish "loch" | xat
h | breathy h, always pronounced | hafta
sh | sh, like "ship" | shahar
ch | ch, like "church" | choy
ng | like "ng" in "sing" | tong
j | j, like "jam" | jon
y | y, like English "y" | yil
NOTE: In o' and g' the apostrophe is part of the letter. It is not a pause.
NOTE: Say stands for "how to say it". Capitals mark the stressed syllable.
