[sounds]
INTRO: Chagatai is the classical literary language of Central Asia, the language of Navoi and Babur. It is the ancestor of modern Uzbek and Uyghur. It is written right to left in the Perso-Arabic script. The Latin shown follows the usual Turkological spelling. No one speaks it today, so the sounds are reconstructed.
a / ä | back "a" (ah) and front "ä" (like "a" in "cat") | at, käl
ı / i | ı is a short, flat "uh" in the back of the mouth, i is "ee" | qız, kiši
o / u | back rounded vowels | oq, uč
ö / ü | front rounded vowels, as in German "ö" and "ü" | köz, kün
q | a "k" made far back in the throat, in back-vowel words | qız
k | a plain "k", in front-vowel words | kün
ğ / g | a soft, throaty "gh" and a hard "g" | tağ, gül
ng | like "ng" in "sing" (it has its own letter) | täng
č / š | "ch" and "sh" | čay, kiši
NOTE: Vowel harmony: back vowels (a, ı, o, u) go with q and ğ. Front vowels (ä, i, ö, ü) go with k and g. Suffixes follow the same rule.
NOTE: The script writes only some vowels, so the same letters can be read several ways. The script shown is a guide to the spelling, not a standard.
NOTE: This pool is much smaller than the Spanish one on purpose. Only well-attested forms are included, and they have not been checked by a native reader. Treat it as a first guide.

[grammar]
## Pronouns
> Chagatai has no gender. "Ol" means he, she and it. "Siz" is polite.
row: I | män | من
row: you (informal) | sän | سن
row: you (formal) | siz | سز
row: he / she / it | ol | اول
row: we | biz | بز
row: you (plural) | siz | سز
row: they | alar | آلار
ex: I | män | | من
ex: you | sän | | سن
ex: he | ol | | اول
ex: we | biz | | بز
ex: they | alar | | آلار
## Singular and plural
> The plural is -lar or -lär by vowel harmony. After a number the noun stays singular.
row: -lar (back vowels) | kitāb → kitāblar | کتاب → کتابلر
row: -lär (front vowels) | kiši → kišilär | کیشی → کیشیلر
ex: the book | kitāb | | کتاب
ex: the books | kitāblar | | کتابلر
ex: the person | kiši | | کیشی
ex: the people | kišilär | | کیشیلر
ex: two books | ikki kitāb | | ایکی کتاب
## Negation
> "Yoq" means "there is not". On a verb, -ma- or -mä- is put after the stem: käldi (he came), kälmädi (he did not come). Always compare with the positive form.
row: there is not | yoq | یوق
row: -ma- / -mä- | käl → kälmä | کل → کلمه
ex: there is a book | kitāb bar | | کتاب بار
ex: there is no book | kitāb yoq | | کتاب یوق
ex: he came | käldi | | کلدی
ex: he did not come | kälmädi | | کلمدی
ex: I came | käldim | | کلدیم
ex: I did not come | kälmädim | | کلمدیم

## Past tense
> The past ending is -dı / -di by vowel harmony, then a personal ending: -m (I), -ng (you), none (he), -k (we).
row: I came | käldim | کلدیم
row: you came | käldıng | کلدینگ
row: he came | käldi | کلدی
row: we came | käldik | کلدیک
ex: I saw | kördüm | | کوردوم
ex: he saw | kördi | | کوردی
ex: I wrote | yazdım | | یازدیم

## Possession and cases
