INTRO: Jamaican Patois (Patwa) is an English-based creole spoken by almost everyone in Jamaica. It has no single official spelling. This pack uses a simple, widely used spelling close to the Cassidy-JLU system, with a plain-English respelling in the "say" line. It is the language of reggae, Bob Marley and Jamaican street life, and it is a language, not "broken English".
th | "t" or "d": "think" is "tink", "this" is "dis" | tink
h | often dropped: "hand" is "han" | han
-ing | the ending becomes "-in" or is dropped: "going" is "a go" | a go
w / y | "wah" for "what", "weh" for "where" | wah
NOTE: Jamaican Patois is spoken in a continuum from near-standard English to heavy dialect. This pack is the middle and heavier end. Spellings vary a lot between writers.
NOTE: This pool is much smaller than the Spanish one on purpose and has not been checked by a native speaker.

[grammar]
## Pronouns
> Patois pronouns do not change for subject or object, except he/she/it (im, shi, it). Plural is "dem" after the noun: "di pickney dem" (the children).
row: I / me | mi | mee
row: you | yuh | yuh
row: he / she / him / her | im / shi | eem / shee
row: we | wi | wee
row: they | dem | dem
ex: I am a student | Mi a student | mee a STOO-dent
ex: we are friends | Wi a fren dem | wee a fren dem
ex: they are teachers | Dem a tiicha dem | dem a TEE-cha dem
## Verbs and tenses
> No -ing and no past endings. "A" marks something happening now: "Mi a go". "Did" marks past: "Mi did go". "Go" marks future: "Mi a go kom" (I will come).
row: I go | Mi go | mee go
row: I am going | Mi a go | mee a go
row: I went | Mi did go | mee did go
ex: I eat | Mi nyam | mee nyam
ex: I am eating | Mi a nyam | mee a nyam
ex: I ate | Mi did nyam | mee did nyam
## Negation
> Put "nuh" before the verb or noun. For the past, use "neva". For "can't", use "cyaan".
row: not | nuh | nuh
row: never / did not | neva | NEV-a
row: can't | cyaan | chan
ex: I know | Mi know | mee noh
ex: I don't know | Mi nuh know | mee nuh noh
ex: I saw him | Mi si im | mee see eem
ex: I did not see him | Mi neva si im | mee NEV-a see eem
