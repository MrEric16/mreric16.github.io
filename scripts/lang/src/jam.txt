[sounds]
INTRO: Jamaican Patois (Patwa) is an English-based creole spoken by almost everyone in Jamaica. It has no single official spelling. This pack uses a simple, widely used spelling close to the Cassidy-JLU system, with a plain-English respelling in the "say" line. It is the language of reggae, Bob Marley and Jamaican street life, and it is a language, not "broken English".
th | "t" or "d": "think" is "tink", "this" is "dis" | tink
h | often dropped: "hand" is "han" | han
-ing | the ending becomes "-in" or is dropped: "going" is "a go" | a go
w / y | "wah" for "what", "weh" for "where" | wah
NOTE: Jamaican Patois is spoken in a continuum from near-standard English to heavy dialect. This pack is the middle and heavier end. Spellings vary a lot between writers.
NOTE: The pool is unreviewed by a native speaker. Some school and university words are the English word said in a Jamaican way.

## Pronouns
> Patois pronouns do not change for subject or object. "Mi" is both I and me, and "wi" is we and us. "Unu" (or "yuh") can mean you (plural). Only he / she / it have forms: im, shi, it. The plural is "dem".
row: I / me | mi | mee
row: you (one) | yuh | yuh
row: he / him | im | eem
row: she / her | shi | shee
row: we / us | wi | wee
row: you (plural) | unu | OO-noo
row: they / them | dem | dem
ex: I am a student | mi a student | mee a STOO-dent
ex: you are tall | yuh tall | yuh tawl
ex: she is my older sister | shi a mi big sista | shee a mee big SIS-ta
ex: we are friends | wi a fren dem | wee a fren dem
ex: they are teachers | dem a tiicha dem | dem a TEE-cha dem
## Singular and plural
> Nouns do not change. Add "dem" after the noun for many: di buk dem (the books). Use a number and the noun stays the same: tuu buk.
row: the book | di buk | dee buk
row: the books | di buk dem | dee buk dem
row: the child | di pickney | dee PIK-nee
row: the children | di pickney dem | dee PIK-nee dem
ex: one house, houses | wan hous, hous dem | wun hows, hows dem
ex: one book, books | wan buk, buk dem | wun buk, buk dem
ex: one cat, cats | wan kyat, kyat dem | wun kyat, kyat dem
ex: two books | tuu buk | too buk
ex: the children | di pickney dem | dee PIK-nee dem
## To be: a, deh, ier
> Patois has no verb "to be". "A" links two nouns: mi a tiicha (I am a teacher). Adjectives stand on their own: shi tall. "Deh" says where: mi deh a skuul (I am at school).
row: I am a teacher | mi a tiicha | mee a TEE-cha
row: this is a book | dis a buk | dis a buk
row: I am at home | mi deh a yaad | mee deh a yard
row: I am tall | mi tall | mee tawl
ex: I am a student | mi a student | mee a STOO-dent
ex: you are tired | yuh tai'd | yuh tied
ex: he is a doctor | im a daktar | eem a DOK-ta
ex: we are happy | wi hapi | wee HAP-ee
ex: they are at home | dem deh a yaad | dem deh a yard
## Having: mi av
> "Av" (or "hav") means have: mi av a kyar (I have a car). Say "mi nuh av" for I do not have. "Deh" with "a" can also say "there is": a deh wan problem.
row: I have a car | mi av a kyar | mee av a kyar
row: you have a car | yuh av a kyar | yuh av a kyar
row: he / she has a car | im av a kyar | eem av a kyar
row: I do not have | mi nuh av | mee nuh av
ex: I have an older brother | mi av wan big breda | mee av wun big BRED-a
ex: I have a car | mi av a kyar | mee av a kyar
ex: I do not have time | mi nuh av taim | mee nuh av time
ex: there is a problem | wan problem deh | wun PROB-lem deh
ex: there is no water | nuh wata nuh deh | nuh WAH-ta nuh deh
## Present tense

## Asking questions

## Formal vs informal
> Patois has no formal verb form. Show respect with "sa" (sir) or "ma'am", and by speaking in a gentler, more English way.
row: sir | sa | sah
row: madam | ma'am | mam
ex: how are you? (informal) | ow yuh du? | ow yuh DOO
ex: how are you? (formal) | ow yuh du, sa? | ow yuh DOO sah
ex: where are you going? (informal) | weh yuh a go? | weh yuh a go
ex: where are you going? (formal) | weh yuh a go, sa? | weh yuh a go sah
ex: please sit down (formal) | pliiz, sit dong, sa | pleez sit dong sah
## Past and future (simple)
