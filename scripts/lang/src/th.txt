[sounds]
INTRO: Thai has five tones: mid, low, falling, high and rising. The same syllable changes meaning with the tone. Here the tones are not marked, so listen to a native speaker. The Latin line follows the Royal Thai General System, which is simple but ignores tones.
kh / th / ph | breathy "k", "t", "p", with a puff of air | khao
k / t / p | plain "g", "d", "b", no puff | kin
ng | like "ng" in "singer", also at the start of a word | ngan
ao | like "ow" in "cow" | khao
ai | like "eye" | chai
oe | like "ur" in "fur" | choe
ue | an "uh" sound with lips spread | mue
NOTE: Thai has no spaces between words. Spaces mark the end of a phrase or sentence.
NOTE: Men end polite sentences with "khrap" (ครับ) and women with "kha" (ค่ะ) or "kha?" (คะ) for questions.

## Pronouns
> Thai has many pronouns, and the choice shows politeness and gender. Men say "phom" for I and women say "dichan" (formal) or "chan" (informal). Friends often use their names instead.
row: I (man) | phom | ผม
row: I (woman, formal) | dichan | ดิฉัน
row: I (informal) | chan | ฉัน
row: you | khun | คุณ
row: he / she | khao | เขา
row: we | rao | เรา
row: they | phuak khao | พวกเขา
ex: I am a student | chan pen nakrian | chan pen nak-RIAN | ฉันเป็นนักเรียน
ex: you are tall | khun sung | koon SOONG | คุณสูง
ex: she is my older sister | thoe pen phi sao khong chan | tur pen pee sow kong CHAN | เธอเป็นพี่สาวของฉัน
ex: we are friends | rao pen phuean kan | row pen peuan GAN | เราเป็นเพื่อนกัน

> Thai nouns do not change for the plural. Context or a number shows how many. With a number, a classifier word goes between the number and the noun, such as "lem" for books.
row: book (one or many) | nangsue | หนังสือ
row: two books | nangsue song lem | หนังสือสองเล่ม
row: house | ban | บ้าน

ex: the children | dek dek | dek dek | เด็ก ๆ
## To be: pen, khue, yu
> Thai has no single verb "to be". Use "pen" before a job or role, "khue" before a noun that equals another, "yu" for a place, and nothing at all before an adjective.

## Having: mi

## Formal vs informal
