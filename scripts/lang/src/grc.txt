INTRO: This is Attic Greek of the 5th and 4th centuries BC, the language of Plato, Sophocles and Thucydides. It is written in the Greek alphabet. The Latin line is a simple transliteration and the third line gives a reconstructed classical pronunciation. Nobody speaks it, so the pronunciation is a scholars' reconstruction.
a | ah, like "father" | ἀγορά (agora)
e / ē | "e" in "bed" / a long "ay" | ἐγώ / ἦν
o / ō | "o" in "more" / a long "aw" | ἄνθρωπος / ὦ
u | like French "u" or German "ü" | ὕδωρ (hydor)
ph / th / kh | p, t, k with a puff of air | φίλος, θεός, χορός
ei | a long "ay", like "bay" | εἶναι
ou | like "oo" in "food" | οὐ
NOTE: Ancient Greek has marks above the vowels: breathings (᾿ and ῾) and accents (´ ` ῀). A rough breathing (῾) means an "h" sound at the start of a word: ὁ (ho).
NOTE: This pool is much smaller than the Spanish one on purpose. Only well-attested words and forms are included. Nothing has been checked by a classicist, so treat the accents as a first guide.

[grammar]
## Pronouns
> Greek verbs already show the person, so the pronoun is added only to stress it. The article agrees with the noun in gender: ho (m), he (f), to (n).
row: I | ego | ἐγώ
row: you (one) | sy | σύ
row: he / she / it | autos / aute / auto | αὐτός / αὐτή / αὐτό
row: we | hemeis | ἡμεῖς
row: you (plural) | hymeis | ὑμεῖς
row: they | autoi | αὐτοί
ex: I am a student | ego mathetes eimi | eh-GAW mah-the-TAYS eh-MEE | ἐγὼ μαθητής εἰμι
ex: you are wise | sy sophos ei | SEW so-PHOS AY | σὺ σοφὸς εἶ
ex: he is a teacher | autos didaskalos esti | ow-TOS dee-DAS-kah-los ES-tee | αὐτὸς διδάσκαλός ἐστι
ex: we are friends | philoi esmen | PHEE-loy ES-men | φίλοι ἐσμέν
ex: they are men | andres eisi | AN-dres eh-SEE | ἄνδρες εἰσί
## To be: eimi
> The verb "to be" is irregular: eimi, ei, esti, esmen, este, eisi. The verb often comes after the noun, as in "mathetes eimi".
row: I am | eimi | εἰμί
row: you are | ei | εἶ
row: he / she is | esti | ἐστί
row: we are | esmen | ἐσμέν
row: you are (plural) | este | ἐστέ
row: they are | eisi | εἰσί
ex: I am a man | aner eimi | ah-NAIR eh-MEE | ἀνήρ εἰμι
ex: you are good | agathos ei | ah-gah-THOS AY | ἀγαθὸς εἶ
ex: he is in the house | en to oiko esti | en toh OY-koh ES-tee | ἐν τῷ οἴκῳ ἐστί
ex: we are in the city | en te polei esmen | en tay POH-lay ES-men | ἐν τῇ πόλει ἐσμέν
## Negation
> Put "ou" before the verb to deny a fact. "Ouk" is used before a vowel. "Me" is used for commands, wishes and conditions.
row: not (fact) | ou / ouk | οὐ / οὐκ
row: not (command or wish) | me | μή
ex: I know | oida | OY-dah | οἶδα
ex: I do not know | ouk oida | OOK OY-dah | οὐκ οἶδα
ex: he is good | agathos esti | ah-gah-THOS ES-tee | ἀγαθός ἐστι
ex: he is not good | ouk agathos esti | OOK ah-gah-THOS ES-tee | οὐκ ἀγαθός ἐστι
ex: do not say | me lege | MAY LEH-geh | μὴ λέγε
## Commands
> The second-person singular command is usually the bare stem: lege (say!), grapse (write!). Plural adds -ete: legete.
row: say! | lege | λέγε
row: write! | grapse | γράψον
row: come! | deuro | δεῦρο
ex: come here | deuro | DEH-oo-ro | δεῦρο
ex: say | lege | LEH-geh | λέγε
ex: listen | akoue | ah-KOO-eh | ἄκουε
ex: wait | mene | MEH-neh | μένε
## Questions
> Add "ara" at the start for a yes or no question, or use a question word.
row: Is it...? | ara | ἆρα
ex: is he a teacher? | ara didaskalos esti | AH-rah dee-DAS-kah-los ES-tee | ἆρα διδάσκαλός ἐστι;
