[sounds]
INTRO: Czech is written in the Latin alphabet with accents. It is spelled almost exactly as it sounds. Nouns have three genders and seven cases. The stress is always on the first syllable of a word.
č | ch, like "church" | čaj
š | sh, like "ship" | škola
ž | like "s" in "measure" | žena
ř | roll an "r" and say "zh" at the same time, a sound only Czech has | řeka
ě | ye, like "ye" in "yes" | město
c | ts, like "ts" in "cats" | cukr
ch | like "ch" in Scottish "loch" | chléb
á / é / í / ó / ú / ů / ý | the same vowel, said longer | máma
ď / ť / ň | a soft "d", "t" or "n" with the tongue pressed to the palate | ťukat
NOTE: Czech has long and short vowels. A long vowel has an accent (á, é, í, ó, ú, ý) or a ring (ů). Length changes the word.
NOTE: Some words have no vowel. "r" and "l" can be a vowel: krk (throat), vlk (wolf).

## Pronouns
> Czech often drops the pronoun, because the verb ending shows who is speaking. "On" is he, "ona" is she, "ono" is it. "Ty" is informal and "vy" is formal.
row: I | já | yah
row: you (informal) | ty | ti
row: he / she / it | on / ona / ono | on / O-nah / O-no
row: we | my | mi
row: you (formal or plural) | vy | vi
row: they | oni | O-nyi
ex: I am a student | jsem student | yem STOO-dent
ex: you are tall | jsi vysoký | ysi VI-so-kee
ex: she is my older sister | je to moje starší sestra | yeh to MO-yeh STAR-shee SES-trah
ex: we are friends | jsme přátelé | ysmeh PRZHAH-teh-leh
ex: they are teachers | jsou učitelé | ysoh OO-chi-teh-leh
## Singular and plural
> The ending changes with gender. Feminine -a becomes -y: kniha, knihy. Masculine often adds -y or -i: dům, domy. Neuter -o becomes -a: město, města. After 5 and higher, the noun takes a different form.

row: dítě (child) | děti | DYEH-tyi
