INTRO: Classical (Written) Mongolian is written in the traditional script: top to bottom, with the lines running left to right. It was the written language of the Mongol Empire and is still used in Inner Mongolia. The Latin shown is the scholars' transliteration, which keeps the classical spelling and the sounds that spoken Khalkha has lost.
a / e | back and front "a" (a = ah, e = eh) | ger
o / u | back rounded vowels (o = oh, u = oo) | nom
ö / ü | front rounded vowels, as in German "ö" and "ü" | öglüge
γ | a soft, throaty "gh" sound, used in back-vowel words | γal
g | a hard "g", used in front-vowel words | ger
q | a hard "kh" made at the back of the mouth, in back-vowel words | qara
k | the same letter as q, but said as "kh" in front-vowel words | kümün
š / č / j | "sh", "ch" and "j" | baγši, čai, jaγun
ng | like "ng" in "sing" | mingγan
y / w | "y" and "w" glides | yeke, dawa
- | a hyphen shows where a suffix attaches. It is not written in the script | bayin-a
NOTE: Vowel harmony: the back vowels a, o, u go with q and γ. The front vowels e, ö, ü go with k and g. The vowel i is neutral. Suffixes follow the same rule.
NOTE: The letters change shape at the start, in the middle and at the end of a word, and the spelling follows tradition rather than sound. The script needs a Mongolian font, which this app includes.
NOTE: This pool is smaller than the Spanish one on purpose. Only forms that could be reconstructed with confidence are included, and the forms have not been checked by a native reader of the script. Treat it as a first guide.

[grammar]
## Pronouns
> Mongolian has no gender. "Tere" means he, she and it. Use "ta" for one person you respect.
row: I | bi
row: you (informal) | či
row: you (formal) | ta
row: he / she / it | tere
row: we | bide
row: you (plural) | ta nar
row: they | tede
ex: I am a student | bi surγaγči bui
ex: you are tall | či öndür bui
ex: he is a teacher | tere baγši mön
ex: we are friends | bide nayiji nar bui
ex: they are teachers | tede baγši nar bui
ex: she is my elder sister | tere minu egeči mön
## Singular and plural
> The plural is -ud / -üd by vowel harmony (nom → nom-ud, ger → ger-üd). For people "nar" is common: baγši nar. After a number the noun stays singular.
row: -ud (back vowels) | nom → nom-ud
row: -üd (front vowels) | ger → ger-üd
row: nar (people) | baγši → baγši nar
row: after a number | qoyar nom
ex: the books | nom-ud
ex: the houses | ger-üd
ex: the teachers | baγši nar
ex: two books | qoyar nom
ex: many people | olan kümün
## Negation
> "Ügei" means "not" or "there is none". "Biši" means "is not" (for nouns). A verb in -qu takes "ügei": yabuqu ügei (will not go). Always compare with the positive form.
row: there is none | ügei
row: is not | biši
row: will not | -qu ügei
ex: there is a book | nom bui
ex: there is no book | nom ügei
ex: I have a house | minu ger bui
ex: I do not have a house | minu ger ügei
ex: he is a teacher | tere baγši mön
ex: he is not a teacher | tere baγši biši
ex: I will go | bi yabuqu
ex: I will not go | bi yabuqu ügei
## Asking questions
> A yes / no question ends in "uu" (back vowel) or "üü" (front vowel). The word order does not change.
row: after a back vowel | uu
row: after a front vowel | üü
ex: is it a book? | ene nom uu
ex: is he a teacher? | tere baγši uu
ex: is it big? | yeke üü
ex: do you have a book? | tanu nom bui uu
