NAME: Korean
ID: ko
BADGE: Solid
NOTE: Written in Latin letters (no Hangul). Standard polite speech (-yo style), as spoken in Seoul. Capitals mark the stress; Korean stress is light, so say every syllable evenly.

[sounds]
INTRO: Korean has no tones. These are the sounds that are new for English speakers.
a | ah, like "father" | sa-ram (person)
eo | "uh", like "up" said with an open mouth | eo-di (where)
o | oh | no-rae (song)
u | oo, like "food" | u-yu (milk)
eu | "uh", lips flat (no English match) | eum-sik (food)
i | ee, like "see" | i-reum (name)
ae | like "bed" | ae-gi (baby)
e | like "bed" (same as ae now) | ne (yes)
oe / we | "weh" | oe-guk (foreign country)
wi | "wee" | wi (up)
g / k | between g and k, soft | gi-cha (train)
kk | tense k, no puff of air | kko-ri (tail)
n, m | as in English | nara, mul
d / t | between d and t, soft | dal (moon)
tt | tense t | ttal (daughter)
b / p | between b and p, soft | bap (rice)
pp | tense p | ppang (bread)
s | s; ss is tense s | sa, ssa
j | between j and ch, soft | jip (house)
jj | tense j | jjim (stew)
ch | ch with a puff of air | chin-gu (friend)
r / l | between r and l; "r" before a vowel, "l" at the end | ri-bon, mul
ng | as in "sing"; at the end of a syllable only | ban-gu
NOTE: Each syllable is a unit. Final consonants are said short and unreleased: "bap" has no puff at the end.
NOTE: Korean puts the verb at the END of the sentence. Particles mark the role of each word (eun/neun = topic, i/ga = subject, eul/reul = object).

## Formal and informal
> Three levels: formal (-mnida / -seumnida): news, business, meeting strangers. Polite (-yo): everyday, shops, adults you do not know well. Informal (drop the -yo): close friends of the same age, younger people, children. Use -yo when unsure.

## Pronouns
> Korean often drops pronouns. For "you" it is better to use the person's name or title + -ssi, because "dangsin" can sound rude or intimate. 
row: I (polite) | jeo | juh
row: I (informal) | na | nah
row: you (polite) | dangsin | DAHNG-sheen
row: you (informal) | neo | nuh
row: he | geu | geu
row: she | geunyeo | GEU-nyuh
row: we (polite) | jeohui | JUH-hwee
row: we (informal) | uri | OO-ree
row: they | geudeul | GEU-deul
ex: I am a student | jeo-neun haksaeng-ieyo | juh-neun hahk-saeng-ee-eh-yoh
ex: you are tall | ki-ga keoyo | kee-gah kuh-yoh

ex: they are teachers | geudeul-eun seonsaengnim-ieyo | GEU-deu-reun suhn-saeng-neem-ee-eh-yoh
## Singular and plural
> Korean usually does not mark the plural. Add -deul to show "many" (chingu → chingudeul). Numbers and context show how many.
row: friend | chingu | CHEEN-goo
row: friends | chingudeul | CHEEN-goo-deul
row: student | haksaeng | HAHK-saeng
row: students | haksaengdeul | HAHK-saeng-deul
row: counter for things | gae | gae
row: counter for people | myeong | myuhng
ex: one friend, friends | chingu, chingudeul | CHEEN-goo CHEEN-goo-deul
ex: one student, students | haksaeng, haksaengdeul | HAHK-saeng HAHK-saeng-deul
ex: two people | dumyeong | doo-myuhng
ex: three apples | sagwa se gae | SAH-gwah seh gae
ex: five books | chaek da-seot gwon | chaek DAH-suht gwuhn

## Regular verbs (present tense)
> Take the verb stem (drop -da), add -ayo or -eoyo (a / o vowel → -ayo, other vowels → -eoyo). Gada (go) → gayo, meokda (eat) → meogeoyo. Hada becomes haeyo.
row: gada (go) | gayo | gah-yoh
row: meokda (eat) | meogeoyo | muh-guh-yoh
row: hada (do) | haeyo | hae-yoh
row: ilgda (read) | ilgeoyo | il-guh-yoh
row: masida (drink) | masyeoyo | mah-shyuh-yoh
row: junbi-hada (prepare) | junbi-haeyo | joon-bee-hae-yoh
ex: I work in a bank | jeo-neun eunhaeng-eseo ilhaeyo | juh-neun eun-haeng-eh-suh eel-hae-yoh
ex: you live in Seoul | seoul-e saseyo | suh-oo-leh sah-seh-yoh

## Basic sentences
> Word order is subject, object, verb (verb last). Particles mark roles: -eun/-neun (topic), -i/-ga (subject), -eul/-reul (object).
row: subject + object + verb | Jeo-neun bap-eul meogeoyo. | juh-neun bah-beul muh-guh-yoh
row: adjective before noun | keun jip | keun jeep
row: add time or place | Jeo-neun oneul yeogi-eseo ilhaeyo. | juh-neun oh-neul yuh-gee-eh-suh eel-hae-yoh

## Negation
> Put an before the verb (short form) or add -ji anayo to the stem (long form). Never use "an" with "isseoyo": use "eopseoyo".
row: not (short) | an + verb | ahn
row: not (long) | stem + -ji anayo | jee ah-nah-yoh
row: nothing | amugeotdo | ah-moo-guht-doh
row: nobody | amudo | ah-moo-doh
row: never | jeoldae ...ji anayo | juhl-dae
ex: I do not speak French | peurangseueo-reul an haeyo | peu-rahng-seu-uh-reul ahn hae-yoh
ex: she does not live here | geunyeo-neun yeogi an sarayo | GEU-nyuh-neun yuh-gee ahn sah-rah-yoh
ex: I do not want anything | amugeotdo wonhaji anayo | ah-moo-guht-doh wuhn-hah-jee ah-nah-yoh
ex: nobody is here | yeogi amudo eopseoyo | yuh-gee ah-moo-doh uhp-suh-yoh
ex: I never eat meat | jeoldae gogi-reul meokji anayo | juhl-dae goh-gee-reul muhk-jee ah-nah-yoh

## Formal vs informal
> Add -nim (title) to teachers and elders. Use -ssi after a name for equals: Minsu-ssi. Do not use -ssi to older people. Always speak politely to adults.
row: polite ending | -yo | yoh
row: formal ending | -mnida | mnee-dah
row: informal ending | (no ending) | -
row: respectful title | -nim | neem
row: after a name | -ssi | shee
ex: hello (formal) | annyeonghasimnikka | ahn-NYUNG-hah-seem-nee-kah
ex: hello (polite) | annyeonghaseyo | ahn-NYUNG-hah-seh-yoh
ex: hello (informal) | annyeong | ahn-NYUNG
ex: teacher (respectful) | seonsaengnim | SUHN-saeng-neem
