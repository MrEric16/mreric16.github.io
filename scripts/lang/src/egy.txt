[sounds]
INTRO: This is Middle Egyptian, the classical language of the pharaohs around 2000 to 1300 BC. It was written in hieroglyphs, which show only consonants. Nobody knows how the vowels sounded, so Egyptologists put an "e" between consonants to make words speakable. The Latin line uses the scholars' transliteration. The pronunciation is therefore a convention, not a real sound. No hieroglyph script is shown here because many devices cannot display it.
ꜣ | the Egyptian "aleph", said as a soft "a" by convention | nꜣ
j | said as "i" or "y" | jt
ꜥ | the Egyptian "ayin", a throaty "a" | ꜥnḫ
w | "w" or "oo" | wꜥ
b / p / f / m / n / r / h / ḥ | like in English; ḥ is a stronger "h" | ḥtp
ḫ / ẖ | like "ch" in Scottish "loch" | ḫpr
s / š | "s" and "sh" | sš
k / q / g | "k", a deeper "k", and "g" | qd
t / ṯ / d / ḏ | "t", "ch" as in "church", "d", and "dj" | ḏd
NOTE: Hieroglyphs were also written as ideas: a picture of a house means "house" (pr). The sign for a word is often followed by a picture that shows its kind, such as a man or a book roll.
NOTE: This pool is much smaller than the Spanish one on purpose. The vowels are conventions of modern Egyptologists, and nothing has been checked by an Egyptologist. Treat the forms as a first guide.

[grammar]
## Pronouns
> Egyptian has the independent pronouns (jnk, ntk, ntf) for statements about who someone is. For "he", "she", "it" and "they" it also has suffix pronouns attached to words: .f (his), .s (her), .j (my), .k (your), .sn (their).
row: I | jnk | EE-nek
row: you (m) | ntk | EN-tek
row: you (f) | nṯ | ench
row: he | ntf | EN-tef
row: she | nts | EN-tes
row: we | jnn | EE-nen
row: they | ntsn | EN-tesen
ex: I am a scribe | jnk sš | EE-nek sesh
ex: you are a king | ntk nsw | EN-tek NEH-soo
ex: he is a man | ntf z | EN-tef sah
ex: she is a woman | nts ḥmt | EN-tes HEM-et
ex: they are scribes | ntsn sšw | EN-tesen SESH-oo
## Sentences with no verb
> Egyptian says "is" by putting two words side by side: "nfr pw" (good it-is) means "it is good". The little word "pw" means "this" or "it is".
row: it is good | nfr pw | NEH-fer poo
row: the king is great | nsw wr | NEH-soo wer
ex: the house is big | pr wr | per wer
ex: the boy is small | ẖrd nds | KHE-red NEH-dess
ex: the heart is good | jb nfr | eeb NEH-fer
## Negation
> Put "n" before the verb or noun to deny it: "n rḫ.j" (I do not know). "Nn" means "there is not".
row: not | n | en
row: there is not | nn | nen
ex: I know | jw rḫ.j | yoo REKH-ee
ex: I do not know | n rḫ.j | en REKH-ee
ex: there is bread | wnn ꜥḳw | WEH-nen AH-koo
ex: there is no bread | nn ꜥḳw | nen AH-koo
## Singular and plural
> Masculine nouns add -w for the plural (sš becomes sšw). Feminine nouns end in -t and make the plural with -wt. A dual (two) adds -wj.
row: scribe, scribes | sš, sšw | sesh, SESH-oo
row: house, houses | pr, prw | per, PEH-roo
row: city, cities | njwt, njwwt | NEE-oot, NEE-oo-et
ex: a scribe | sš | sesh
ex: scribes | sšw | SESH-oo
ex: a house | pr | per
ex: houses | prw | PEH-roo
