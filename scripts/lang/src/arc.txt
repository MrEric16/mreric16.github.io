NAME: Aramaic (Jesus-era)
ID: arc
FLAG: arc
SCRIPT: Hebrew letters

[sounds]
INTRO: This is Galilean Aramaic of the first century, the everyday language of Jesus and his followers. It is written right to left in the square Hebrew letters of that time. The Latin shown is a simple transliteration. No one speaks it as a mother tongue today, so the vowels are reconstructed.
a | ah, like "father" | abba
e | eh, like "bed" | ena
i | ee, like "see" | rabbi
o / u | oh and oo | yoma, qum
kh | like "ch" in Scottish "loch" (the letter khet) | lakhma
k | a plain "k" (the letter kaf) | kokhva
q | a "k" made further back in the throat | qum
' | a throaty catch in the voice (the letter ayin) | ar'a
ts | like "ts" in "cats" (the letter tsade) | tsave
sh | like "sh" in "ship" | shlama
NOTE: Stress is usually on the last syllable.
NOTE: Aramaic has no capital letters and no verb "to be" in the present: "ana gavra" is "I a man", meaning "I am a man".
NOTE: This pool is much smaller than the Spanish one on purpose. No one can record a living speaker of first-century Galilean Aramaic, so only forms that are well attested (in the Gospels' Aramaic words, the Targums and the Talmud) are included. The forms and the script have not been checked by a native reader. Treat it as a first guide.

[grammar]
## Pronouns
> Aramaic has no verb "to be" in the present. You put the pronoun and the noun side by side: "ana gavra" (I a man = I am a man).
row: I | ana | אנא
row: you (m) | at | את
row: you (f) | at | את
row: he | hu | הוא
row: she | hi | היא
row: we | anan | אנן
row: you (plural) | atun | אתון
row: they | inun | אינון
ex: I am a man | ana gavra | | אנא גברא
ex: you are a teacher | at rabbi | | את רבי
ex: he is a king | hu malka | | הוא מלכא
ex: we are students | anan talmidin | | אנן תלמידין
ex: they are teachers | inun rabbanin | | אינון רבנין
## Singular and plural
> Masculine plural nouns end in -in (gavra, gavrin). Feminine plural nouns end in -an (malkata, malkatan). The ending -a on a noun means "the".
row: -in (masculine plural) | gavra → gavrin | גברא → גברין
row: -an (feminine plural) | karta → kartan | קרתא → קרתן
ex: the man | gavra | | גברא
ex: the men | gavrin | | גברין
ex: the city | karta | | קרתא
ex: the cities | kartan | | קרתן
## Negation
> Put "la" before the word you deny. "Layt" means "there is not". Always compare with the positive form.
row: not | la | לא
row: there is not | layt | לית
ex: I want | ana tsave | | אנא צבי
ex: I do not want | la tsave ana | | לא צבי אנא
ex: I know | ana yada | | אנא ידע
ex: I do not know | la yada ana | | לא ידע אנא
ex: there is bread | it lakhma | | אית לחמא
ex: there is no bread | layt lakhma | | לית לחמא
ex: be afraid | tidchal | | תדחל
ex: do not be afraid | la tidchal | | לא תדחל

## Commands
> The command is the short stem. For a woman the ending is -i: qum (to a man), qumi (to a woman).
row: get up (to a man) | qum | קום
row: get up (to a woman) | qumi | קומי
row: come | ta | תא
row: go | zil | זיל
ex: get up (to a man) | qum | | קום

## Formal and polite
> There is no separate polite pronoun. You show respect with a title: "mari" (my lord), "rabbi" (my teacher), "marya" (Lord).
row: my lord | mari | מרי
row: my teacher | rabbi | רבי
row: Lord | marya | מריא
ex: my teacher | rabbi | | רבי
ex: my lord | mari | | מרי
ex: peace be with you | shlama lakh | | שלמא לך
## Possession
> Short endings are added to the noun: -i (my), -akh (your), -eh (his).
row: my house | beiti | ביתי
row: your house | beitakh | ביתך
row: his house | beiteh | ביתיה
ex: my father | abbi | | אבי
ex: my house | beiti | | ביתי
ex: your house | beitakh | | ביתך
ex: his house | beiteh | | ביתיה
