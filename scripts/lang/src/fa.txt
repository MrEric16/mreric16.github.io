[sounds]
INTRO: Farsi has no articles and no gender. Learn these sounds and you can read the Latin spelling.
a | short "a", like "cat" | ab (water)
aa | long "ah", like "father" | aab, naan
e | short "e", like "bed" | che
i | "ee", like "see" | ranj
o | "o", like "go" | pol
u | "oo", like "food" | doost
kh | harsh h, like Scottish "loch" | khub (good)
gh | throaty, like French "r", also like a gargling g | ghabl
zh | like "s" in "measure" | zhaket (jacket)
ch | as in "church" | chai
sh | as in "ship" | shab
j | as in "jam" | jaa
q / gh | same sound as gh | ghahve (coffee)
' | small stop in the throat | ta'rif
r | rolled, tongue taps | raftan

## Formal and informal
> to (informal): friends, family, children. shomaa (formal): strangers, elders, shops. shomaa is also the plural "you". Verb endings change too (-i with to, -id with shomaa). Tarof is a polite style: you may refuse a gift once or twice before accepting.

## Pronouns
> Farsi has no gender: "u" means he, she or it. shomaa is polite "you" and plural "you".
row: I | man | man
row: you (informal) | to | to
row: he / she | u | oo
row: we | maa | maa
row: you (formal / plural) | shomaa | sho-MAA
row: they | aanhaa | aan-HAA
ex: I am a student | man daaneshjoo hastam | man daa-nesh-JOO has-TAM
ex: you are tall | to ghad-bolandi | to ghad-bo-lan-DEE
ex: she is my sister | u khaahar-e man-e | oo khaa-HAR-e man-EH
