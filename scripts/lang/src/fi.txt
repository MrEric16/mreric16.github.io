[sounds]
INTRO: Finnish is written in the Latin alphabet and is spelled exactly as it sounds. It is not related to Swedish or Russian. There are no genders and no articles. Words are long because endings pile up. The stress is always on the first syllable.
ä | like "a" in "cat" | äiti
ö | say "ur" with rounded lips, like "ur" in "fur" | työ
y | say "ee" with rounded lips, like German "ü" | yö
aa / ee / ii / oo / uu / yy | a double vowel is held long | maa
kk / pp / tt / ss | a double consonant is held long, with a pause before it | kukka
r | rolled, as in Spanish or Italian | rakas
h | breathy, always said | hei
ng | like "ng" in "finger" (both sounds are said) | kenkä
NOTE: Double letters change the meaning. "Tuli" is fire, "tuuli" is wind, "tulli" is customs.
NOTE: Vowel harmony: a word uses either back vowels (a, o, u) or front vowels (ä, ö, y) in its endings, never both. Talossa (in the house), kylässä (in the village).

## Pronouns
> Finnish has no he / she difference: "hän" means both. In speech people often use "se" (it) for a person and "me" or "mä" for I. "Sinä" is you (informal) and "te" is you (formal or plural).
row: I | minä | MI-nah
row: you (informal) | sinä | SI-nah
row: he / she | hän | hahn
row: we | me | meh
row: you (formal or plural) | te | teh
row: they | he | heh
ex: I am a student | minä olen opiskelija | MI-nah O-len OP-pis-keh-li-yah
ex: you are tall | sinä olet pitkä | SI-nah O-let PIT-kah
ex: she is my older sister | hän on isosiskoni | hahn on I-so-sis-ko-ni
ex: we are friends | me olemme ystäviä | meh O-lem-meh EWS-tah-vi-ah
ex: they are teachers | he ovat opettajia | heh O-vaht OP-pet-tah-yi-ah
## Singular and plural
> Add -t to the noun for the plain plural: kirja, kirjat. After a number, the noun stays singular and takes the "-a / -ä" ending: kaksi kirjaa (two books).

row: talo (house) | talot | TAH-lot
row: lapsi (child) | lapset | LAHP-set
