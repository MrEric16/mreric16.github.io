[sounds]
INTRO: Proto-Indo-European (PIE) is the reconstructed ancestor of English, Spanish, Russian, Hindi, Greek, Persian and hundreds more, spoken about 4500 to 3500 BC. Nobody wrote it down. Every form here is a scholarly reconstruction, marked with an asterisk (*). Treat it as the best guess of linguists, not as a real record. Forms differ between scholars, and the "say" line is only a rough convention.
h₁ / h₂ / h₃ | the three "laryngeals", unknown sounds like a breathy "h"; h₂ coloured a nearby "e" into "a", h₃ into "o" | *h₂ster-
ḱ / ǵ / ǵʰ | "k", "g" and breathy "gh" said further forward in the mouth | *ḱlew-
kʷ / gʷ / gʷʰ | "kw", "gw" and breathy "gwh" | *kʷetwóres
bʰ / dʰ | "b" and "d" with a puff of breath | *bʰréh₂tēr
ḱ vs k | in Latin the first became "k" and in Russian it became "s", which is why "hundred" is "centum" but "sto" | *ḱm̥tóm
NOTE: A mark over a vowel (ē, ō) means long. A small circle under r, l, m, n (r̥, l̥, m̥, n̥) means the consonant acted as a vowel. An accent (´) shows where the stress fell.
NOTE: This pool is much smaller than the Spanish one on purpose. Everything is reconstructed and unreviewed. Many details are argued about among scholars.

[grammar]
## Pronouns
> PIE had no he/she/it as we know them. It used the pointing word *so (m), *seh₂ (f), *tod (n) meaning "this or that".
row: I | *h₁éǵ | eg
row: you | *túh₂ | too
row: he / that | *só | so
row: she / that (f) | *séh₂ | SAH
row: we | *wéy | way
row: you (pl) | *yuHs | yoos
ex: I am a king | *h₁éǵ h₁és-mi h₃rḗǵs | eg ES-mee ROHGS
ex: you are a man | *túh₂ h₁és-si h₂nḗr | too ES-see a-NAIR
ex: he is my father | *só h₁és-ti ph₂tḗr | so ES-tee pa-TAIR
## Verb endings
> PIE verbs took endings for the person: -mi (I), -si (you), -ti (he/she), -mes (we), -te (you pl.), -(e)nti (they). Spanish, Latin and Greek endings come from these.
row: I am | *h₁és-mi | ES-mee
row: you are | *h₁és-si | ES-see
row: he is | *h₁és-ti | ES-tee
row: we are | *h₁s-més | smes
row: you (pl) are | *h₁s-té | ste
row: they are | *h₁s-énti | SEN-tee
ex: I eat | *h₁éd-mi | ED-mee
ex: you give | *dídoh₃-si | DEE-doh-see
ex: he carries | *bʰér-e-ti | BER-eh-tee
## Negation
> Put *ne before the verb: "*ne h₁és-ti" (he is not). For a command use *méh₁: "*méh₁ h₁éd-e" (do not eat).
row: not | *ne | neh
row: do not (command) | *méh₁ | MEH
ex: he is | *h₁és-ti | ES-tee
ex: he is not | *ne h₁és-ti | neh ES-tee
ex: I know | *ǵéh₃-n-h₂-mi | GNOH-mee
ex: I do not know | *ne ǵéh₃-n-h₂-mi | neh GNOH-mee
ex: eat! | *h₁éd-e | ED-eh
ex: do not eat! | *méh₁ h₁éd-e | MEH ED-eh
