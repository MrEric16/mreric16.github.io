[sounds]
INTRO: Swedish is written in the Latin alphabet with three extra letters at the end: å, ä, ö. Nouns are "en" or "ett" words. The language has a sing-song pitch, and long and short vowels matter.
å | like "aw" in "saw" | å, båt
ä | like "e" in "bed" (like "air" before r) | äpple, är
ö | say "ur" with rounded lips, like "ur" in "fur" | öl, öga
y | say "ee" with rounded lips, like German "ü" | by, ny
u | a rounded "ew", like a short "oo" said with the lips forward | hus
sj / skj / stj | a hushing sound, between English "sh" and German "ch" | sju, skjorta
tj / k (before e, i, y, ä, ö) | like a soft "ch" in "chin" | tjugo, kista
g / j | "y", as in "yes", before e, i, y, ä, ö | gärna, jag
NOTE: Double consonants make the vowel before them short. A single consonant after a stressed vowel means the vowel is long.

## Pronouns
> Swedish always uses the pronoun. "Du" is you, "ni" is the polite or plural you. "Han" is he, "hon" is she, "det" or "den" is it.
row: I | jag | yah
row: you (informal) | du | dew
row: he / she / it | han / hon / det | han / hoon / deh
row: we | vi | vee
row: you (formal or plural) | ni | nee
row: they | de | dom
ex: I am a student | jag är student | yah air stew-DENT
ex: you are tall | du är lång | dew air long
ex: she is my older sister | hon är min storasyster | hoon air min STOO-rah-sews-ter
ex: we are friends | vi är vänner | vee air VEN-ner
ex: they are teachers | de är lärare | dom air LEH-rah-reh
## Singular and plural
> Nouns are "en" words or "ett" words. There are five plural endings: -ar, -or, -er, -n, or no ending. Learn the plural with each noun.

row: en flicka (girl) | flickor | FLIK-kor
row: en bok (book) | böcker | BUK-er

ex: two books | två böcker | tvoh BUK-er
ex: the children | barnen | BAR-nen
