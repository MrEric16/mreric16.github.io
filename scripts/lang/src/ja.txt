NAME: Japanese
ID: ja
BADGE: Solid
NOTE: Polite everyday Japanese in Latin letters. Say each syllable evenly.

[sounds]
INTRO: Japanese has five pure vowels and very even rhythm. Every syllable gets the same time, with no heavy stress.
a | ah, like "father" | sakana
i | ee, short, like "bit" | inu
u | oo, short and relaxed, lips not rounded | kuruma
e | eh, like "bed" | eki
o | oh, like "more" (short) | sora
ou / oo | long o: hold it twice as long | arigatou
uu | long u: hold it twice as long | yuuki
r | between "r", "l" and "d": a quick tap of the tongue | arigatou
f | softer than English f, blow between both lips | fuji
tsu | like the end of "cats", then "oo" | tsuki
ch, sh, j | like English | chizu, shio, jikan
g | hard g, as in "go" (many speakers soften it in the middle of words) | gakkou
doubled consonant (tt, pp, kk, ss) | a tiny pause before it, like saying "hot-tea" | kippu
n | at the end of a word or before consonants: a hum "n" | hon, kinen
NOTE: Words are usually spelled in this course the way a Japanese phone keyboard writes them: "ou" for a long o, "uu" for a long u.
NOTE: Desu and masu are said almost without the final u: "des", "mas".
NOTE: Japanese has no stress accent like English, but each word has a pitch pattern. Do not worry about it yet.

[grammar]
## Desu: to be
> Japanese puts the verb last. "X wa Y desu" means X is Y. Wa (written ha) marks the topic.
row: I am | watashi wa ... desu
row: is not | ... ja arimasen
row: was | ... deshita
row: is it...? | ... desu ka
ex: I am a student | watashi wa gakusei desu
ex: this is a book | kore wa hon desu
ex: he is a teacher | kare wa sensei desu
ex: I am not a doctor | watashi wa isha ja arimasen
ex: is she Japanese? | kanojo wa nihonjin desu ka
## Pronouns
> Japanese often drops pronouns. Use names instead of "you". The word "anata" (you) can sound too direct.
row: I | watashi
row: you | anata
row: he | kare
row: she | kanojo
row: we | watashitachi
row: they | karera
ex: I am Eric | watashi wa eriku desu
ex: she is my friend | kanojo wa tomodachi desu
ex: we are students | watashitachi wa gakusei desu
ex: he is not here | kare wa koko ni imasen
ex: they are teachers | karera wa sensei desu
## Particles
> Small words after nouns show their job. They are the glue of Japanese.
row: wa | topic ("as for...")
row: ga | subject; also with suki and wakaru
row: o | object (what you do something to)
row: ni | to, at (time), in (place of being)
row: de | at / by (where or how you do it)
row: no | of, 's
row: mo | also
row: ka | question marker
ex: I eat sushi | watashi wa sushi o tabemasu
ex: I go to school | gakkou ni ikimasu
ex: I go by train | densha de ikimasu
ex: it is my book | watashi no hon desu
ex: me too | watashi mo
## Verbs: polite forms
> Polite verbs end in -masu. Change it to -masen (not), -mashita (did), -masen deshita (did not).
row: eat | tabemasu
row: do not eat | tabemasen
row: ate | tabemashita
row: did not eat | tabemasen deshita
ex: I eat bread | pan o tabemasu
ex: I do not drink coffee | koohii o nomimasen
ex: I went to Tokyo | toukyou ni ikimashita
ex: I did not go | ikimasen deshita
ex: what did you eat? | nani o tabemashita ka
## This, that, which
row: this (near me) | kore
row: that (near you) | sore
row: that (far) | are
row: which | dore
row: this + noun | kono + noun
row: that + noun | sono + noun
ex: what is this? | kore wa nan desu ka
ex: this is a pen | kore wa pen desu
ex: that book is mine | sono hon wa watashi no desu
ex: which is yours? | dore ga anata no desu ka
ex: that is a station | are wa eki desu
## Adjectives
> There are two kinds. I-adjectives end in -i (takai). Na-adjectives need "na" before a noun (kirei na hana). To make an i-adjective negative, change -i to -kunai.
row: big | ookii
row: not big | ookikunai
row: was big | ookikatta
row: quiet (na-adjective) | shizuka (na)
ex: it is expensive | takai desu
ex: it is not expensive | takakunai desu
ex: it was delicious | oishikatta desu
ex: a beautiful flower | kirei na hana
ex: the town is quiet | machi wa shizuka desu
## Wanting and asking
> Add -tai to the verb stem to say "want to". Add -te kudasai to ask politely.
row: want to eat | tabetai desu
row: want to go | ikitai desu
row: please eat | tabete kudasai
row: please wait | matte kudasai
ex: I want to go to Japan | nihon ni ikitai desu
ex: I want to eat sushi | sushi ga tabetai desu
ex: please wait here | koko de matte kudasai
ex: please speak slowly | yukkuri hanashite kudasai
ex: please show me | misete kudasai
## Existing and having
> Imasu is for living things, arimasu for things. To say "I have", say "[thing] ga arimasu".
row: there is (living) | imasu
row: there is (thing) | arimasu
row: there is not | arimasen / imasen
ex: there is a cat | neko ga imasu
ex: there is a bank here | koko ni ginkou ga arimasu
ex: I have time | jikan ga arimasu
ex: I do not have money | okane ga arimasen
ex: I have a brother | otouto ga imasu
