[sounds]
INTRO: Georgian has its own alphabet with 33 letters, no capitals, no genders and no articles. The Latin shown is the standard romanisation. Stress is light, so say each syllable evenly. Verbs are the hard part, but the sounds are easy once you hear the "popping" consonants.
k' / p' / t' | pop the sound out with a tight throat, with no puff of air | k'argi, p'uri, t'elefoni
q' | a "k" popped far back in the throat | q'ava
ts' / ch' | popped "ts" and "ch" | ts'qali, ch'ama
k / p / t | plain, with a puff of air, like English | kali, pekhi, tavi
gh | a gargled French "r" | ghame
kh | like "ch" in Scottish "loch" | khe
zh | like "s" in "measure" | zhurnali
dz | like "ds" in "kids" | dzma
NOTE: The apostrophe in the Latin marks a popped consonant. It is the key difference between pairs such as t / t' and k / k'.
NOTE: The Georgian-script line is written by rule from the Latin.
